What license do I need for armed security work in Texas? +
Armed security officers in Texas must hold a Level III Armed Security Officer Commission issued by the Texas Department of Public Safety. This requires completing the standard Level II unarmed training plus a minimum of 40 additional hours of firearms training and a passing range qualification. The commission must be renewed every two years with a current range qualification.

Entry-level armed security in Dallas typically pays $20–24/hr. Experienced armed officers earn $24–30/hr. Armed overnight posts carry an additional $1–3/hr shift differential on top of the armed rate.

Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Does an Armed Security Officer Do in Dallas?

Armed security officers in Dallas carry a holstered firearm and are deployed at sites where the risk profile requires a higher deterrent and faster response capability than unarmed coverage provides. Common Dallas assignments include bank branch protection, data center access control, medical facility high-risk area coverage, jewelry and luxury retail security, and government building protection.

The role requires a Texas Level III Armed Security Officer Commission and ongoing range qualification. Dallas armed officers are expected to maintain composure in high-pressure situations, exercise sound judgment about use of force, and document all incidents with a higher degree of detail than standard unarmed posts.

Armed positions in Dallas pay meaningfully above comparable unarmed roles — typically $5–10/hr more — reflecting the additional training burden, liability considerations, and elevated responsibility of the assignment.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Provide armed deterrence and response at high-risk or high-value facilities
  • Control access to secured areas requiring Level III armed coverage
  • Conduct armed patrols of financial institution branches, data centers, and medical facilities
  • Respond to security incidents, threats, and emergencies with appropriate force protocols
  • Coordinate with law enforcement for escalated incidents requiring police response
  • Maintain detailed shift logs and incident reports with full documentation of any use-of-force situations
  • Conduct security screenings at entry points for sensitive government and financial facilities
  • Provide executive protection and close-detail coverage when required by employer
