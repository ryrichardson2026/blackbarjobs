Do I need a guard card for hotel security in Dallas? +
Yes. A valid Texas Level II Security Officer Commission is required for all paid hotel security positions. Most Dallas hotel security roles are unarmed — Level III Armed Commission is required only for properties that maintain armed security programs, which is less common in hotel settings than in commercial or healthcare environments.
Is hotel security a good job in Dallas? +
Yes — particularly for candidates who value stable scheduling, indoor climate-controlled work environments, and the professional development that comes with representing a major hotel brand. Dallas's large convention and business travel market means hotel security positions are relatively stable year-round, and overnight shifts at downtown properties offer competitive pay with lower foot traffic than day shifts.
Do you need hospitality experience for hotel security in Dallas? +
Not required, but it's a strong advantage. Dallas hotel brands prefer candidates who can manage guest interactions professionally alongside their security functions. Candidates with front desk, bell service, or F&B management backgrounds are highly competitive — their combination of guest relations skills and security function training is exactly what downtown Dallas hotels look for in overnight security officers.
What hotels hire security officers in Dallas? +
Omni Dallas, Hilton Anatole, Hyatt Regency Dallas, Renaissance Dallas, The Adolphus, and Sheraton Dallas are among the larger downtown properties with dedicated security programs. Many hire through contracted guard firms including Allied Universal and Securitas. Boutique properties in Uptown and the Design District often hire directly or through smaller regional staffing firms.
What does hotel security pay in Dallas? +
Entry-level hotel security in Dallas runs $15–18/hr. Experienced officers at luxury downtown properties earn $18–24/hr. Senior security and overnight supervisor roles reach $24–30/hr. Overnight shifts carry an additional $1–3/hr differential on top of base pay.

Hotel Security Demand in Dallas

Hotel security in Dallas blends traditional security functions with a strong hospitality orientation. Officers are often the most visible staff members during late-night and overnight hours — serving as the primary point of contact for guests, managing disturbances discreetly, and maintaining the property's reputation alongside its physical security. Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Does a Hotel Security Officer Do in Dallas?

Hotel security officers in Dallas perform a blend of traditional security and guest service functions. The role involves lobby monitoring, perimeter patrol, incident response, and guest assistance — all conducted with the professional demeanor that luxury and upper-midscale Dallas hotel brands require of anyone representing their property.

Overnight hotel security is the most common shift type for dedicated security positions. Most Dallas hotels reduce management staff significantly after 10pm, making the security officer the senior on-site presence for noise complaints, room disturbances, medical incidents, and any after-hours emergencies that require coordination with Dallas PD or EMS.

Hotel security candidates with prior hospitality experience — front desk, bell service, F&B management — are highly competitive in Dallas, as they bring both security function skills and the guest relations orientation that hotel brands prioritize. Military veterans are also well-regarded across the Dallas hotel security market.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Monitor hotel lobby, entrances, parking areas, and guest corridors throughout the shift
  • Respond to noise complaints, guest disturbances, and room incidents professionally and discreetly
  • Conduct scheduled perimeter patrols of the property including parking structures and exterior areas
  • Coordinate with Dallas PD and EMS on incidents requiring law enforcement or medical response
  • Assist guests with lockouts, safety concerns, and after-hours property access
  • Monitor and manage access to pool areas, fitness centers, and restricted property zones after hours
  • Write accurate shift logs and incident reports for all security-related events
  • Serve as primary on-site contact during overnight hours when management staffing is minimal

Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level II Security License required for all paid hotel security positions
  • Prior hospitality, front desk, or hotel operations experience highly valued across Dallas hotel security postings
  • Professional appearance and strong verbal communication skills — hotel brands consistently prioritize guest-facing presentation

  • Weekend availability consistently required — Friday and Saturday nights are the busiest security periods at most Dallas hotels
