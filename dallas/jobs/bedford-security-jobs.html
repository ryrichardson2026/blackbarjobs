FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What types of security jobs are in Bedford TX?+
Bedford anchors the DFW mid-cities at the intersection of Hwy 121 and Hwy 183 (Airport Freeway), giving officers in Bedford access to security accounts across western Tarrant County without a long commute. Account types include commercial retail security along the 121 corridor and Central Drive shopping district, light industrial and distribution accounts in Bedford's business corridors, residential community security for Bedford's established neighborhoods and multifamily developments, and office security for the business parks along Mid-Cities Boulevard. Allied Universal, Securitas, and contracted commercial security firms serve Bedford area accounts.
How does Bedford's location affect security opportunities?+
Bedford is centrally located in the DFW mid-cities with excellent highway access. Hwy 121 runs through Bedford connecting to Euless (east, DFW Airport adjacent) and North Richland Hills (west, toward Fort Worth). Hwy 183 connects north to Grapevine and south to Arlington. This geography makes Bedford a strong residential base for officers who want to work accounts across the mid-cities corridor without committing to a specific suburb. An officer in Bedford can reach Euless airport accounts, Mansfield industrial accounts, or North Richland Hills residential accounts without extending past a 20-minute commute in any direction.
What does security pay in Bedford?+
Standard commercial and residential security in Bedford runs $16–20/hr, consistent with the broader mid-cities market. Retail LP at big-box stores along the 121 corridor runs $16–19/hr. Light industrial and distribution accounts run $17–20/hr. Office building security in Bedford's business parks runs $16–19/hr. Armed accounts in the area run $19–23/hr. The Bedford market doesn't have the industrial premium of Mansfield or the logistics concentration of Grand Prairie, but the central location and account diversity make it a stable mid-cities security market.
Which employers hire security in Bedford?+
Allied Universal (mid-cities corridor accounts including Bedford area), Securitas, Security Engineers Inc (posts Irving/mid-cities unarmed at $17/hr), and contracted residential and commercial security firms serving Bedford's established neighborhoods. The mid-cities' manufacturing and industrial operations — Pro-Tech Staffing's Bedford office describes the corridor as having aerospace, automotive logistics, and 3PL distribution as dominant industries — draw from the Bedford officer pool for production-adjacent security accounts. Tom Thumb, Walmart, and retail accounts in the 121 corridor also generate LP demand.
Is Bedford security work close to other DFW markets?+
Bedford is 20 minutes from downtown Fort Worth, 25 minutes from downtown Dallas, 10 minutes from DFW Airport (via Hwy 183), and 15 minutes from Arlington. For officers who want schedule flexibility across multiple employers or account types, Bedford's central location is genuinely useful. The mid-cities transit corridor (Hwy 121/183) is one of the few DFW suburban areas with meaningful bus service connecting to employment centers.
Overview

Bedford Security Market: Mid-Cities Hub at 121 and 183

Bedford's security market reflects its character as a mature, established mid-cities community rather than an emerging growth market. The account mix is commercial retail, light industrial, office building, and residential — the diversified profile of a suburban city with decades of commercial development along its primary corridors. It lacks the industrial scale of Mansfield or the logistics concentration of Grand Prairie, but it offers consistent demand across multiple account types and the geographic advantage of sitting at the center of the mid-cities labor market.

The Hwy 121 and Central Drive commercial corridors anchor Bedford's retail security demand. Big-box retail, strip mall commercial accounts, and the mixed commercial development that has accumulated along these arterials generate standard LP and patrol demand year-round. For officers who prefer working commercial retail environments close to home, Bedford's established retail base provides consistent options without extending to larger suburban retail concentrations like Colleyville's Town Center or Southlake Town Square.

Bedford's residential character adds a stable community patrol dimension. The city's established neighborhoods — primarily single-family with some multifamily development — generate residential security accounts for officers comfortable with community-oriented patrol work. The mid-cities' diverse population (the Hurst-Euless-Bedford school district serves a demographically varied community) means bilingual officers are particularly valued in residential and retail security accounts throughout the corridor.

Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Security Officers Do in Bedford

Commercial retail security along the 121 corridor is the most common Bedford work environment. Officers patrol shopping centers, staff retail LP at big-box anchors, and provide the visible deterrent presence that retail accounts need during peak shopping hours. The environment is customer-facing and incident-responsive — less procedurally intensive than industrial gate work, more interpersonal than overnight warehouse patrol.

Office building security in Bedford's business parks adds the professional interior environment. Day and swing shift lobby security, visitor management, and after-hours patrol of mid-rise and low-rise office buildings is standard work that rewards professional presentation and consistent documentation. Bedford's business parks house a mix of small-to-mid business tenants rather than Fortune 500 headquarters, which means the security work is less rigorous than Uptown Dallas corporate accounts but more accessible for officers building their professional track record.
