What surveillance security jobs are hiring in Dallas?+
Active surveillance and monitoring openings in Dallas span central CCTV monitoring stations covering retail and commercial properties across the metro, corporate campus security control rooms in the CBD and Uptown, data center monitoring operations along the Stemmons corridor, and industrial facility surveillance positions in South Dallas and along I-20. Do I need experience with cameras for surveillance security jobs in Dallas?+
Prior experience with CCTV systems or VMS platforms is strongly preferred but not always required for entry-level monitoring positions. Many Dallas monitoring centers will train candidates on their specific systems. Candidates with prior dispatch, IT support, or 911 call center experience are competitive even without dedicated surveillance backgrounds.
Is surveillance security a good career path in Dallas?+
Yes — particularly for candidates interested in the technology side of security. Officers who develop proficiency with major VMS platforms and access control systems build skills that transfer into security systems management, security analyst, and corporate security director roles. The Dallas market has a strong demand for technically proficient security professionals, and surveillance experience is increasingly valued at senior corporate security positions.
What does surveillance security pay in Dallas?+
Entry-level surveillance positions in Dallas run $16–19/hr. Experienced monitoring officers with VMS proficiency earn $19–25/hr. Senior control room supervisors and systems analysts reach $25–34/hr. Overnight monitoring shifts carry an additional $1–3/hr differential on top of base pay.
What software do surveillance security officers use in Dallas?+
The most common VMS platforms in the Dallas commercial security market are Genetec Security Center, Milestone XProtect, and Avigilon Control Center. Access control systems most frequently encountered include Lenel OnGuard and Software House C·CURE 9000. Familiarity with any of these platforms is a meaningful differentiator when applying for Dallas surveillance security positions.
Can I do surveillance security work remotely in Dallas?+
Some monitoring operations use hybrid models where officers monitor cameras from an off-site control room — but the monitoring itself is still an on-site role at a central station, not work-from-home. True remote monitoring positions are emerging in the industry but remain uncommon in the Dallas market for commissioned security officers who require a license to perform security functions.
Dallas Surveillance Market

Surveillance Security Demand in Dallas

Surveillance and monitoring security roles are a growing category in the Dallas market as commercial property owners and corporate campuses invest in CCTV infrastructure and remote monitoring capabilities. Surveillance officers work in dedicated control rooms — monitoring live camera feeds across multiple properties, responding to alerts from access control systems, and coordinating with on-site patrol officers when camera activity warrants a physical response.

Dallas's scale as a commercial real estate market makes centralized surveillance operations economically practical. Guard firms and corporate security departments managing dozens of client sites across the metro increasingly use central monitoring stations to provide cost-effective oversight across properties that can't justify a dedicated on-site officer. Remote monitoring centers in the Dallas area typically cover retail, industrial, and commercial properties across the DFW metro from a single control room.

Surveillance positions are a strong fit for candidates who prefer a seated, technology-focused work environment over physical patrol. The role requires sustained attention, comfort with multiple monitors and camera systems, and the judgment to distinguish routine activity from genuine security events in real time. Overnight surveillance shifts at large monitoring centers are the most consistently available and carry strong shift differentials. Active hiring zip codes include 75207, 75247, and 75201.

Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Does a Surveillance Security Officer Do in Dallas?

Surveillance security officers in Dallas monitor live CCTV feeds from assigned properties, respond to access control alerts, and communicate with on-site officers or law enforcement when camera activity requires a physical response. In a central monitoring station, a single officer may simultaneously oversee camera feeds from 10–50 properties across the DFW metro.

Day-to-day work involves continuous monitoring of multiple screens, logging routine observations, flagging alerts when activity crosses security thresholds, and dispatching on-site resources when needed. Surveillance officers also review recorded footage when incidents are reported after the fact — pulling clips for incident documentation, insurance claims, and law enforcement requests.

The surveillance role is growing in Dallas as technology investment in commercial security infrastructure increases. Officers who develop proficiency with major VMS platforms (Genetec, Milestone, Avigilon) and access control systems (Lenel, Software House) are increasingly valuable — the technical skill set compounds over time and opens doors to security systems management and analyst roles.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Monitor live CCTV feeds across assigned properties from a central control room
  • Respond to access control alerts and verify authorized versus unauthorized entry events
  • Dispatch on-site patrol officers or contact Dallas PD when camera activity requires response
  • Review and export recorded footage for incident documentation and law enforcement requests
  • Log all monitored activity, alerts, and dispatched responses in shift documentation
  • Test and verify camera and access control system functionality during assigned shifts
  • Coordinate with property managers and clients on access events and after-hours activity
  • Write detailed incident reports for all security events identified through surveillance monitoring

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Security License required
  • Comfort with multiple monitors, camera systems, and access control software interfaces
  • Prior security, IT support, dispatch, or monitoring center experience strongly valued
  • Strong sustained attention and the ability to remain focused during low-activity monitoring periods
  • Familiarity with VMS platforms (Genetec, Milestone, Avigilon) or access control systems (Lenel, Software House) a competitive advantage
  • Strong written documentation skills for shift logs and incident reports reviewed by clients and management
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Overnight monitoring shifts (10pm–6am) are the most consistently available in Dallas surveillance operations
  • 8 and 12-hour monitoring shifts available depending on employer and monitoring center size
  • Surveillance roles are primarily indoor, climate-controlled, seated positions — physically less demanding than patrol
  • Weekend and holiday coverage required at 24-hour monitoring centers
  • Shift differentials of $1–3/hr standard for overnight surveillance positions
  • Some Dallas monitoring centers operate rotating shift schedules — confirm shift structure before accepting
