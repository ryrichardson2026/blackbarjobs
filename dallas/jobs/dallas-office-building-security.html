What does office building security pay in Dallas?+
Entry-level office building security in Dallas runs $15–18/hr. Experienced lobby officers and building security supervisors at Class A properties earn $18–24/hr. Senior and armed office building positions reach $24–32/hr. Class A CBD and Uptown tower assignments consistently pay at the upper end of the range.
Do I need experience for office building security jobs in Dallas?+
For Class A CBD and Uptown tower lobby officer positions — which are the most visible and professionally demanding — prior security, hospitality, or corporate front desk experience is strongly preferred. Entry-level candidates can access office building security through suburban office park and single-tenant campus assignments where the screening criteria are somewhat more flexible.
What do you wear for office building security in Dallas?+
Dress standards vary by building class and client. Class A CBD and Uptown towers typically require formal security uniforms — dress slacks, button-down shirts, and blazers or suit jackets provided by the employer. Class B office parks and suburban campus assignments may use standard guard uniforms. Always confirm the dress code with the employer before your first shift.
Is office building security a stable job in Dallas?+
Yes — among the most stable in the Dallas security market. Large commercial office buildings have multi-year security service contracts, and turnover among high-performing officers at Class A assignments is low. Officers who build tenure at CBD and Uptown properties and develop relationships with property management teams often have very long-term job stability.
What security companies work in Dallas office buildings?+
Allied Universal, Securitas, and several specialized corporate security firms hold the major service contracts across Dallas's CBD and Uptown office tower inventory. Some large corporate campuses — particularly Fortune 500 headquarters in the CBD — employ in-house security departments rather than contracting through guard firms. Both paths are active hiring channels in the Dallas office building security market.
Dallas Office Security

Office Building Security Demand in Dallas

Dallas is one of the largest office markets in the United States — with over 200 million square feet of commercial office space across the CBD, Uptown, Las Colinas, the Galleria corridor, and multiple suburban office parks. This concentration of corporate real estate makes office building security one of the most stable and consistently in-demand role types in the Dallas security market.

The CBD alone supports dozens of high-rise office towers housing Fortune 500 headquarters, major law firms, financial services companies, and corporate regional offices — all of which require dedicated lobby security, access control, and overnight coverage. Uptown's growing mid-rise office inventory adds to the demand. The Las Colinas urban center in Irving, just west of Dallas, is one of the largest suburban office concentrations in Texas and draws Dallas-area security candidates for its corporate campus assignments.

Office building security is one of the most professionally oriented roles in the Dallas security market. Officers represent the building brand and property management company in addition to providing security functions. Professional appearance, strong communication skills, and the ability to manage a mix of tenant, visitor, and vendor interactions throughout the shift are consistently the top screening criteria for Dallas office security employers. Role Overview

What Does an Office Building Security Officer Do in Dallas?

Office building security officers in Dallas manage lobby access control, visitor management, and building patrol across commercial office properties ranging from single-tenant corporate headquarters to 50-story multi-tenant high-rises. Day shift positions at CBD towers are the most visible and professionally demanding — managing the flow of hundreds of tenants, visitors, and vendors through secured lobby entrances during morning rush and throughout the business day.

Evening and overnight office building security shifts shift from active visitor management to patrol and monitoring. Once the building empties at the end of business hours, officers conduct floor-by-floor security checks, monitor access control logs for after-hours badge activity, patrol parking structures, and manage any after-hours vendors or maintenance personnel requiring building access.

Office building security is a strong career path for candidates interested in corporate security management. The consistent exposure to corporate clients, building management teams, and property management professionals builds a professional network that many officers leverage into corporate security director and facilities management roles over a 5–10 year horizon.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Manage lobby access control and visitor management during business hours
  • Verify tenant badges, issue visitor passes, and log all building access
  • Conduct scheduled floor patrol and stairwell checks throughout the shift
  • Monitor building CCTV, access control panels, and fire alarm systems
  • Manage after-hours access for authorized vendors and maintenance personnel
  • Patrol parking structures and building exterior during evening and overnight shifts
  • Respond to medical emergencies, fire alarms, and security incidents
  • Write detailed shift logs and incident reports for building management review

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Security License required
  • Professional appearance and polished verbal communication — consistently the primary screening criteria for Class A office assignments
  • Prior security, corporate facilities, hospitality, or front desk experience all competitive
  • Strong customer service orientation — office building security involves frequent tenant and visitor interaction
  • Familiarity with access control and CCTV systems preferred for senior and supervisory positions
  • Business casual to formal presentation standard at most Dallas Class A office tower assignments
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Day shifts (6am–2pm or 7am–3pm) dominate at CBD and Uptown office towers during business hours
  • Evening and overnight positions available at most Dallas office buildings for patrol and monitoring coverage
  • Monday through Friday staffing patterns at single-tenant corporate campuses — weekend coverage at multi-tenant buildings
  • 8-hour shifts most common; some large corporate campuses use 12-hour rotations
  • Overtime available during major tenant events, building maintenance windows, and holiday staffing gaps
  • Indoor climate-controlled environment at all Class A Dallas office assignments — a meaningful advantage during Texas summers
