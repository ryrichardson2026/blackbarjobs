What license do I need for armed security in Texas? +
Armed security officers in Texas must hold a Level III Armed Security Officer Commission from the Texas DPS. This requires completing Level II unarmed training plus a minimum of 40 hours of firearms training and a passing range qualification. The commission renews every two years with a current range qualification required.

Entry-level armed security in Fort Worth pays $20–24/hr. Experienced officers earn $24–30/hr. Senior and specialized roles reach $30–38/hr. Fort Worth Market

Armed Security Demand in Fort Worth

Fort Worth's armed security market is anchored by two distinct demand centers. The Alliance corridor in north Fort Worth — home to one of the largest inland ports in the US — generates significant armed security demand from high-value freight operations, bonded warehouses, and logistics facilities where cargo theft is an active risk. Major distribution operators in this corridor contract armed officers specifically for overnight and early morning shifts when theft risk is highest.

Downtown Fort Worth's financial district, government buildings around the Tarrant County Courthouse, and the cultural institutions along Camp Bowie Boulevard create a second armed security market concentrated in the CBD and Near Northside. JPS Health Network and Cook Children's Medical Center in the Near Southside maintain active armed security programs for high-risk patient areas and pharmacy protection.

The Stockyards District and Sundance Square entertainment areas add evening and weekend armed security demand, particularly for private events and VIP areas at high-profile venues. Active hiring zip codes include 76102, 76104, 76107, 76131, and 76177.

Role Overview

What Does an Armed Security Officer Do in Fort Worth?

Armed security officers in Fort Worth are deployed at sites where the value of assets, the vulnerability of occupants, or the history of criminal activity justifies a higher response capability than unarmed coverage provides. In the Alliance corridor, that typically means high-value freight protection and bonded warehouse security. In the medical district, it means armed coverage of pharmaceutical storage and high-risk patient areas.

Downtown Fort Worth armed assignments tend to be more visible and public-facing — bank branches, government lobbies, and courthouse security where the armed presence serves as both deterrent and rapid-response capability. These roles require strong composure and professional bearing alongside tactical readiness.

Fort Worth armed officers earn meaningfully more than comparable unarmed roles — typically $5–10/hr above unarmed rates — reflecting the additional licensing requirements, ongoing range qualification, and elevated responsibility.

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Provide armed deterrence and response at freight facilities, financial institutions, and medical campuses
  • Control access to high-security areas requiring Level III armed coverage

  • Respond to security incidents and threats with appropriate use-of-force protocols
  • Coordinate with law enforcement for escalated incidents requiring police response
  • Maintain detailed shift logs and incident reports with full documentation of any use-of-force events
  • Staff armed posts at government buildings, courthouses, and financial institution branches
  • Provide armed venue security at private events and VIP areas in Sundance Square and the Stockyards
