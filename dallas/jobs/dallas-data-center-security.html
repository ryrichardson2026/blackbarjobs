What does data center security pay in DFW?+
Entry-level data center security in DFW runs $19–24/hr — meaningfully above standard unarmed commercial security rates. Experienced officers at hyperscale and colocation facilities earn $24–32/hr. Senior security and supervisory roles at major DFW data centers reach $32–45/hr.
Is data center security a good career in DFW?+
Yes — one of the best commercial security career tracks in the metro. The combination of above-average pay, professional working environment, consistent scheduling, and credentials that transfer well to corporate and government security makes data center security a strong career choice for officers who qualify. The DFW market is particularly strong given the metro's data center density.
What qualifications do I need for data center security in DFW?+
A valid Texas Level 2 guard card, clean background, professional demeanor, and prior security or military experience are the baseline requirements at most DFW data center facilities. Some facilities serving federal cloud infrastructure clients require enhanced background investigations beyond standard DPS requirements. Experience with access control technology and precision documentation is specifically valued.
What companies hire data center security in DFW?+
Equinix, CyrusOne, QTS, and Iron Mountain are the largest colocation data center security employers in DFW. Allied Universal and Securitas hold major contracted security accounts at hyperscale Google, Microsoft, and Amazon Web Services facilities in the metro. Some hyperscale operators hire direct security staff for their DFW campuses.
Is data center security the same as IT security?+
No. Data center physical security is the protection of the physical facility, servers, and access control — it's a security officer role. IT security (cybersecurity) is the protection of data and networks — it's a technology role. Physical data center security officers do not require IT or cybersecurity knowledge. The role is access control, patrol, and incident response applied to a critical infrastructure facility.

Role Overview

What Does Data Center Security Look Like in DFW?

Data center security officers in DFW manage multi-layer access control systems that are among the most sophisticated in the commercial security industry. Entry to the facility, server halls, cage areas, and individual client suites requires credential verification at multiple checkpoints — biometric readers, man-trap airlocks, and RFID access control systems that log every entry and exit with time-stamped documentation.

Visitor and vendor management at DFW data centers is a high-accountability function. Every individual entering the facility — from delivery personnel to C-suite client executives — must be pre-authorized, identity-verified, escorted by an authorized employee, and logged with timestamped documentation. Officers are the last line of physical access control between the facility's public entry and its critical infrastructure.

Perimeter security at larger DFW data center campuses involves vehicle patrol of facility grounds, monitoring of physical security systems (fence sensors, exterior cameras, motion detection), and response to any unauthorized approach or intrusion attempt. The combination of interior access control and exterior perimeter security makes data center security one of the most comprehensive and professionally demanding security roles in the DFW market.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Manage multi-layer access control at facility entry, server halls, and client cage areas
  • Verify and log all visitor, vendor, and contractor access with identity verification
  • Escort authorized visitors through secured areas of the data center campus
  • Monitor CCTV, perimeter sensors, and security systems at data center operations centers
  • Conduct exterior vehicle patrol of data center campus perimeter and access roads
  • Respond to access control alarms, unauthorized entry attempts, and security incidents
  • Maintain precise access logs and incident reports meeting data center audit requirements
  • Enforce strict clean desk, photography, and device policies within the facility

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Security License required — Level 3 for armed data center posts
  • Prior security, military, or law enforcement experience strongly preferred at hyperscale facilities
  • Professional demeanor and precision documentation — data center clients hold security to institutional standards
  • Comfort with technology-driven access control systems — biometrics, RFID, man-traps are standard
  • Background investigation clearance — some DFW data centers serving federal clients require enhanced screening
  • Clean background with no financial crimes history — data centers handle sensitive client infrastructure
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • All three shifts available — data centers operate 24/7 with no reduction in security staffing
  • 12-hour rotating shifts standard at major DFW hyperscale and colocation facilities
  • Overnight and weekend shifts available across all DFW data center security accounts
  • Holiday coverage required — data center operations are continuous and security does not reduce on holidays
  • Background investigation and badging process may extend onboarding to 3–6 weeks at high-security sites
  • Consistent scheduling at fixed facilities — data center security is not mobile patrol work
