Arlington Market

Why Arlington Is One of the Biggest Event Security Markets in Texas

Arlington is home to one of the densest concentrations of major entertainment venues in the entire southern United States. Within a few square miles of the Entertainment District along I-30 and Collins Street, you have two major professional sports stadiums, a major league ballpark, one of the most visited theme parks in North America, a dedicated esports arena, and a convention center — all operating on overlapping and often simultaneous event schedules.

AT&T Stadium, home of the Dallas Cowboys, seats over 80,000 fans and regularly hosts NFL games, college football playoffs, boxing matches, international soccer, and concerts that push capacity above 100,000. Globe Life Field, home of the Texas Rangers, runs a full MLB season from spring through fall, plus postseason events. Six Flags Over Texas draws millions of visitors annually and requires consistent security staffing across the park's operating season. Esports Stadium Arlington hosts major gaming tournaments and live events year-round. The Arlington Convention Center and nearby hotels add corporate events, trade shows, and private functions to the mix.

For security professionals, Arlington represents a uniquely high-volume opportunity — particularly for those seeking flexible part-time, or flexible event-based work that pays well on game days and event nights.

Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Does an Event Security Officer Do?

Event security officers in Arlington work in some of the highest-volume, highest-visibility environments in the state. The job is fundamentally different from commercial or overnight security — you're managing large crowds, working compressed high-intensity shifts, and dealing with a range of situations that escalate quickly in dense public settings.

Shifts are typically tied to event schedules — arriving 2–3 hours before doors open for setup and post-event duties that extend 1–2 hours after the final whistle or encore. A Cowboys game day or a major concert at AT&T Stadium can require hundreds of officers working simultaneously across entrances, seating sections, field level, VIP areas, and perimeter zones.

Event security is well-suited for candidates who want flexible, flexible work with above-average hourly rates on active event days, and who thrive in fast-paced, public-facing environments. Many officers work multiple venues across the Entertainment District depending on the event calendar.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Staff entrance gates and ticket checkpoints during ingress and egress
  • Conduct bag checks and wand screenings per venue security protocols
  • Manage crowd flow in seating sections, concourses, and standing areas
  • Monitor for prohibited items, intoxicated guests, and disruptive behavior
  • Respond to ejections, medical situations, and disturbances in coordination with venue staff
  • Staff field-level, VIP, and restricted-access zones requiring heightened vigilance
  • Assist with emergency evacuations and incident response procedures
  • Complete post-event sweep and property check before clearing the venue

Requirements & Experience
Requirements

Training & Credential Requirements

Valid Texas Security License (Level II or higher)
Clean criminal background check
Armed License (Level III) — required for armed event detail roles
Crowd management or venue security training (preferred at major stadium venues)
CPR / First Aid certification (preferred, required at some venues)
Reliable transportation — event shifts often start and end at off-peak transit hours

Requirements vary by venue and employer. Verify directly with the hiring company.

Experience

Experience Requirements

  • Entry-level event security positions available for candidates with a valid guard card — prior experience not always required for gate and floor staffing roles
  • Prior experience in crowd management, venue security, or hospitality strongly preferred at AT&T Stadium and Globe Life Field
  • Candidates with military, law enforcement, or prior security backgrounds often fast-tracked to supervisory and field-level posts
  • Strong communication skills and calm under pressure are the most consistently cited attributes by Arlington venue employers
  • Physical requirements include extended standing for 4–8+ hours, navigating large crowds, and responding quickly to incidents in loud, high-stimulation environments
  • Six Flags and theme park roles may require comfort working outdoors in Texas summer heat for extended shifts
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Work Environments

  • Shifts are event-driven — not fixed daily schedules. Cowboys games, Rangers games, concerts, and Six Flags operating days all drive separate staffing calls
  • Pre-event arrival typically 2–3 hours before doors open; post-event duties extend 1–2 hours after conclusion
  • NFL season runs September through January; MLB season runs April through October — creating near-continuous event demand across the Entertainment District for most of the year
  • Six Flags season runs spring through fall with extended holiday hours — summer months are the highest-staffing period
  • Esports Stadium and Convention Center events are distributed year-round and tend to be less physically demanding than stadium work
  • Weekend availability is essential — the vast majority of major Arlington events fall on Friday, Saturday, and Sunday
  • flexible and part-time structures are common, with many officers working multiple venues across the same week

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get a job doing security at AT&T Stadium or Globe Life Field? +
Both venues staff their events through contracted security firms rather than direct employment. Companies like Allied Universal, Securitas, and regional event staffing firms hold the service contracts for major Arlington venues. The fastest path is to obtain your Texas Level II Security License, then apply through the staffing firm currently contracted to the venue. Sign up for alerts here and we'll notify you when openings are posted.
Do I need a guard card for event security in Texas? +
Yes. Texas requires a valid Security Officer Commission (Level II) through the Department of Public Safety for paid security work at venues like AT&T Stadium and Globe Life Field. Some very limited volunteer or usher roles may not require a guard card, but any paid security position does. Entry-level candidates can typically complete training and obtain their commission in 2–4 weeks.
Is event security in Arlington full-time or part-time? +
Mostly flexible and part-time, especially at the stadium and ballpark venues. You're staffed for specific events rather than fixed daily shifts. The Cowboys and Rangers schedules create high event density from spring through winter, so active officers can piece together strong weekly income by working multiple events — sometimes across multiple venues in the same week. Some security firms also offer full-time positions for supervisory and standing-post roles within the Entertainment District.
Are there event security jobs at Six Flags Over Texas? +
Yes. Six Flags Over Texas hires security staff seasonally, with the bulk of openings from late spring through summer and into the fall holiday season. Roles range from guest-facing crowd management and access control to perimeter patrol across the park's large property. Summer heat is a real factor — outdoor shifts during Texas summers require physical stamina and appropriate preparation.
What does event security pay in Arlington? +
Entry-level event security in Arlington typically pays $15–19/hr for standard gate and floor roles. Experienced officers in supervisory, VIP, or field-level positions earn $19–25/hr. Armed event detail roles — covering talent, high-profile guests, or sensitive zones — can reach $25–35/hr. Major stadium events like Cowboys playoff games or high-profile concerts often carry premium rates above standard event pay.
Do I need to be available on weekends for event security in Arlington? +
Yes — weekend availability is essentially required. The overwhelming majority of major Arlington events fall on Friday, Saturday, and Sunday. Officers who limit themselves to weekday availability will find very limited event security work in the Entertainment District. Flexibility across both weekend days significantly increases the volume of shifts available to you.
