Do I need a driver's license for industrial security in Dallas?+
For the vast majority of Dallas industrial security positions, yes. Industrial campuses are large-scale operations where vehicle patrol is the standard operating mode. A valid Texas driver's license and clean driving record are effectively required for most overnight industrial patrol openings in the Dallas market.
What does industrial security pay in Dallas?+
Entry-level industrial security runs $15–18/hr with overnight differentials of $1–3/hr. Experienced officers at large distribution centers earn $18–25/hr. Senior patrol supervisors and armed industrial posts reach $25–32/hr. High-value cargo and pharmaceutical operations tend to pay at the upper end.
Is industrial security easy to get into in Dallas?+
Yes — it's one of the most accessible overnight security entry points in the market. Employers consistently prioritize overnight availability, a clean background, and a valid driver's license over prior security experience. If you have a guard card and can work overnight shifts reliably, you are a competitive candidate for most Dallas industrial security openings.
What is the difference between warehouse and industrial security in Dallas?+
In practice they overlap significantly — both involve vehicle patrol of large facility footprints, gate access control, and overnight coverage of high-value inventory. Industrial security tends to refer to manufacturing, processing, and utility facility security, while warehouse security refers specifically to distribution and storage operations. The role requirements, pay, and schedule structure are largely identical.
What companies hire industrial security officers in Dallas?+
Allied Universal, Securitas, and several regional Texas-based guard firms hold the major industrial security contracts across Dallas's logistics corridors. Some large manufacturing and distribution operators in the South Dallas and Stemmons zones also hire direct security staff. Both channels are active hiring sources for industrial security in Dallas.
Dallas Industrial Security

Industrial Security Demand in Dallas

Dallas's industrial sector is concentrated in three primary corridors — the Stemmons Freeway zone northwest of downtown, the South Dallas and Hutchins logistics corridor along I-20 and I-35E, and the Trinity Groves and West Dallas industrial area along the Trinity River. Together these zones house manufacturing plants, food processing facilities, freight consolidation operations, utility infrastructure, and cold storage facilities that require continuous security coverage across all three shifts.

Industrial security in Dallas is primarily overnight-heavy. The facilities that house the highest-value assets — freight, raw materials, finished goods inventory, and critical utility infrastructure — operate with reduced staff after business hours, making overnight security the most essential and most in-demand shift window. Vehicle patrol is the standard operating mode across most industrial sites given the scale of the campuses involved.

Cargo theft is an active and growing challenge across Dallas's industrial corridors, which has driven increased investment in security staffing at logistics and distribution operations in recent years. This environment has pushed wages upward for experienced industrial security officers — particularly those with vehicle patrol experience, clean driving records, and demonstrated overnight reliability. Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Does Industrial Security Look Like in Dallas?

Industrial security officers in Dallas primarily work vehicle patrol roles covering large campus footprints that span multiple buildings, loading dock areas, and outdoor yard inventory. Most industrial facilities are too large for effective foot patrol alone — officers cover assigned perimeter and interior routes in patrol vehicles, stopping to conduct physical checks at designated points throughout the shift.

Gate access control is a critical function at busy industrial sites — verifying and logging inbound freight vehicles, authorized maintenance personnel, and after-hours employee access. During peak freight movement periods (often midnight to 4am at major distribution facilities), gate officers manage significant vehicle flow with tight documentation requirements for each transaction.

Industrial security in Dallas is among the most straightforward career entry points in the security market — employers consistently prioritize availability, reliability, and driving record over prior security experience. Officers who prove themselves in overnight industrial patrol frequently move into higher-paying commercial, corporate, or armed roles as they build their security credentials.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Conduct vehicle patrol of industrial campus perimeter, loading dock areas, and yard inventory zones
  • Control vehicle and pedestrian access at facility gates during assigned shift
  • Verify and log all inbound and outbound freight, vehicles, and personnel
  • Monitor surveillance systems and alarm panels at industrial facility control rooms
  • Respond to perimeter alarms, trespassing incidents, and cargo discrepancies
  • Secure building entrances, loading dock doors, and perimeter gates at shift transitions
  • Write accurate shift logs and incident reports — documentation critical at logistics facilities
  • Coordinate with facility management and Dallas PD on cargo theft and trespassing incidents

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Security License required
  • Valid driver's license and clean driving record — effectively required for all vehicle patrol industrial roles
  • Physical stamina for extended outdoor patrol in all weather conditions including Texas summer heat
  • Prior security, military, or logistics experience preferred but not required for entry-level industrial patrol
  • Cargo theft awareness and freight security familiarity a plus at distribution and logistics assignments
  • Overnight availability — the most consistently in-demand shift window across all Dallas industrial sites
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Overnight shifts (10pm–6am or 11pm–7am) are the dominant schedule type at Dallas industrial facilities
  • 12-hour overnight rotations common at larger distribution centers
  • Weekend and holiday coverage required year-round — industrial operations do not pause on weekends
  • Shift differentials of $1–3/hr above base pay standard for overnight industrial hours
  • Vehicle patrol is the standard mode — extended time in a patrol vehicle across large outdoor footprints
  • Outdoor exposure year-round — loading dock areas, perimeter fencing, and yard patrol are standard
