Yes. Is hospital security a good career in DFW?+
Yes — consistently one of the best security career tracks in DFW. Hospital Security Jobs Across DFW

The Dallas–Fort Worth metro is home to one of the largest and most complex healthcare systems in the United States. Major health systems including Parkland Health, UT Southwestern Medical Center, Baylor Scott & White, Texas Health Resources, Methodist Health System, Medical City Healthcare, Children's Health, and the Fort Worth systems — JPS Health Network, Texas Health Harris Methodist, and Cook Children's — collectively employ thousands of healthcare security officers across Dallas and Tarrant counties.

Healthcare security in DFW is one of the most stable and professionally developed security employment categories in the market. Hospitals operate 24/7/365, employ security staff on all three shifts, and consistently fund their security programs even during economic downturns. The combination of recession-resistant employment, competitive pay, and direct institutional employment opportunities at major health systems makes hospital security one of the most sought-after security career tracks in DFW.

The direct county employment opportunities at Parkland Health (Dallas County) and JPS Health Network (Tarrant County) represent the top of the DFW hospital security market — officers employed directly as county employees receive full government benefits including pension plans, health insurance, and structured pay scales that significantly exceed standard security industry compensation. These positions are competitive but consistently available as both systems continue to grow.

Role Overview

What Hospital Security Jobs Are Available in DFW?

Emergency department security is the highest-intensity and typically highest-compensating healthcare security category in DFW. Parkland's Level I trauma center and JPS Health Network's emergency department handle the highest-acuity patient populations in their respective counties — requiring officers with strong de-escalation skills, physical response capability, and composure in high-stress clinical environments. These positions are not entry-level and typically require prior security or law enforcement experience.

General hospital security across DFW's mid-size and community hospitals — Baylor, Texas Health, Methodist, Medical City, and dozens of suburban and specialty facilities — provides a more structured and accessible introduction to healthcare security. Access control, patient safety monitoring, visitor management, and campus patrol are the primary functions at non-trauma hospital settings.

Specialty healthcare security at Children's Health and Cook Children's presents a unique environment — officers work in proximity to critically ill children and their families in a highly emotionally charged setting. Pediatric hospital security requires specific temperament and de-escalation skill sets that are genuinely distinct from adult hospital security. Officers who build credentials in pediatric hospital security develop a specialized profile valued across DFW's healthcare security market.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Control access to patient care areas, emergency departments, and restricted clinical zones
  • Respond to Code Gray behavioral health incidents and combative patient situations
  • Conduct de-escalation with patients, visitors, and family members
  • Patrol hospital campuses, parking structures, and facility perimeters
  • Escort staff, patients, and vendors throughout facility campuses
  • Assist clinical staff during medical emergencies and security-sensitive situations
  • Coordinate with Dallas PD, Fort Worth PD, and hospital medical staff on incidents
  • Write detailed incident reports reviewed by clinical and regulatory teams

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Security License required — CPR/First Aid required at most DFW hospitals
  • De-escalation training required or provided at Level I trauma centers and behavioral health facilities
  • Prior security, military, or law enforcement experience preferred at most DFW hospital systems
  • Physical ability to respond quickly and assist clinical staff in demanding environments
  • Strong written documentation skills — hospital incident reports have regulatory and legal significance
  • Comfort working in clinical environments around patients, medical equipment, and high-acuity situations
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • All three shifts available — DFW hospitals operate 24/7/365
  • 12-hour rotating shifts standard at Parkland, JPS, and major system hospitals
  • Overnight and weekend shifts carry additional differentials on top of healthcare base rates
  • Holiday coverage required — hospital security staffing does not reduce on holidays
  • Emergency department assignments typically require probationary period on general hospital security first
  • Direct county employment at Parkland and JPS includes structured pay scales with annual increases
