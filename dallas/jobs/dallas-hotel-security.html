What does hotel security pay in DFW?+
Entry-level hotel security in DFW runs $15–18/hr at budget and limited-service properties. Full-service and convention hotel security earns $18–24/hr. Luxury property and senior hotel security positions reach $24–32/hr. DFW Airport corridor hotels often pay at the upper end of the range given the 24-hour operational demand.
Is hotel security a good job in DFW?+
Yes — particularly for candidates who enjoy a service-oriented environment with varied daily interactions. Hotel security in DFW provides exposure to hospitality operations, consistent scheduling, and a professional working environment that builds credentials applicable to corporate and VIP security careers. The customer service dimension of hotel security is both a challenge and a differentiator for candidates who excel at it.
What is the difference between hotel security and event security at hotels?+
Hotel security is an ongoing operational role — officers manage the property's daily security needs across all shifts. Event security at hotels is shift-specific and tied to conventions, galas, and ballroom events. Many full-service DFW hotels employ both — a core hotel security team supplemented by contracted event security officers during convention periods.
Do DFW hotels hire security officers directly?+
Some do. Larger independent and branded properties in downtown Dallas and Fort Worth maintain in-house security departments and hire directly. Many DFW hotels contract security through Allied Universal or Securitas. Direct-hire hotel security positions often include hotel employee benefits — a meaningful advantage given the hospitality industry's benefit packages.
What hotels in DFW have the most security openings?+
The Omni Dallas, Marriott Marquis Dallas, Hyatt Regency Dallas, and Hilton Anatole are the largest single-property hotel security employers in downtown Dallas. The DFW Airport Hyatt Regency, Marriott, and Westin properties are the largest in the airport corridor. The Omni Fort Worth and Worthington Renaissance are the primary Fort Worth hotel security employers. All of these properties maintain active security programs year-round.

Role Overview

What Does Hotel Security Look Like in DFW?

Hotel security officers in DFW manage a uniquely diverse set of responsibilities across a single shift. Lobby security involves monitoring guest and visitor access, managing disturbances or unauthorized individuals, and serving as a security-aware point of contact for guests reporting safety concerns. Parking structure security covers one of the highest-risk areas on most hotel campuses — where vehicle break-ins, theft, and unauthorized parking are the primary issues.

Overnight hotel security at DFW properties is a multi-zone function — officers conduct floor patrol of guest corridors, monitor lobby cameras, respond to noise complaints, assist distressed guests, and manage any after-hours incidents without the daytime support staff present. The overnight hotel security officer is effectively the property's entire security and guest services operation during the early morning hours.

Convention and event security at Dallas's convention center hotels peaks dramatically during major convention weeks — requiring significantly above-normal security staffing for event ingress, ballroom monitoring, and VIP protection. Officers with event security experience are more competitive for these roles than standard hotel security candidates.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Monitor lobby access and manage unauthorized individuals or disturbances
  • Conduct floor patrol of guest corridors and common areas throughout the shift
  • Provide parking structure security and assist guests with vehicle-related issues
  • Respond to guest complaints, noise disturbances, and room-related security calls
  • Staff event and convention security during hotel ballroom and meeting room events
  • Monitor hotel CCTV systems and review footage for security incidents
  • Coordinate with hotel management and local law enforcement on incidents
  • Write detailed incident reports for hotel security and management review

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Security License required
  • Professional hospitality presentation — hotel security officers represent the property brand
  • Strong verbal communication and de-escalation skills for guest-facing incident management
  • Prior security, hospitality, or customer service experience all relevant
  • Overnight availability — overnight hotel security is consistently the most available shift
  • Bilingual English/Spanish competitive across DFW Airport corridor and Las Colinas hotel properties
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Overnight shifts (11pm–7am) are the most consistently available at DFW hotels
  • All three shifts available at larger full-service hotels with 24-hour security programs
  • Convention calendar drives above-normal demand — downtown Dallas and Fort Worth convention weeks
  • Weekend demand higher at leisure properties; weekday demand higher at business travel hotels
  • DFW Airport corridor properties have consistent demand across all shifts given 24-hour airport operations
  • Part-time and flexible event security available at convention properties during peak calendar periods
