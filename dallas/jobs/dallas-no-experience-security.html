FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get a security job in Dallas with no experience? +
Yes. Many security employers in Dallas hire candidates without prior security experience — particularly for event security, residential community gate positions, and retail floor presence roles. The minimum requirements are typically a valid Texas Level II guard card, a clean background check, and reliable transportation. Customer service, hospitality, retail, and military backgrounds are all considered relevant.
How do I get a guard card in Dallas with no experience? +
To get a Texas Level II Security Officer Commission, you need to complete a DPS-approved training course (typically 30 hours, available in weekend intensive formats), pass a written exam, submit fingerprints through IdentoGO, and apply through the Texas DPS Private Security online portal. Total cost is typically $100–$260. You do not need prior security experience to enroll. The full process takes 2–4 weeks.
What security jobs in Dallas hire immediately? +
Event security positions and residential community gate jobs are the most likely to move quickly in Dallas. Large guard firms like Allied Universal and Securitas hire on an ongoing basis and can sometimes move from application to first shift within 1–2 weeks once your guard card is active. Signing up for job alerts is the best way to be notified the moment a position that matches your profile opens.
Do security jobs in Dallas drug test? +
Yes — the majority of Dallas security employers require a pre-employment drug screen, and many conduct random testing throughout employment. This applies to all role types including entry-level unarmed positions. Candidates should be prepared for a standard urine drug test as part of the hiring process.
How much do entry-level security jobs pay in Dallas? +
Entry-level unarmed security in Dallas typically starts at $14–17/hr. Overnight and weekend shifts carry an additional $1–3/hr differential. Most officers move into the $17–22/hr range within 12–18 months of building a track record. Armed roles, which require additional licensing, pay meaningfully more — typically $20–28/hr at the entry level.
What is the fastest way to get a security job in Dallas? +
The fastest path is: 1) enroll in a weekend intensive Level II guard card course, 2) pass the written exam, 3) submit your DPS application and fingerprints immediately, 4) apply to large guard firms like Allied Universal and Securitas while your application processes — they can often conditionally onboard you during the DPS review window. Entry-Level in Dallas

Can You Get a Security Job in Dallas With No Experience?

Yes — and Dallas is one of the better markets in Texas to do it. The city's high volume of security job openings, driven by its mix of corporate campuses, residential communities, retail centers, and entertainment venues, means employers consistently hire candidates without prior security backgrounds. The baseline requirements for most entry-level positions are a valid Texas Level II Security License, a clean background check, and reliable transportation.

Event security is typically the easiest entry point — venues like those in the Deep Ellum and Uptown entertainment corridors regularly hire flexible staff with no prior security experience for crowd management and access control during events. Residential community gate security and retail floor presence positions are the next most accessible, often hiring candidates whose only prior experience is in customer service, hospitality, or retail.

The path from no-experience entry to competitive mid-level security work in Dallas typically runs 12–18 months. Officers who build a track record at residential or retail sites move into corporate campus and healthcare assignments where pay is meaningfully higher. Active hiring zip codes for entry-level work include 75201, 75204, 75207, 75219, 75226, and 75247.

Role Overview

What Security Jobs in Dallas Don't Require Experience?

Several security role types in Dallas are consistently accessible to candidates without prior experience. Event security — staffing entrances, managing crowd flow, and monitoring sections at venues — is the most common first job in the industry. Residential community security, covering gate access and patrol at apartment complexes and HOA properties across Dallas, is another well-documented entry point.

Retail security and loss prevention positions at shopping centers along Preston Road, Mockingbird Lane, and the Design District retail corridor often hire based on customer service background rather than security experience. Warehouse and industrial overnight positions in south Dallas and along the I-20 corridor also commonly hire without experience, prioritizing availability and reliability over a security resume.

What almost all entry-level Dallas security jobs do require is a valid Texas Level II Security Officer Commission — commonly called a guard card. The good news is that obtaining one typically takes 2–4 weeks and costs $100–$260 total. Some Dallas employers will conditionally hire you while your application is being processed by DPS.

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need to Get Started

  • Valid Texas Level II Security License (guard card) — required before starting most paid security work
  • Clean criminal background check — no felonies, no Class A or B misdemeanors within the past 20 years
  • Reliable transportation — most Dallas security sites are not accessible by public transit
  • Customer service, hospitality, retail, or military backgrounds all count as relevant experience
  • Willingness to work evenings, weekends, and overnight shifts significantly expands available positions
  • Basic English writing skills for shift logs and incident reports required at most sites
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Event security shifts are scheduled around event dates — primarily evenings and weekends
  • Residential community gate positions often have consistent fixed schedules — good for candidates wanting predictability
  • Overnight and weekend shifts are the most accessible for entry-level candidates and carry the best pay rates relative to experience
  • Retail security positions typically follow store hours — day and evening shifts most common
  • Many Dallas employers offer part-time and flexible scheduling for entry-level candidates building their hours
  • Full-time opportunities become more available after 3–6 months of demonstrated reliability at a site
