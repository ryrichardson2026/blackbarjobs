Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Does an Unarmed Security Officer Do?

Unarmed security officers provide visible deterrence, access control, and incident response without carrying a firearm. In Plano's corporate-heavy environment, the role often blends traditional security functions with professional front-of-house responsibilities — greeting visitors, managing badge access, and serving as the first point of contact for anyone entering a secured facility.

Day shifts at Legacy Business Park campuses tend to be higher-visibility and more customer-facing. Evening and overnight shifts shift toward independent patrol, monitoring, and documentation. Healthcare assignments add a de-escalation component that is less common in purely commercial settings.

Unarmed positions are a strong entry point for candidates new to security, as well as a preferred long-term option for officers who want professional, stable daytime schedules without the additional licensing burden of armed roles.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Manage lobby access, visitor sign-in, and badge issuance at corporate facilities
  • Conduct scheduled interior and exterior patrols throughout the shift
  • Monitor CCTV systems and access control panels
  • Respond to disturbances, medical situations, and alarm activations
  • Write accurate shift logs and incident reports for each assignment
  • Coordinate with building management, tenants, and law enforcement as needed
  • Enforce parking and trespassing policies on corporate or retail properties
  • Provide visible security presence that deters theft, vandalism, and unauthorized access

Do I need a guard card for unarmed security work in Texas? +
Yes. Texas requires a valid Security Officer Commission (Level II) through the Department of Public Safety for all paid unarmed security work. Unlike armed roles, there is no firearm qualification requirement — the Level II commission covers unarmed officers. Most candidates can complete the required training and obtain their commission within 2–4 weeks through a state-approved provider.

What's the difference between unarmed and armed security jobs in Plano? +
Unarmed security officers carry no firearm and require only a Texas Level II Security License. Armed officers carry a weapon and must hold a Level III Armed Commission, which requires additional training and qualification. Armed positions pay more — typically $5–10/hr above comparable unarmed roles — but carry greater liability and licensing overhead. Most Plano corporate campus and retail assignments are unarmed; armed roles in Plano tend to be concentrated at financial institutions, data centers, and high-value asset sites.
