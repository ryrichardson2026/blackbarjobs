Frequently Asked Questions

Which Austin areas have the most overnight warehouse security jobs?+
Austin overnight warehouse security concentrates along the SH-130 corridor east of the city, anchored by Amazon fulfillment centers in San Marcos, Pflugerville, Hutto, and Del Valle, with additional distribution around Round Rock, Georgetown, and the Buda and Kyle corridor to the south. These facilities run 24 hours a day, which makes third shift the hardest segment to staff.
What does overnight warehouse security pay in Austin?+
Entry-level overnight warehouse security in Austin runs about $15-18/hr, with overnight differentials of $1-3/hr on top of base. Experienced officers at major distribution centers earn $18-22/hr, and armed or supervisory posts reach $22-25/hr. A driving access-control post at a busy fulfillment site commonly runs around $18-20/hr for an overnight schedule.
What types of overnight warehouse security roles are there?+
Overnight warehouse security in Austin breaks into distribution center vehicle patrol across large footprints, fulfillment center security at high-throughput Amazon sites, cold storage and food distribution posts, truck gate and access control that processes freight and logs trailers, and trailer yard and dock patrol where staged cargo sits overnight.
Is overnight warehouse security dangerous in Austin?+
The primary risk at Austin warehouse accounts is cargo theft, not physical confrontation. Officers are expected to observe, document, and report rather than intervene in an active theft. Most overnight distribution work is quiet, independent patrol. The practical challenges are staying alert through a graveyard shift and the drive home afterward.
What do I need to get hired for overnight warehouse security in Austin?+
A valid Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license covers most unarmed warehouse posts, and armed posts require a Level 3 commission. A valid driver's license is expected because most distribution posts are vehicle patrol, and reliable transportation matters since the SH-130 corridor has limited overnight transit. Prior gate or access control experience helps toward the higher-paying posts.
Overview

Austin Warehouse Security: The SH-130 Distribution Corridor

Austin's distribution footprint has grown fast along the SH-130 corridor east of the city, anchored by Amazon fulfillment centers in San Marcos, Pflugerville, Hutto, and Del Valle, plus distribution around Round Rock, Georgetown, and the Buda and Kyle corridor to the south. These facilities run around the clock, and overnight security is consistently the hardest segment to keep staffed.

Overnight warehouse security is patrol-and-gate work. Officers cover large distribution footprints by vehicle, control the truck and employee gates, verify manifests and seals, and log every load moving through during the overnight window. Firms like GardaWorld and Allied Universal staff the fulfillment-center and distribution accounts across the metro.

Cargo theft and facility protection drive overnight staffing, and officers are expected to observe, document, and report rather than physically intervene. Entry is straightforward for officers with a Level 2 license and reliable transportation, and gate and access control experience moves you toward the higher-paying supervisory and armed posts.

Roles & Duties
Role Types

Types of Overnight Warehouse Security Roles

  • Distribution center vehicle patrol, covering large multi-building footprints, dock arrays, trailer yards, and perimeter fencing
  • Fulfillment center security at high-throughput Amazon and e-commerce sites along SH-130
  • Cold storage and food distribution posts, adding temperature-controlled facility checks
  • Truck gate and access control, processing freight during peak windows, verifying manifests and logging trailers
  • Trailer yard and dock patrol, focused on perimeter and yard checks where staged cargo sits overnight
Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Run scheduled overnight patrol of the assigned distribution or warehouse facility, every point logged with a timestamp
  • Staff truck gate and access control posts during peak freight windows, typically midnight to 5am at major centers
  • Verify manifests, check trailer seals, and keep chain-of-custody records for every load entering and leaving
  • Monitor CCTV and alarm systems from the overnight console at larger distribution and fulfillment accounts
  • Observe, document, and report cargo theft activity rather than physically intervening
  • Complete gate logs and access records, the primary output of most overnight warehouse posts
  • Conduct perimeter and trailer yard checks across the facility footprint through the shift
  • Maintain radio check-ins with dispatch throughout the overnight shift

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license, required for unarmed warehouse posts
  • Level 3 commission for armed posts at higher-value freight accounts, which pay above unarmed rates
  • Valid Texas driver's license, since most distribution center posts are vehicle patrol
  • Reliable personal transportation, since the SH-130 corridor sites have limited overnight transit
  • Prior truck gate or access control experience preferred at busy fulfillment accounts
  • Genuine overnight availability, since these accounts need consistent third shift coverage
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Third shift 10pm to 6am or 11pm to 7am, the graveyard window when warehouse theft risk peaks
  • Peak freight processing runs midnight to 5am at major fulfillment centers
  • 4x10 patterns common at 24/7 distribution accounts to reduce shift handoffs
  • Weekend overnight is the hardest segment to fill and the easiest to get hired into
  • Consistent voluntary overtime, since overnight understaffing is common across Austin logistics
  • Weekly pay common at the larger security firms
