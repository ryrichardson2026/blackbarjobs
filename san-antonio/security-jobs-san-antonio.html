How do I get a Texas security license in San Antonio?+
Level 2 (Non-Commissioned, unarmed): complete a 6-hour DPS-approved online course for $47–$100 (providers include Defencify Training at $47, TEEX, TX Security Academy, SRT Security); submit your TOPS application with approximately $40 DPS fee; complete IdentoGO fingerprinting for approximately $30; wait 2–4 weeks for DPS processing. Total cost: $117–$170. You can begin working once your status shows ACTIVE in the TOPS system — no need to wait for the physical pocket card. Level 3 (Commissioned, armed): Level 2 first, then 45-hour course from DPS-licensed Level III Training School (in-person firearms and defensive tactics required per HB 3424, January 2024), DPS range qualification at 70% minimum, MMPI psychological evaluation. Total Level 3 cost: $350–$610. Minimum age 21.

What makes San Antonio's security market different from DFW or Houston?+
Three things distinguish SA's security market. First, the veteran pipeline — JBSA's three installations (Lackland, Fort Sam Houston, Randolph) plus eight operating locations continuously transition service members with security-relevant backgrounds into the civilian workforce. No other Texas city has this volume of veteran candidates or the DoD-adjacent security employment that results from it. Second, institutional employer depth — USAA and Valero both hire security officers directly at compensation levels ($21–$30/hr with corporate benefits and pension) that contracted security firms can't match. Third, the military city's bilingual reality — San Antonio is a majority-Hispanic city where bilingual Spanish is not a nice-to-have but a genuine market differentiator that measurably improves candidate placement across hospital, hospitality, and residential security accounts.
How does SA's security market perform year-round vs seasonally?+
San Antonio's security market is more year-round stable than most Texas metros because its four economic pillars — military, healthcare, tourism, and manufacturing — don't share a seasonal cycle. JBSA, Methodist, University Health, and Toyota run 24/7/365 regardless of season. The River Walk's tourism economy has genuine seasonality (Fiesta in April, holiday season, convention peaks) but doesn't reduce to the point where security employment contracts materially. School security follows the academic calendar for K-12 campus positions but UTSA and Alamo Colleges run year-round. The biggest seasonal variation is event security — Fiesta San Antonio's 11-day April run creates the largest single event security employment spike of the year, but the Alamodome's year-round calendar, the Spurs season (October–April), and the JW Marriott's convention schedule sustain consistent demand outside of Fiesta.
