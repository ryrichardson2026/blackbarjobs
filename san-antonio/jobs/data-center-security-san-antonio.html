Overview

San Antonio Data Center Security: The West-Side Cloud Corridor

San Antonio is one of the largest data center markets in Texas, anchored by Microsoft's long-running west-side campus at Westover Hills and a growing cluster of cloud and colocation operators along the far west side. These are mission-critical, 24/7 sites where physical security runs continuously and access control is the core of the job.

Data center security is a step up from standard patrol. Posts center on the security operations console, access control and badging, loading dock and equipment intake, and escorting vendors and technicians inside restricted space. Many campuses run cleared or screened programs, and firms like Allied Universal staff the officer posts under strict site security standards.

Because these are critical-infrastructure sites, reliability and documentation matter more than physical intervention. Officers control who enters, verify credentials, monitor CCTV and alarms, and log every access event. The clearance or screening path opens the higher-paying posts, and the work is stable, indoor, and consistent year round.

Roles & Duties
Role Types

Types of Data Center Security Roles

  • Security operations console operator, monitoring CCTV, alarms, and access systems across the campus
  • Access control and badging officer, verifying credentials and controlling entry at the lobby and man-traps
  • Loading dock and equipment intake, screening deliveries and inbound hardware
  • Interior escort, accompanying vendors and technicians through restricted server space
  • Perimeter and exterior patrol of the campus footprint and generator yards
Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Control access at lobby, man-trap, and restricted entry points, verifying credentials for every person
  • Monitor CCTV, alarm, and access-control systems from the security operations console
  • Screen and log inbound deliveries and equipment at the loading dock
  • Escort vendors, contractors, and technicians through restricted server halls
  • Maintain access logs and incident documentation to critical-infrastructure standards
  • Conduct interior and perimeter patrols, checking doors, generator yards, and fence lines
  • Respond to alarms and access exceptions, assess, and escalate per site protocol
  • Maintain radio and console contact with the site security team throughout the shift

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license, required for unarmed data center posts
  • Ability to pass site, customer, and in some cases government security screening for cleared campuses
  • Clean background and reliable attendance, since critical-infrastructure posts run continuous coverage
  • Comfort with console and computer work, including CCTV, access-control, and incident-reporting systems
  • Valid Texas driver's license and reliable transportation for west-side campus locations
  • Professional communication for badging, escorting, and vendor interaction
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • 24/7 coverage in 8 or 12 hour shifts, including overnight and weekend rotations
  • Third shift is the quietest and steadiest, focused on access control and monitoring
  • Consistent schedules, since critical-infrastructure sites value continuity over churn
  • Overnight differential common on graveyard rotations
  • Weekly pay common at the larger security firms
  • Indoor, climate-controlled environment, unlike most patrol posts
