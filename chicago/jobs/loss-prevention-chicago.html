Overview

Loss Prevention Officer Jobs in Chicago: Retail and Investigations

Loss prevention covers the retail side of security across Chicago, from the Magnificent Mile and State Street to suburban malls like Woodfield in Schaumburg, Oakbrook Center, and Fashion Outlets of Chicago in Rosemont, plus big-box stores throughout the metro. Roles run both uniformed and plainclothes, and organized retail crime has pushed demand for asset-protection and LP officers at flagship Michigan Avenue stores in particular. Hiring peaks in the fourth quarter, when the holiday shopping season brings a wave of seasonal loss-prevention and asset-protection posts across the Magnificent Mile, State Street, and the big suburban malls, making Q4 the busiest stretch of the year for retail security.

Organized retail crime has pushed demand for investigators who can build cases, not just make floor stops. That specialty pays more and offers a clear path up from entry-level LP work.

Role Overview

What Loss Prevention Officers Do in Chicago

LP officers watch the sales floor uniformed or in plainclothes, monitor camera systems, and make apprehensions strictly within company policy and the law. The job leans heavily on observation, documentation, and restraint.

Investigators go further, building organized retail crime cases, documenting evidence, coordinating with police, and testifying in court. In-house LP programs and contract security roles can differ on licensing, so confirm the requirement with each employer.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Floor surveillance, uniformed or plainclothes
  • Monitoring CCTV and exception-based reporting systems
  • Apprehensions made strictly within company policy and the law
  • Building organized retail crime cases and documenting evidence
  • Coordinating with local police on theft and fraud
  • Writing detailed reports and testifying in court when needed
Compensation

What This Role Pays in Chicago

Chicago loss prevention pays about 18 to 24 dollars an hour for floor roles, with LP investigators and case managers reaching 25 to 30 dollars an hour. In-house corporate programs often pay above contract rates.

FAQ

Security Job FAQs

Common questions from security officer job seekers in Chicago.

Do I need a PERC card for loss prevention jobs in Chicago?
▼
For contract loss prevention roles, yes, a PERC card is generally required. Some in-house retail LP programs handle licensing differently, so confirm the requirement with each employer.
How much do loss prevention officers make in Chicago?
▼
Floor LP roles pay about 18 to 24 dollars an hour. LP investigators and case managers earn 25 to 30 dollars an hour, and in-house corporate programs often pay above contract rates.
What is the difference between uniformed and plainclothes LP?
▼
Uniformed officers provide visible deterrence at entrances and on the floor. Plainclothes officers blend in to observe and document theft. Many programs use both, and assignments can shift by store.
What is organized retail crime work?
▼
Organized retail crime, or ORC, involves coordinated, repeat theft and fraud rings. ORC investigators build cases across incidents, document evidence, and work with police, which is a higher-paying LP specialty.
Where do loss prevention officers work in Chicago?
▼
The Magnificent Mile and State Street, suburban malls like Woodfield and Oakbrook, and big-box retailers across the metro. Demand is steady given the size of the retail base.
