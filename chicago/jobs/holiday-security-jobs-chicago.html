Frequently Asked Questions

When should I apply for holiday security jobs in Chicago?+
Retailers and warehouses post seasonal security roles from August through October and hire September through November for work that runs October through January. Apply in September and October, and get licensed now so you are ready the moment postings go up.
How do I get licensed for seasonal security work in Illinois?+
Illinois requires a PERC card (Permanent Employee Registration Card) from IDFPR plus a 20-hour basic training certificate for unarmed work. The PERC card takes about 4 to 6 weeks to process, so late summer is the best time to start if you want to be ready for holiday hiring.
Which Chicago malls and sites hire seasonal security?+
Retail corridors like the Magnificent Mile and State Street, along with Water Tower Place, Woodfield Mall in Schaumburg, Oakbrook Center, and Fashion Outlets of Chicago in Rosemont, staff loss-prevention and mall-security officers for the shopping season, and the CenterPoint intermodal hub in Joliet plus Amazon distribution centers around Joliet and Aurora add warehouse and gatehouse posts for holiday fulfillment.
Are holiday security jobs part-time or full-time?+
Both. Many seasonal roles are part-time or temporary, which fits students and anyone wanting extra cash for the holidays, but full-time seasonal assignments and paths to permanent work are common too.
Do seasonal security jobs pay well in Chicago?+
Chicago seasonal security pays about $16 to $21 an hour for most retail and warehouse posts, with overnight and weekend shifts adding a differential. It is a solid way to earn extra income during the holidays.
Can a seasonal security job turn into permanent work?+
Often, yes. Retailers and security firms use the holiday season to evaluate seasonal officers, and reliable performers are frequently offered permanent roles after the season ends.
Overview

Holiday & Seasonal Security Hiring in Chicago

Holiday hiring is the biggest seasonal surge in the security calendar, and Chicago runs it hard. From late summer through the fall, retailers along the Magnificent Mile and State Street, plus Water Tower Place, Woodfield Mall in Schaumburg, Oakbrook Center, and Fashion Outlets of Chicago in Rosemont, staff up on loss-prevention and mall-security officers for the shopping season, while the CenterPoint intermodal hub in Joliet and Amazon distribution centers around Joliet and Aurora add warehouse and gatehouse coverage for holiday fulfillment. Allied Universal, GardaWorld, St. Moritz, and Titan Security run the bulk of these seasonal accounts.

The pattern is predictable: postings go up from August through October, hiring runs September through November, and the work covers October through January. Many roles are part-time or temporary, which fits students, second-job seekers, and anyone who wants extra cash for the holidays. Full-time seasonal assignments and paths to permanent work are common too.

Officers who are already licensed and ready to start have a real edge, because seasonal hiring moves fast and the best assignments fill first. The move now, in mid-summer, is to start your Illinois PERC card so you are ready when postings open in September.

Role Overview

What Seasonal Security Jobs Are Available in Chicago?

Holiday security in Chicago spans a few clear role types. Loss-prevention officers, both uniformed and plainclothes, work the malls and big-box stores through the shopping season. Mall and common-area security cover the retail corridors during peak hours. Warehouse and distribution-center officers staff the gatehouse, trailer yard, and docks as fulfillment volume climbs across Chicago's logistics hubs.

Overnight and weekend coverage is the other big bucket, because retail and logistics both run long hours over the holidays. Many of these posts are part-time or temporary, which is ideal for students, people picking up a second job, and anyone who just wants extra cash for the holidays.

Because the season is short, employers value reliability and a clean record over years of experience. An Illinois PERC card and the 20-hour training certificate and open availability, especially nights and weekends, open the most assignments, and strong seasonal performers are often kept on permanently.

  • Incident reporting and coordination with store and site management
