Getting hired

What the process usually looks like from applying to your first shift.

  1. Apply onlineMost Chicago warehouse employers use a short form, not a resume upload. Ten minutes.
  2. Phone screen or textUsually within two to five days. They confirm your shift availability, whether you can lift the stated weight, and how you will get to the facility.
  3. On-site interview or hiring eventOften a walk-through of the floor. Larger distribution centers run open hiring events where you can interview the same day you show up.
  4. Background check and drug screenStandard at most large facilities. Cannabis is legal in Illinois, but employers can still test and can withdraw an offer over a positive result.
  5. Start dateTwo weeks from application is typical. Peak season can be days.

Forklift certification, in detail

OSHA requires certification to operate a powered industrial truck. On this board a forklift role runs about $19 an hour at the median, though the sample is small and rates vary widely by employer, equipment class, and shift. The classroom portion takes one to four hours and costs roughly $40 to $60 online, but that alone does not certify you: OSHA also requires a hands-on evaluation by your employer in the actual workplace, and certification is specific to the class of truck you trained on.

Most Chicago employers pay for the whole process once you are hired, so buying it first is rarely necessary. What it does is broaden the roles and shifts you qualify for and let you start faster, since a certified operator saves an employer four to eight hours of training. Certification is valid for three years, then requires re-evaluation.

Shifts, pay structure and benefits

Shifts

Forklift roles run across first, second, and overnight schedules, and equipment operators are often the first hires a facility makes for a new shift. Overnight and second shift usually add a dollar to three dollars an hour.

Benefits

Full-time roles at larger employers typically include health coverage after 30 to 90 days, a 401(k) with some match, and paid time off that accrues by hours worked. Illinois also requires up to 40 hours of paid leave a year under the Paid Leave for All Workers Act. Part-time and seasonal roles usually carry less.

Union warehouses

Chicago has real union presence in warehousing and distribution, especially at freight carriers, grocery distribution, and some cold storage. Teamsters Local 705 (about 17,000 members locally) and Local 710 (about 12,000 to 13,500 across Illinois, Indiana, and Iowa) both represent warehouse and dock work here. Union facilities generally pay above the medians on this page and have defined step increases, but hire less often and fill by seniority. It is a fair question on the phone screen.

What the work is actually like

A forklift operator moves pallets and product between receiving, storage, and the dock: sit-down counterbalance, reach truck, order picker, or cherry picker depending on the site. It involves far less repetitive lifting than picking and packing, which is why operators with a bad back often move into it.

The tradeoff is responsibility. You are running a heavy machine around people and racking to a rate, with zero tolerance for quantity errors or near misses, and powered-equipment roles are the most likely to be drug tested. Sites vary between climate-controlled and not, and cold storage pays more because it is harder.

Because certification is class-specific and employer-verified, the operator who already holds the classroom portion and can pass the on-site evaluation starts faster than one who cannot.

Common questions

Do I need to be certified before I apply?

Usually not at your own expense. Most Chicago employers pay for certification after hire, and an online course alone does not certify you because OSHA also requires a hands-on employer evaluation. Holding the classroom portion still helps you start faster.

How much do forklift operators make in Chicago?

About $19 an hour at the median on this board, though the sample is small and rates vary widely by employer, equipment, and shift. Overnight and second shift add a dollar to three dollars more.

What kind of forklift will I run?

Depends on the site: sit-down counterbalance, reach truck, order picker, or cherry picker. Certification is specific to the class you trained on, so read the listing.

How long is forklift certification good for?

Three years under OSHA, then it requires re-evaluation. It stays with you across employers, though each new employer still does its own on-site evaluation.

Is forklift work easier on the body than picking?

Generally yes, less repetitive lifting, but it carries more responsibility. You are operating a heavy machine to a rate around people and racking.

Will I be drug tested?

Very likely. Powered-equipment roles are the most likely warehouse jobs to test. Illinois allows lawful off-duty cannabis use but employers can still enforce zero-tolerance policies and withdraw an offer over a positive result.

What does certification cost if I pay myself?

The online classroom portion is roughly $40 to $60 and takes one to four hours, but it does not complete certification on its own. The full process, including the employer evaluation, runs $50 to $300 and most employers cover it.

What shifts are available for forklift roles?

First, second, and overnight. Equipment operators are often among the first hires for a new shift, so overnight openings are common.

Where do forklift jobs go from here?

Operator to lead or trainer, then shift supervisor, then operations. Equipment experience is a common step toward running a zone or a crew.

Do I need a car?

For the I-55 and I-80 distribution corridors, in practice yes. Many city facilities are reachable by CTA or Metra.
