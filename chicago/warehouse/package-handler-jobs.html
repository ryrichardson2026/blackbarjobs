Getting hired

What the process usually looks like from applying to your first shift.

  1. Apply onlineMost Chicago warehouse employers use a short form, not a resume upload. Ten minutes.
  2. Phone screen or textUsually within two to five days. They confirm your shift availability, whether you can lift the stated weight, and how you will get to the facility.
  3. On-site interview or hiring eventOften a walk-through of the floor. Larger distribution centers run open hiring events where you can interview the same day you show up.
  4. Background check and drug screenStandard at most large facilities. Cannabis is legal in Illinois, but employers can still test and can withdraw an offer over a positive result.
  5. Start dateTwo weeks from application is typical. Peak season can be days.

Forklift certification

OSHA requires certification to operate a powered industrial truck, so it is one of the most requested add-ons in warehousing. Most Chicago employers pay for it after hire, and an online course alone does not certify you: OSHA also requires a hands-on employer evaluation. Holding the classroom portion still helps you start faster and broadens the roles and shifts you can be placed in.

Shifts, pay structure and benefits

Shifts

Package handling is built around sort windows, so short, defined shifts are common: early morning, twilight, and overnight sorts, plus weekend loads. Many handler roles are part-time by design, which is why students and second-job workers fill a lot of them.

Benefits

Full-time roles at larger employers typically include health coverage after 30 to 90 days, a 401(k) with some match, and paid time off that accrues by hours worked. Illinois also requires up to 40 hours of paid leave a year under the Paid Leave for All Workers Act. Part-time and seasonal roles usually carry less.

Union warehouses

Chicago has real union presence in warehousing and distribution, especially at freight carriers, grocery distribution, and some cold storage. Teamsters Local 705 (about 17,000 members locally) and Local 710 (about 12,000 to 13,500 across Illinois, Indiana, and Iowa) both represent warehouse and dock work here. Union facilities generally pay above the medians on this page and have defined step increases, but hire less often and fill by seniority. It is a fair question on the phone screen.

What the work is actually like

A package handler loads, unloads, scans, and sorts parcels through a facility to a set rate, often on a conveyor or in a trailer. The pace is fast and steady rather than heavy, though loading and unloading trailers is genuine physical work in whatever temperature the dock is that day.

Because the work is tied to sort windows, shifts are short and defined, and a lot of handler roles are part-time. That makes them a common first warehouse job and a common second job. Parcel carriers and 3PLs are the main employers, and several extend partial benefits even to part-time sort staff.

The usual next step is a full-time associate or equipment role once you have shown up reliably through a peak season.

Common questions

Do package handler jobs need experience?

No. Sortation is one of the most common no-experience warehouse jobs in Chicago. Employers train the process and screen mainly for reliability and showing up to the full shift.

Are package handler jobs full-time or part-time?

Many are part-time by design, built around a sort window of a few hours. Full-time handler and loader roles exist too, especially at freight docks. The listing states which.

How much do package handlers make in Chicago?

Handler roles tend to sit at the lower end of the $13 to $28 warehouse range, against a $20 overall median. Overnight sorts usually pay a differential.

What is the work like?

Loading, unloading, scanning, and sorting parcels to a rate, often on a conveyor or inside a trailer. Fast and steady rather than heavy, though trailer work is physical and the dock is whatever temperature it is outside.

Which companies hire package handlers here?

Parcel carriers and last-mile and 3PL operations are the main employers on this board. Openings turn over constantly because sort volume runs year round and spikes in autumn.

Do part-time handler jobs have benefits?

Usually less than full-time, but several large parcel employers extend partial benefits to part-time sort staff. Check the listing and ask on the phone screen.

What shifts are available?

Early morning, twilight, and overnight sorts, plus weekend loads. Short defined windows are the norm.

Can a package handler job become full-time?

Often, yes. Showing up reliably through a peak season is the usual path to a full-time associate or equipment role.

Will I be drug tested?

Many large facilities test. Illinois allows lawful off-duty cannabis use, but employers can still run zero-tolerance policies and withdraw an offer over a positive result.

Do I need a car?

Many parcel hubs sit near transit, but overnight and early sorts can end before service is frequent. Confirm the commute before you accept an overnight sort.
