Frequently Asked Questions

When does holiday security hiring start?+
Retailers and warehouses post seasonal security roles from August through October and hire September through November for work that runs October through January. The move is to get licensed over the summer so you are ready the moment postings open.

Do I need a license for seasonal security work?+
Yes, and it depends on the state. Texas requires a Level 2 non-commissioned license for unarmed work, a short DPS-approved course. Illinois requires a PERC card plus a 20-hour training certificate, which takes about 4 to 6 weeks to process, so start early.
Are holiday security jobs part-time or full-time?+
Both. Many seasonal roles are part-time or temporary, which fits students and second-job seekers, but full-time seasonal assignments and paths to permanent work are common too.

Overview

Holiday & Seasonal Security Hiring, by the Numbers

Holiday hiring is the biggest seasonal surge in the security calendar. From late summer through the fall, retailers staff up on loss-prevention and mall-security officers for the shopping season, while fulfillment centers and distribution hubs add warehouse and gatehouse coverage as holiday volume climbs. The national firms, Allied Universal, GardaWorld, Securitas, and their regional peers, run the bulk of these seasonal accounts across every major metro.

The pattern is the same city to city: postings go up from August through October, hiring runs September through November, and the work covers October through January. Many roles are part-time or temporary, which fits students, second-job seekers, and anyone who wants extra cash for the holidays. Full-time seasonal assignments and paths to permanent work are common too.

What changes by city is the licensing and the pay. Texas markets hire on a Level 2 non-commissioned license; Chicago hires on an Illinois PERC card and a 20-hour certificate. Pick your city below for local openings, pay ranges, and the exact licensing path.

Role Overview

What Seasonal Security Jobs Are Available?

Holiday security spans a few clear role types across every metro. Loss-prevention officers, both uniformed and plainclothes, work the malls and big-box stores through the shopping season. Mall and common-area security cover the retail corridors during peak hours. Warehouse and distribution-center officers staff the gatehouse, trailer yard, and docks as fulfillment volume climbs.

Overnight and weekend coverage is the other big bucket, because retail and logistics both run long hours over the holidays. Many of these posts are part-time or temporary, which is ideal for students, people picking up a second job, and anyone who just wants extra cash for the holidays.

Because the season is short, employers value reliability and a clean record over years of experience. A license and open availability, especially nights and weekends, open the most assignments, and strong seasonal performers are often kept on permanently.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Uniformed and plainclothes loss prevention at malls and big-box retail
  • Mall, common-area, and parking-structure patrol during peak shopping hours
  • Warehouse and distribution-center gatehouse, trailer, and dock checks
  • Overnight and weekend coverage as holiday fulfillment volume peaks
  • Access control and visitor screening at retail and DC entrances
  • Incident reporting and coordination with store and site management
