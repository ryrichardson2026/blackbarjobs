What is the first step to getting a security job in Dallas with no experience? +
Get your Texas Level II Security Officer Commission. Enroll in a DPS-approved training course — weekend intensive formats complete all required training in a single weekend. After training, pass the written exam, submit fingerprints through IdentoGO, and apply through the Texas DPS portal. The full process takes 2–4 weeks. Once your commission is active, you are eligible for the majority of entry-level Dallas security positions.
What is the easiest security job to get in Dallas? +
Event security is typically the easiest entry point — venues and guard firms staffing Deep Ellum and Uptown events regularly hire candidates with minimal experience as long as they have a guard card and can pass a background check. Residential gate security is the second most accessible category, and warehouse overnight patrol is the most consistent option for candidates who can work nights.
How long does it take to move from entry-level to better-paying security in Dallas? +
Most officers move from entry-level ($14–17/hr) to mid-range ($17–22/hr) positions within 6–18 months of consistent work. The fastest path is to perform reliably at your first assignment, communicate your availability and willingness to take on more shifts, and express interest in advancement to your supervisor. Dallas guard firms consistently promote from their existing rosters.
Do entry-level security jobs in Dallas offer benefits? +
It varies significantly by employer. Large national guard firms like Allied Universal and Securitas offer benefits packages including health insurance, paid time off, and retirement contributions for full-time employees. Part-time and flexible positions typically do not include benefits. Some Dallas hospital systems that hire security directly offer comprehensive benefits even at the entry level.
Can I get a security job in Dallas while I'm waiting for my guard card? +
Some employers will conditionally onboard you — completing HR paperwork, background checks, and site orientation — while your DPS application processes. Others require an active commission before scheduling your first shift. Ask each employer directly about their policy during the DPS processing window. Entry-Level Security Jobs in Dallas

Dallas is one of the most accessible cities in Texas for candidates starting a security career without prior experience. The city's high volume of retail, residential, and event security openings means there is a consistent pipeline of positions that prioritize availability, reliability, and basic qualifications over an established security resume.

The most accessible entry-level paths in Dallas are event security (flexible staffing at Deep Ellum and Uptown venues), residential community gate security (HOA and apartment complex coverage, often weekend-only), and retail floor presence at shopping centers along Preston Road and Mockingbird Lane. These role types are explicitly structured for candidates who are new to security and willing to build their skills on the job.

The one consistent requirement across all entry-level Dallas security jobs is a valid Texas Level II Security Officer Commission — the guard card. Getting licensed takes 2–4 weeks and costs roughly $100–$260. Once you have it, Dallas's volume of entry-level openings makes finding your first security job relatively straightforward. Active hiring zip codes include 75201, 75204, 75219, 75226, and 75235.

Role Overview

What Entry-Level Security Jobs Are Available in Dallas?

Entry-level security jobs in Dallas span several role types, each with different demands and scheduling structures. Event security is the most flexible — you work specific events rather than fixed shifts, which makes it easy to start part-time. Residential community gate security offers more predictable scheduling — many Dallas HOA and apartment communities post specifically for weekend gate officers.

Retail and loss prevention floor presence positions at Dallas shopping centers are another strong entry point. Employers in this category value customer service orientation over security experience, making candidates from retail management, food service, and hospitality backgrounds highly competitive. Warehouse and industrial overnight security along the I-20 and Stemmons corridors rounds out the entry-level landscape — these roles prioritize availability and reliability above all else.

Most entry-level Dallas security positions include on-the-job training for site-specific procedures. What employers universally screen for is a professional demeanor, consistent availability, and the basic licensing requirement. Candidates who show up reliably and handle their first assignment well move quickly into better-paying and more stable positions.

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Attend required ongoing training and recertification sessions as scheduled by employer

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level II Security License (guard card) — the one non-negotiable requirement
  • Clean criminal background check — no felonies, no Class A or B misdemeanors in the past 20 years
  • Reliable transportation to and from assigned sites
  • Professional appearance — uniformed security roles require a neat, presentable presentation
  • Weekend and evening availability dramatically increases entry-level options in Dallas
  • No prior security experience required for most entry-level event, residential, and retail positions
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Entry-level positions span all shift types — evenings and weekends offer the most availability for new candidates
  • Event security is flexible — you get called for specific events rather than scheduled for fixed shifts
  • Residential gate positions often have fixed weekend schedules — Saturday and Sunday coverage in demand
  • Retail security follows store hours — day and evening shifts most available for entry-level candidates
  • Many Dallas employers offer part-time entry-level hours that can build toward full-time over 3–6 months
  • Overnight entry-level positions in warehouse and industrial zones are among the most consistently available in Dallas
