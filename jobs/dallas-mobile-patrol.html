Do I need my own car for mobile patrol jobs in Dallas? +
It depends on the employer. Many larger guard firms provide company patrol vehicles for their route officers. Smaller regional firms may require you to use your own vehicle with mileage reimbursement. Always confirm the vehicle arrangement before accepting a mobile patrol position.
What does mobile patrol pay in Dallas? +
Entry-level mobile patrol in Dallas runs $16–19/hr — above standard unarmed security rates. Experienced patrol officers earn $19–25/hr. Senior patrol supervisors managing multiple routes or armed patrol positions reach $25–32/hr. Overnight differentials apply on top of base pay.
Is mobile patrol harder than stationary security in Dallas? +
It's different rather than harder. Stationary posts require sustained alertness at a single location. Mobile patrol requires active driving, navigation across multiple properties, and the flexibility to respond to incidents that break from the standard route. Officers who prefer variety and independence typically find patrol more engaging than stationary work.
Do I need a guard card for mobile patrol in Dallas? +
Yes. A valid Texas Level II Security Officer Commission is required for all paid mobile patrol security work. A clean driving record and valid Texas driver's license are equally non-negotiable. Most candidates can obtain their guard card within 2–4 weeks of enrolling in a DPS-approved training course.
What companies hire mobile patrol officers in Dallas? +

Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Does a Mobile Patrol Officer Do in Dallas?

Mobile patrol officers drive a designated route covering multiple client properties during their shift — conducting visual checks, testing locks and gates, monitoring for trespassing or vandalism, and documenting any incidents at each stop. In Dallas, routes commonly cover a mix of commercial properties, retail centers, industrial facilities, and residential communities within a defined geographic zone.

Each client site on a patrol route gets a timed visit — officers scan the property, check access points, look for anything out of the ordinary, log their visit in a patrol management system, and move to the next stop. Response calls from clients within the route zone are also part of the job — an alarm activation or reported incident means breaking from the standard route to respond.

Mobile patrol is well-suited for candidates who prefer an active, varied shift over a stationary post. The combination of driving, physical inspection, and response work keeps the shift moving — and the variety of client properties on a typical Dallas route means no two shifts are exactly alike.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Drive assigned patrol route covering 10–20 client sites per shift on a defined schedule
  • Conduct visual property checks, lock and gate tests, and perimeter inspections at each stop
  • Monitor for trespassing, vandalism, suspicious vehicles, and unauthorized access
  • Respond to alarm activations and client-reported incidents within the patrol zone
  • Log each site visit in patrol management software with timestamps and observations
  • Write detailed incident reports for any security events encountered on route
  • Coordinate with Dallas PD on incidents requiring law enforcement response
  • Maintain patrol vehicle in clean and operational condition throughout the shift

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level II Security License required
  • Valid Texas driver's license and clean driving record — non-negotiable for all mobile patrol positions
  • Comfortable operating a patrol vehicle for extended periods in all weather and traffic conditions
  • Prior security, law enforcement, or military experience preferred but not always required
  • Strong independent judgment — mobile patrol officers work alone with minimal supervision
  • Familiarity with patrol management software or willingness to learn — most Dallas guard firms use digital patrol logging systems
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Overnight and evening routes (6pm–6am) are the most common for mobile patrol in Dallas
  • 8 and 12-hour shift options available depending on the employer and route assignment
  • Weekend coverage consistently required — commercial and residential patrol demand does not slow on weekends
  • Some Dallas guard firms offer split route coverage with day patrol routes for residential communities
  • Shift differentials of $1–3/hr above base pay standard for overnight patrol hours
  • Routes are geographically assigned — officers typically cover the same zone each shift, building familiarity with client properties
