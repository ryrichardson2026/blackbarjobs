Do I need a guard card for warehouse security in Dallas? +
Yes. A valid Texas Level II Security Officer Commission is required for all paid warehouse security work. Armed posts at high-value facilities require a Level III Armed Commission. Most candidates can complete training and obtain their commission within 2–4 weeks.
Do I need a driver's license for warehouse security in Dallas? +
For the majority of Dallas warehouse security positions, yes. Dallas industrial sites are large campuses where vehicle patrol is the standard operating mode — a valid Texas driver's license and clean driving record are effectively required for most overnight industrial patrol openings in the Dallas area.
What companies hire warehouse security officers in Dallas? +
Allied Universal, Securitas, and several regional Texas-based guard firms hold contracts at major Dallas distribution centers. Large e-commerce operators, cold storage companies, and freight forwarding firms in the South Dallas and Stemmons corridor contract through these firms for their overnight warehouse security coverage.
Are overnight warehouse security jobs in Dallas easy to get? +
Overnight industrial security is one of the more accessible entry points in the Dallas security market. What does warehouse security pay in Dallas? +
Entry-level warehouse security in Dallas runs $15–18/hr with overnight differentials of $1–3/hr. Experienced officers at large distribution centers earn $18–24/hr. Senior patrol supervisors and armed warehouse posts reach $24–30/hr. Cargo-sensitive and high-value freight facilities tend to pay at the upper end of the range.

Warehouse & Industrial Security Demand in Dallas

Dallas's position as a major logistics hub — sitting at the intersection of I-20, I-35E, I-45, and I-30 — has made the city one of the most warehouse-dense metros in the southern United States. The distribution and logistics corridor stretching from South Dallas through Hutchins, Wilmer, and Lancaster to the south, and along the Stemmons Freeway corridor to the north, supports millions of square feet of warehouse and distribution space requiring continuous security coverage.

E-commerce fulfillment operations, cold storage facilities, freight forwarding companies, and regional distribution centers for major national retailers all operate within Dallas city limits and the immediate surrounding area. These facilities run 24-hour operations — and their overnight shifts, when high-value inventory is most vulnerable, generate the strongest demand for security officers willing to work nights.

Cargo theft is an active and growing concern in the Dallas logistics corridor, making warehouse security a higher-priority investment for facility operators than it was a decade ago. This has pushed wages for experienced overnight warehouse security officers upward, particularly for candidates with vehicle patrol experience and clean driving records. Role Overview

What Does a Warehouse Security Officer Do in Dallas?

Warehouse security officers in Dallas primarily work in vehicle patrol roles covering large industrial campuses that span hundreds of thousands of square feet across multiple buildings. The scale of Dallas's distribution facilities makes foot patrol alone impractical — most positions require a valid driver's license and comfort operating a patrol vehicle across large outdoor properties in all weather conditions.

Common duties include gate access control for incoming and outgoing freight, perimeter patrol, overnight monitoring of loading dock areas and yard inventory, and coordination with facility management on any access or theft incidents. Documentation accuracy is particularly important at logistics and distribution facilities where inventory discrepancies trigger formal investigations.

Overnight warehouse security in Dallas is one of the most accessible overnight security roles for candidates without an extensive security background — employers consistently prioritize availability, reliability, and a clean driving record over prior security experience for entry-level industrial patrol positions.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Conduct vehicle patrol of warehouse campus perimeter, loading dock areas, and yard inventory
  • Control vehicle and pedestrian access at facility gates during assigned shift
  • Monitor surveillance systems and alarm panels at distribution and logistics facilities
  • Verify and log incoming and outgoing freight and vehicle access
  • Respond to alarms, trespassing incidents, and cargo discrepancies
  • Secure building entrances, loading dock doors, and perimeter gates at shift start and end
  • Write accurate shift logs and incident reports — documentation critical at logistics facilities
  • Coordinate with facility management and law enforcement on cargo theft and trespassing incidents
