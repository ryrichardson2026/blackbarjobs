Role Overview

What Does an Armed Security Officer Do?

Armed security officers are deployed in environments where a visible, credentialed deterrent is required. Common work settings include banks, jewelry retailers, cannabis dispensaries, government facilities, data centers, and high-value logistics operations.

Most armed positions involve stationary post assignments, access control, and monitoring. Employers typically require a valid armed security license, a clean background check, and demonstrated firearms proficiency. Some positions include patrol responsibilities via vehicle or foot.

Shifts vary widely — armed roles often include overnight and weekend coverage. Full-time, part-time, and contract arrangements are all common depending on the employer and site requirements.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Maintain armed post at assigned location
  • Control and monitor access points
  • Conduct foot or vehicle patrols of premises
  • Monitor surveillance and alarm systems
  • Respond to incidents, disturbances, and emergencies
  • Write detailed incident and daily activity reports
  • Coordinate with law enforcement when required
  • Deter theft, vandalism, and unauthorized access
  • Escort personnel, assets, or cash as directed

Requirements & Experience
Requirements

Training & Credential Requirements

Valid Texas Security License (Level II or higher)
Armed Security License (Level III) — required for armed roles
Clean criminal background check
Firearms qualification and proficiency documentation
CPR / First Aid certification (preferred by most employers)
Valid driver's license (required for patrol roles)

Requirements vary by employer and site. Verify directly with the hiring company.

Experience

Experience Requirements

  • Entry-level roles available for candidates with a valid armed license and no prior security experience
  • 1–2 years of prior security experience preferred for most employer postings
  • Military or law enforcement background is consistently preferred and may qualify for higher starting pay
  • Customer service or public-facing work experience is considered relevant by many employers
  • Physical requirements typically include standing for extended periods, walking patrol routes, and responding quickly to incidents

Schedules

Typical Schedules & Work Environments

  • Day shifts (6am–2pm or 7am–3pm) common at banks, office buildings, and retail
  • Overnight shifts (10pm–6am) frequently available with shift differentials
  • Weekend availability often required — many sites operate 7 days
  • Rotating 8 or 12-hour schedules common with contract security firms
  • Indoor post assignments at lobbies, vaults, and access points
  • Outdoor patrol in varying weather conditions for perimeter and parking assignments
  • Extended standing and walking expected in most roles
