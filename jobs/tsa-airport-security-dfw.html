FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What does TSA pay at DFW Airport?+
TSA Transportation Security Officers at DFW International Airport are paid on the federal TSA pay band system. Entry-level TSO: Band D, starting at approximately $22–$25/hr. With experience and performance: Band E, $26–$33/hr. Senior TSO and Lead TSO: Band F, $33–$46/hr. DFW is one of the largest airports in the US and a high-priority TSA location — officers at DFW are eligible for locality pay, federal benefits (FEHB health insurance, FERS retirement, TSP 401k-equivalent, paid leave), and TSA-specific benefits including uniform allowance. Total compensation including benefits is significantly above the base hourly rate.
How do I apply for a TSA Officer position at DFW?+
All TSA Officer applications go through USAJobs.gov — search 'Transportation Security Officer' and filter for DFW or Dallas, TX. The TSA hiring process includes: online application and assessment, a Computer Based Test (CBT) at an authorized testing center, a structured interview, medical evaluation (vision, hearing, color vision), background investigation, and drug test. The TSA is a federal position — US citizenship is required. The DFW TSA hiring process typically takes 3–6 months from application to start date. TSA also uses expedited hiring events periodically — check TSA.gov/jobs for current events at DFW.
Are there non-TSA airport security jobs at DFW?+
Yes, several categories. Private aviation screeners — companies like Unifi (formerly Argenbright), Allied Universal, and G4S hold contracts for non-TSA screening at some DFW terminals and Love Field. Airline security officers — American Airlines (DFW hub) and Southwest Airlines (Love Field hub) employ direct security staff for corporate and airside operations. Ramp and airside security — security officers who patrol the airside ramp areas, fuel farms, and cargo facilities at DFW require an airport security credential (SIDA badge) but are employed by ground handlers and airport contractors rather than TSA. Airport concession and terminal security — contracted through Allied Universal and Securitas for landside and terminal areas.
What is the difference between DFW International and Love Field?+
DFW International Airport is one of the largest airports in the world — 5 terminals, 200+ gates, 75 million passengers annually, DFW Airport Board-governed, located in Irving/Grapevine. It is the hub for American Airlines and generates the vast majority of aviation security employment in the DFW metro. Dallas Love Field is a smaller municipal airport (Southwest Airlines hub, 16 million passengers annually) owned by the City of Dallas, located in northwest Dallas. Love Field has its own TSA checkpoint operation and contracted security presence but at significantly smaller scale than DFW International. For employment volume, DFW International is the primary target.
Do I need a security clearance for DFW airport security?+
A full federal security clearance is not required for most TSA Officer positions, but a thorough background investigation is — TSA checks the past 10 years of employment history, criminal record, financial history, and residential history. A SIDA (Secured Identification Display Area) badge is required for any airside access at DFW — this requires a separate background check administered by DFW Airport Authority. Officers who work airside positions (ramp security, cargo security, fuel farm security) need SIDA credentials. The background requirements are stringent but distinct from full federal security clearance programs like Secret or Top Secret.
Overview

DFW Airport Security: One of the World's Busiest Airports, Federal Pay, and Year-Round Hiring

Dallas/Fort Worth International Airport is the fourth-busiest airport in the world by operations and one of the largest employers in the DFW metro — approximately 28,000 employees on airport property across airlines, concessions, ground handling, and security. TSA at DFW operates one of the largest Transportation Security Officer workforces in the country, with continuous hiring given the scale of passenger throughput across 5 terminals and 200+ gates.

The federal employment benefits — FEHB health coverage, FERS retirement, TSP, paid leave, and locality pay — make TSA Officer positions at DFW among the most comprehensively compensated security positions in Texas. Starting Band D pay of $22–$25/hr with federal benefits packages that commercial security employers cannot match makes the 3–6 month hiring process worth pursuing for candidates who want long-term security employment stability.

The private aviation security market at DFW — screener contracts, airline security, ramp security, terminal security — provides additional airport employment for candidates who want the airport environment without the TSA hiring timeline. Unifi, Allied Universal, and Securitas all have active DFW airport security contracts.

Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Airport Security Officers Do at DFW

TSA Transportation Security Officers at DFW operate passenger and baggage screening lanes — operating walk-through magnetometers and advanced imaging technology (AIT), processing carry-on baggage through X-ray, conducting pat-downs when required, and managing the flow of thousands of passengers per hour through DFW's high-throughput checkpoints. The operational environment at DFW is among the highest-volume and most technically demanding TSA checkpoint operations in the US.

Private screeners and airline security officers work in more specialized airport environments — American Airlines security at corporate facilities and terminals, ground handler airside security for ramp access control, and cargo facility security for the significant cargo operations at DFW. The SIDA credentialing process and airside operational requirements distinguish airport security from standard contracted commercial security — officers who build DFW airport credentials develop a specialized employment profile that transfers to aviation security roles at airports nationwide.

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • US citizenship — required for all TSA Officer positions
  • Minimum age 18 for TSA; high school diploma or GED required
  • Vision, hearing, and color vision requirements — TSA medical evaluation required
  • 10-year background investigation — employment, criminal, financial, residential history
  • SIDA badge eligibility for airside positions — DFW Airport Authority background check
  • Texas Level 2 for contracted private screener and terminal security positions
  • Drug test — TSA and most airport security positions require pre-employment and random drug screening
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • TSA: 24/7 operation — all shifts, rotating schedule including early morning (4am start), afternoon, and overnight
  • DFW Airport operates 24 hours — security staffing covers all operational hours
  • Shift bid system for TSA — schedule preferences based on seniority
  • Private screener/contractor: follows checkpoint hours, typically 4am–midnight peak
  • Airline security: follows airline operational schedule
