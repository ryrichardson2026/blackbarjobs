What Does a TSA / Airport Security Officer Do?

TSA and airport security officers screen passengers, baggage, and cargo to ensure aviation safety. The Transportation Security Administration is a federal agency — TSA officers are federal employees with a separate hiring process, pay scale, and benefits package compared to private security contractors.

DFW International Airport and Dallas Love Field are two of the busiest airports in the country, generating consistent demand for both TSA officers and private airport security personnel. Private contractors handle perimeter security, access control, and non-screening functions alongside TSA.

Airport security roles require passing a federal background check, drug screening, and in the case of TSA, a medical evaluation and color vision test. Most positions offer full federal benefits including health insurance and retirement.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Screen passengers and carry-on baggage using X-ray and imaging equipment
  • Operate advanced imaging technology and walk-through metal detectors
  • Conduct pat-down searches and resolve security alarms
  • Control access to secured areas of the terminal and tarmac
  • Monitor surveillance systems and report suspicious activity
  • Enforce TSA regulations and airport security policies
  • Assist passengers with screening procedures professionally and efficiently
  • Respond to security incidents and coordinate with law enforcement

Requirements & Experience
Requirements

Training & Credential Requirements

US Citizenship required for TSA federal positions
Federal background investigation and security clearance
High school diploma or GED
Drug screening — TSA has strict drug-free workplace requirements
Medical evaluation including color vision test (TSA)
Texas Security License (Level II) for private airport security roles
Experience

Experience Requirements

  • TSA entry-level positions are open to candidates with no prior security experience — training is provided on the job
  • One year of security experience or one year of customer service experience qualifies for TSA Officer positions
  • Military or law enforcement background is a strong advantage and may qualify for higher pay bands
  • Private airport security roles may require a Texas guard card and 1–2 years of prior security experience
  • Customer service skills are heavily weighted — officers interact with hundreds of passengers daily
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Work Environments

  • Airport operations run 24/7 — all shifts available including early morning, evening, and overnight
  • TSA officers typically work rotating schedules covering early (4am–12pm), mid (12pm–8pm), and late shifts
  • Part-time and full-time positions both available through TSA and private contractors
  • High-volume indoor environment — busy terminals with continuous passenger flow
  • Extended standing at screening checkpoints for full shift duration
  • DFW and Love Field both operate year-round with consistent staffing demand
