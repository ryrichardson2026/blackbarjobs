Do I need a guard card for hospital security in Dallas? +
Yes. A valid Texas Level II Security Officer Commission is required for all paid hospital security work in Dallas. Most Dallas hospital systems also require CPR/First Aid certification as a condition of employment. De-escalation training is strongly preferred and required at some facilities.
How much do hospital security officers make in Dallas? +
Entry-level hospital security in Dallas runs $17–20/hr — above the unarmed commercial baseline. Experienced officers earn $20–26/hr. Senior and supervisory roles at major systems like Parkland and UT Southwestern reach $26–34/hr. Overnight and weekend differentials apply on top of base pay.
Is hospital security in Dallas a good career? +
Yes — particularly for candidates interested in long-term stability, advancement, and the possibility of transitioning into law enforcement or clinical careers. Dallas hospital systems are large organizations with formal career paths from security officer to supervisor to security manager. Officers at Parkland and UT Southwestern also work alongside one of the most complex patient populations in Texas, which builds skills applicable across security, healthcare, and law enforcement careers.
What is the hardest part of hospital security in Dallas? +
The emergency department. Dallas Level I trauma centers like Parkland operate some of the busiest EDs in Texas, and security officers assigned there regularly manage combative patients, individuals in psychiatric crisis, and high-tension family situations — often simultaneously. Officers who can handle ED environments are among the most valued in the Dallas healthcare security market.
Are armed hospital security jobs available in Dallas? +
Yes, at select facilities. Some Dallas hospital systems maintain armed security programs for specific posts — typically perimeter patrol and access control at high-security areas. These roles require a Level III Armed Commission and typically pay at the upper end of the hospital security pay range.

Role Overview

What Does a Hospital Security Officer Do in Dallas?

Hospital security officers in Dallas perform a broader range of duties than most commercial security roles. Access control, patient management, de-escalation, and emergency response are all core functions — and all of them occur in a clinical environment where the stakes of every interaction are elevated.

Emergency department security is the most demanding assignment within hospital settings. Dallas Level I trauma centers like Parkland operate some of the busiest emergency departments in Texas, and security officers assigned to the ED regularly manage combative patients, intoxicated individuals, and family members in crisis. These assignments require strong de-escalation skills and composure under pressure.

Hospital security in Dallas offers strong long-term stability. Healthcare is recession-resistant, facilities operate year-round with no seasonal slowdowns, and officers who build tenure at major systems like Parkland or UT Southwestern often advance into supervisory roles, security management, or lateral moves into law enforcement.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Control access to patient care areas, pharmacy, and restricted clinical zones
  • Respond to behavioral health crises, combative patients, and Code Gray situations
  • Conduct de-escalation with agitated patients, visitors, and family members
  • Patrol hospital grounds, parking structures, and campus perimeter
  • Escort staff, patients, and visitors to ensure safe movement within the facility
  • Assist clinical staff during medical emergencies requiring security support
  • Write detailed incident reports for all security-related events
  • Coordinate with Dallas PD and EMS on incidents requiring law enforcement response

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level II Security License required — most Dallas hospital systems also require CPR/First Aid certification
  • Prior security, law enforcement, military, or healthcare background strongly preferred — particularly for emergency department assignments
  • De-escalation training or certification a strong differentiator for Parkland Memorial and UT Southwestern postings
  • Level III Armed Commission required for armed hospital security posts — some Dallas facilities maintain armed security programs
  • Physical ability to respond quickly, assist with patient restraint, and work extended shifts in demanding environments required
  • Strong documentation skills — hospital incident reports are reviewed by clinical, legal, and regulatory teams
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • All three shifts available — hospitals operate 24/7/365 with no seasonal slowdown
  • 8 and 12-hour shifts both common — larger systems like Parkland and UT Southwestern use 12-hour rotations for most security positions
  • Overnight and weekend hospital shifts carry additional pay differentials on top of the healthcare security premium
  • Holiday coverage required at all Dallas hospital systems — holiday pay typically applies
  • Emergency department assignments are highest intensity and typically assigned after a probationary period at lower-acuity posts
  • Parking structure and campus perimeter patrol available for officers who prefer outdoor assignments
