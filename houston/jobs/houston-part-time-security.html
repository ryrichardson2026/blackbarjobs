Overview

Houston's Part-Time Security Market: Flex Scheduling Meets Consistent Demand

Houston's part-time security market is one of the most developed in Texas, driven by the city's size, the diversity of its security account types, and the explicit flex scheduling models of its major employers. Allied Universal — the city's largest security employer with 90-plus live openings at any given time — operates a platform (Claim a Shift) that lets licensed officers pick up shifts across its Houston portfolio without committing to a fixed schedule. S.A.F.E. Management runs NRG Park staffing entirely on an availability-based model — officers indicate which events they can work and get assigned accordingly. These platforms have fundamentally changed how part-time security employment works in Houston.

The event and festival segment is the most naturally part-time-friendly category. NRG Park events (Texans games, Astros games via S.A.F.E., the Livestock Show & Rodeo, concerts, Monster Jam) happen on specific dates — an officer who wants to work 10–15 events per month can do so without committing to anything else. 5 STAR Event Services runs a similar model across Freedom Over Texas, Shell Energy Stadium events, and the broader Houston sports and entertainment calendar. These firms aren't looking for full-time commitments; they're looking for reliable officers who show up when they say they will.

For officers who want more structured part-time work, Walden Security posts dedicated weekend-only positions, TriCorps Security offers part-time unarmed roles at $16/hr paid weekly, and Global Security Consulting Group posts aviation security part-time at IAH at $16/hr. The range from fully flex event staffing to structured 16-hour weekend posts gives Houston's part-time security market something for every schedule preference.

Role Overview

What Types of Part-Time Security Work Exist in Houston?

Part-time security in Houston falls into three models, each with a different scheduling structure. The flex/claim model — exemplified by Allied Universal's Claim a Shift and S.A.F.E. Management's availability system — gives officers maximum scheduling control. You indicate availability, the system offers shifts that match, you accept or decline. There's no fixed commitment beyond what you've accepted. This model suits officers who have primary employment, school schedules, or caregiving responsibilities that make a fixed weekly commitment impossible.

The fixed part-time model — weekend-only posts, set evening shifts, specific recurring assignments — provides more predictability. Walden Security's part-time weekend roles, TriCorps' second-shift part-time positions, and St. Moritz's evening armed posts all fit this model. The schedule is defined and consistent; the hours are just below full-time. Officers who want a reliable secondary income rather than maximum flexibility often prefer this approach.

Event-based part-time — working specific events through S.A.F.E. Management, 5 STAR, or directly with venue operators — is the most dynamic model. Hours vary dramatically by event calendar. A Texans playoff run can mean 4–5 events in 3 weeks; an off-season gap might mean nothing for a month. Officers who work event security supplement with other part-time or flex shifts to smooth out the calendar variation. The hourly rates are competitive and the work is engaging, but it requires active schedule management.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Provide access control, patrol, and security presence at assigned post per site-specific post orders
  • Claim and fulfill assigned shifts reliably — in flex models, shift reliability is the primary performance metric that determines future assignment priority
  • Conduct patrol of assigned area during shift — log observations, report irregularities, document activity per employer reporting requirements
  • Provide guest services and customer interaction at event and venue part-time posts — event security is high-contact public-facing work
  • Complete digital check-in and check-out using employer's mobile platform — most major Houston employers use digital shift management systems
  • Write shift reports and incident documentation for any events during the post — part-time officers have the same documentation obligations as full-time
  • Maintain required licensing currency — Level 2 renewal every 2 years is required regardless of part-time or full-time status
  • Communicate schedule changes promptly — reliability in part-time security determines assignment quality; no-shows directly impact future booking priority

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license — required for all paid security work regardless of part-time or full-time status
  • Reliable attendance — in flex and event models, reliability determines priority for future assignments; unreliability gets you removed from the rotation
  • Smartphone and basic app comfort — Allied Universal Claim a Shift and similar platforms require a functional smartphone for shift management
  • Shift flexibility within your availability window — part-time works best for officers who are genuinely flexible within their available periods
  • Valid Texas driver's license — preferred or required for most part-time patrol and multi-site assignments
  • Level 3 license for armed part-time — St. Moritz and other firms post armed part-time roles requiring Level 3; unarmed part-time requires Level 2 only
Schedules

  • Event-based calendar — S.A.F.E. Management and 5 STAR schedule around the event calendar; officers work what they commit to
  • 16–32 hours per week typical — most part-time Houston security roles fall in this range; some flex roles have no hour minimum
  • Path to full-time available — Allied Universal, Walden, TriCorps all explicitly offer full-time conversion for reliable part-time performers
