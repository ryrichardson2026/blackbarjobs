FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What types of school security jobs exist in Houston?+
Two main categories: district police and sworn campus officers (HISD PD, Cy-Fair ISD PD, Fort Bend ISD PD — require TCOLE law enforcement license), and contracted or private security officers at individual campuses (require Level 2 or Level 3 security license, not TCOLE). This page covers the contracted security officer category. Private schools (The Awty International School, St. John's School, Houston Baptist University) hire security officers directly and offer some of the better-compensated school security roles in the market.
What does Texas law require for school security?+
Texas House Bill 3 (effective 2023) requires public school districts to have an armed security officer on every campus. Most larger districts including HISD are working toward compliance — HISD has 200-plus sworn officers with middle and high school campuses covered, elementary campuses covered by patrol. This law created ongoing expansion of school security staffing across the Houston metro and has driven consistent hiring across districts.
What does school security pay in Houston?+
Private school security runs $20–22/hr at The Awty International School per current postings. Contracted security at public school campuses via firms like Star Protection Agency runs $16–20/hr. HISD sworn police officers (TCOLE required) start at $68,000/yr. Campus security monitors at Clear Creek ISD and Fort Bend ISD run lower than sworn officer roles. Private school and university campus security consistently pays above public district contracted rates.
Is bilingual ability required for school security?+
Not universally required but strongly preferred — especially in Houston ISD and Southwest Houston area schools where Spanish-speaking students and families are the majority. Multiple current Houston school security postings note bilingual Spanish as preferred or required. Officers who are bilingual have a significant competitive advantage in Houston school security hiring.
What makes school security different from other security work?+
The population. School security officers interact with minors daily — students, their parents, and the social dynamics of campus life. De-escalation skills calibrated for youth behavior, trauma-informed awareness, and the ability to build positive relationships with students are practical daily requirements. Officers who approach school security as relationship-building rather than pure enforcement build the strongest campus security programs.
Overview

Houston's School Security Market: HB3 Driving Consistent Expansion

Texas House Bill 3 created a structural expansion in school security hiring across the Houston metro that continues in 2026. The law requires an armed security officer at every public school campus — a mandate that HISD (215,000 students, 274 campuses) is still scaling to meet. HISD's police department has 200-plus sworn officers with coverage focused on middle and high schools, while patrol coverage serves elementary campuses as the department grows. Cypress-Fairbanks ISD and Fort Bend ISD are in similar positions. The result is consistent, ongoing hiring across district police departments and contracted security programs throughout Harris County and surrounding districts.

Private schools add a distinct segment. The Awty International School (Houston's largest international school, 1,700 students, 55 nationalities) posts armed security officer roles at $20–22/hr with traffic control and campus patrol. St. John's School (River Oaks, K–12 private school) hires campus security officers directly. Star Protection Agency contracts armed Level 3 officers for private high school campuses in the Houston area, with school or campus environment experience strongly preferred. Houston Baptist University, Texas Southern University, and University of Houston campuses all staff their own campus security and public safety programs.

The demographics of Houston's school security market are distinctive. HISD and most Houston-area public school districts serve majority-minority student populations with high shares of Spanish-speaking students and families. Officers who are bilingual in Spanish are significantly more effective in these environments and consistently preferred in hiring. The ability to de-escalate with students, communicate with parents in Spanish, and build trust within school communities is what separates good school security officers from effective ones.

Role Overview

What Does a School Security Officer Do in Houston?

School security in Houston covers three main functions depending on the campus type. At public school campuses, officers manage perimeter access during arrival and dismissal (the highest-risk periods), monitor campus entry and visitor management throughout the day, respond to behavioral incidents and altercations, coordinate with campus administration on discipline situations, and maintain a visible deterrent presence. The daily rhythm of a school security officer tracks the school day — arrival coverage, lunch monitoring, dismissal, after-school activities — rather than the continuous 24-hour cycle of other security environments.

At private schools, the function shifts toward campus access management and community safety for a smaller, higher-net-worth population. The Awty International School specifically requires traffic control expertise given the vehicle congestion of a 1,700-student multilingual campus. St. John's campus security manages access to a River Oaks property with 800 students and high community expectations for safety and discretion.

The common thread across all school security work: youth-appropriate de-escalation. Students in conflict, students in distress, and parents who are upset about campus situations all require a fundamentally different approach than adult confrontations in commercial or industrial environments. Officers who understand this and can navigate student behavioral dynamics without escalating situations are the most effective school security professionals in the Houston market.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Manage campus perimeter access during arrival and dismissal — the highest-risk periods for unauthorized entry and traffic incidents
  • Operate visitor management system — verify identity, check against restricted visitor lists, issue visitor passes, escort when required
  • Monitor campus cameras and alarm systems from security console — respond to access events and emergency alerts
  • Respond to student behavioral incidents and altercations — de-escalate with youth-appropriate techniques, coordinate with administration
  • Conduct periodic patrol of campus buildings, parking lots, and grounds during school hours and after-school activities
  • Enforce campus access policies — no unauthorized individuals, no prohibited items, contractor credentialing
  • Coordinate with campus administration, counselors, and HPD School Resource Officers on security incidents
  • Write accurate incident reports for all security events, behavioral situations, and unusual activity

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license — required for contracted school security; Level 3 required for armed private school posts
  • Bilingual Spanish — strongly preferred at HISD and Southwest Houston area campus positions; noted in active postings
  • Youth de-escalation skills — school security requires behavioral management with minors; training in this area is a significant differentiator
  • Clean background — school employment requires FBI background check and often additional screening beyond standard security background
  • Valid Texas driver's license — required for campus patrol at multi-building or multi-campus assignments
  • Prior school, campus, or youth-environment experience — noted as strongly preferred by Star Protection Agency and private school accounts
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • School-day hours dominant — campus security staffing peaks 7am–4pm on school days; afterschool activities extend to 6–7pm
  • Summer and school break coverage reduced but not zero — campuses need security during breaks for facility security and summer programs
  • Athletic event coverage — Friday night football, basketball games, and other events extend officer needs into evenings and weekends
  • August through June academic year is primary season — summer break creates reduced but ongoing security coverage need
  • Arrival and dismissal are highest-intensity periods — additional officer coverage often deployed during these windows
  • Private school schedules vary — college prep schools with extensive extracurricular programs need security beyond standard school-day hours
