FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

How big is the Houston security officer job market?+
It's one of the largest in Texas. There are 570-plus active security officer listings in the Houston metro at any given time, across employers ranging from Allied Universal (the largest, with 90-plus live openings) to regional firms and direct hospital and energy company hires. The market spans every account type — healthcare, industrial, corporate, retail, event, residential, and patrol — and every shift.
What do security officer jobs pay in Houston?+
The range is wide and account-driven. Unarmed Level 2 posts start at $15–17/hr. Hospital accounts at the Texas Medical Center run $17–20/hr with benefits. Armed Level 3 posts run $18–25/hr depending on account type. Specialized plant-gate and industrial posts with TWIC requirements reach $20–24/hr. Overnight differentials of $0.50–1.50 apply across most account types.
What license do I need to work security in Houston?+
A Texas Department of Public Safety Private Security Bureau license. Level 2 (Non-Commissioned) for unarmed posts — 6 hours of training, background check, typically 1–2 weeks to complete. Level 3 (Commissioned) for armed posts — requires Level 2 first, plus a 10-hour firearms proficiency course and range qualification. Some accounts require additional certifications: IAHSS Basic for hospital security, TWIC for Port and industrial accounts.
Which companies hire the most security officers in Houston?+
Allied Universal is the largest by volume. Securitas, GardaWorld, Sunstates Security (Forbes Dream Employer 2026), TriCorps, Prosegur, Inner Parish Security Corporation (IPSC), United Protective Services, DSI Security, Walden Security, and Platinum Group Security are all active. Regional firms like Twin City Security (veteran-owned since 1972), St. Moritz, and Valor Force fill specialized and higher-paying accounts. Hospitals hire directly — MD Anderson, Houston Methodist, Memorial Hermann, and Texas Children's all post security openings independently.
What parts of Houston have the most security jobs?+
The Texas Medical Center (healthcare, 24/7, all shifts) is the densest single employment cluster. The Energy Corridor (corporate campus, day-shift heavy), Galleria/Uptown (high-rise commercial and retail), Downtown/Midtown (government, hotels, high-rise), NRG Park area (event and sports), and the Beltway 8 / Port corridor (warehouse and industrial) round out the primary demand clusters. Security jobs exist citywide — Houston's 665-square-mile footprint means the market is genuinely spread across the metro.
Overview

Houston's Security Market: What Makes It Different From Every Other Texas City

Houston's security market is larger and more diverse than Dallas-Fort Worth, and the reasons are specific to what the city actually is. The Texas Medical Center — 60 institutions, 1,345 acres, 106,000-plus employees — generates a healthcare security employment base that no other Texas city can match. MD Anderson, Texas Children's, Houston Methodist (eight hospitals), Memorial Hermann, and Harris Health's Ben Taub and LBJ trauma centers all run major security departments. Healthcare security at the TMC pays above standard commercial rates, offers full benefits at the major systems, and requires officers who can handle high-stakes environments with clinical composure.

The Houston Ship Channel corridor — the largest petrochemical complex in the Western Hemisphere — generates industrial and plant-gate security demand unique in Texas. ExxonMobil Baytown, Marathon Galveston Bay, Pemex Deer Park, and the East Harris County Manufacturers Association's 120-plus member plants all staff security programs that require TWIC credentials and pay $2–6/hr above standard guard rates. And the Port of Houston — the nation's largest by waterborne tonnage — anchors a warehouse and bonded-facility security market that the Beltway 8 distribution corridor extends across northwest Harris County.

On the corporate and event side, the Energy Corridor's 26 million square feet of office space housing BP, Shell, ConocoPhillips, and hundreds of oilfield services firms generates day-shift corporate campus demand, while NRG Park's 350-acre complex, Toyota Center, Minute Maid Park, and the George R. Brown Convention Center make Houston one of the most active event security markets in the South. The result is a market where officers can choose their specialty — industrial, healthcare, corporate, event, or patrol — and find consistent work in it year-round.

Role Overview

What Do Security Officers Do in Houston? It Depends on the Account.

More than most cities, Houston security work is defined by the account type rather than a generic job description. An officer working overnight at Ben Taub Hospital's Level 1 trauma center is doing something fundamentally different from an officer on the day shift at a Shell Oil corporate lobby in the Energy Corridor — and both of those are different from a Level 3 armed officer at an ExxonMobil plant gate in Baytown or an event officer working crowd management at NRG Stadium during a Texans game. The city's size and industry mix create a security market with more distinct specialties than most.

What's consistent across Houston security: documentation, communication, and the ability to make independent judgment calls. Every account requires accurate shift logs and incident reports. Every environment — clinical, corporate, industrial, event — rewards officers who communicate clearly and de-escalate effectively. And Houston's geography means that many posts run with limited supervisory presence, particularly on overnight and weekend shifts, which puts more responsibility on the individual officer than smaller-market positions typically do.

New officers entering the Houston market typically start in unarmed Level 2 posts through a major staffing firm — Allied Universal, Sunstates, TriCorps, or Inner Parish — and build from there. Officers with military or law enforcement backgrounds and a Level 3 can target industrial, hospital, or high-pay manufacturing accounts from day one. The path is clear regardless of starting point; the market is large enough to support every level of experience.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Provide access control, visitor management, and badge issuance at corporate, healthcare, industrial, and residential facilities
  • Conduct scheduled patrol of assigned buildings, campuses, perimeter zones, and parking structures on foot or by vehicle
  • Monitor CCTV systems, alarm panels, and access control platforms — respond to activations and dispatch as required
  • Respond to disturbances, medical emergencies, behavioral incidents, and security breaches per post orders and use-of-force policy
  • Enforce TWIC and site-specific access requirements at industrial, Port, and bonded warehouse accounts
  • Staff event access control, crowd management, bag screening, and perimeter posts at major venues and conventions
  • Write accurate shift logs and incident reports — documentation standard is account-specific but universally required
  • Maintain required license currency — Level 2 annual renewal, Level 3 firearms qualification, account-specific certifications

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license — required for all unarmed posts; obtainable in 1–2 weeks
  • Valid Texas Level 3 Commissioned Security Officer license — required for all armed posts; PSB pocket card must be in hand
  • TWIC card for Port, industrial, and bonded warehouse accounts — apply through TSA at least 60 days in advance; $125.25 new, 5-year validity
  • IAHSS Basic certification for hospital accounts — required within 6 months at most TMC facilities; Aggression Management within 30 days
  • Valid Texas driver's license — required for patrol, mobile, industrial, and most overnight posts; clean MVR for armed accounts
  • Account-specific clearances — some Energy Corridor campus and Ship Channel plant accounts run site-specific background checks beyond standard guard licensing
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • All three shifts available across Houston — day, swing, and overnight — with overnight carrying the highest demand and consistent differentials
  • 12-hour rotating shifts standard at TMC hospitals and major plant accounts — 3–4 days per week, 24/7 coverage required
  • Day-shift dominant at Energy Corridor corporate campuses and Galleria high-rises — business-hours security programs
  • Event-based flex scheduling at NRG Park, Toyota Center, Minute Maid, and GRB Convention Center — build availability around the event calendar
  • Weekend and holiday coverage required at hospitals, industrial, distribution, and residential accounts — year-round staffing need
  • Overnight differentials of $0.50–1.50/hr standard; healthcare system employers add full benefits packages for officers working 30-plus hours
