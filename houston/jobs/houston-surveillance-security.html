FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What types of surveillance security jobs exist in Houston?+
Three main categories: on-site CCTV console officers (monitor camera systems at a single building or campus from a security console), control room operators (monitor multiple buildings or sites from a centralized dispatch/control room — common at hospital campuses, large corporate properties, and university campuses), and remote video surveillance operators (monitor camera feeds for multiple client sites from a monitoring center, sometimes from home). Pay and sophistication vary significantly across these three.
What does surveillance security pay in Houston?+
On-site CCTV console work embedded in a standard security officer role averages $16.14/hr. Dedicated remote video surveillance in Houston averages $22.41/hr. CCTV monitoring as a specialty role runs $22.50–28.94/hr. Control room operator positions at healthcare and corporate campuses run $16–22/hr with benefits at major employers. The specialization premium for dedicated surveillance roles is $4–8/hr above standard floor patrol.
Do surveillance roles require a security license in Texas?+
Yes for any paid role that involves security functions. The Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license is required for all paid surveillance and CCTV monitoring positions in Texas where the role carries security officer authority. Pure technical roles (camera installer, system technician) use different licensing pathways. If you're monitoring cameras as a security function, you need the Level 2.
Is surveillance security good for people who can't do physical patrol?+
Yes — this is one of the key practical advantages. Surveillance and console security involves extended seated monitoring at computer workstations rather than physical patrol. The physical requirements are attention to detail, visual concentration for extended periods, and the ability to respond quickly to what you observe. Officers with physical limitations that prevent extended patrol work often find strong career longevity in surveillance roles.
What technology skills do I need for surveillance security in Houston?+
Basic computer proficiency is the minimum. Most CCTV platforms (Milestone, Genetec, Avigilon, Hanwha) have intuitive interfaces and provide training. Control room roles may involve dispatching field officers, which requires radio communication comfort and multi-tasking under pressure. Remote video surveillance roles require comfort with software-based monitoring platforms and the discipline to maintain attention across multiple camera feeds. None of these requires IT expertise — they require attention to detail and reliable reporting.

Houston's Surveillance Security Market: Cameras Everywhere, Monitors Needed

Houston's surveillance security market has expanded significantly as camera technology has matured and the cost per camera has dropped. Where a building once needed a patrolling officer, many now use a combination of cameras and a remote monitoring service — creating demand for surveillance operators who may never leave a control room but monitor dozens of properties simultaneously. The Zedcor Security Solutions posting for Surveillance Systems Operators is a live example of this category in Houston, offering $16–17/hr for multi-site camera monitoring roles.

At the higher end, dedicated CCTV monitoring roles at healthcare campuses, university security operations centers, and corporate multi-site programs pay $19–26/hr. The TMC's major hospitals all run Security Operations Communications Centers (SOCCs) — control room environments where operators monitor camera systems across million-square-foot campuses, dispatch field officers, and coordinate with clinical staff on incident response. Patriot Group International posts SOCC operator roles in Houston at full-time non-exempt rates. MD Anderson and Houston Methodist run similar programs.

Remote video surveillance is the fastest-growing surveillance employment category. Providers that offer 24-hour remote monitoring as a service to retail, commercial, and industrial clients hire monitoring operators who watch live feeds for multiple client accounts simultaneously. Average pay for remote video surveillance in Houston is $22.41/hr — above the standard security officer average — reflecting the multi-site responsibility and the technical attention required.

Role Overview

What Does a Surveillance Security Officer Do in Houston?

Surveillance security in Houston varies by the scale and sophistication of the monitoring environment. At the basic level, a console officer in a commercial building watches 8–16 cameras covering the property's entry points, parking structure, and common areas. The work involves periodic review of live feeds, responding to motion alerts, and maintaining an access control log. The officer might also have patrol duties that alternate with console time depending on staffing levels.

At a healthcare SOCC or corporate multi-building monitoring center, the role expands dramatically. Operators monitor hundreds of cameras across multiple buildings simultaneously, use specialized video management software to respond to AI-flagged events, dispatch field officers to specific locations, coordinate with building management on access control events, and maintain detailed logs of all camera events and dispatches. This is active, high-attention work — the monitoring center equivalent of dispatch work in law enforcement.

Remote video surveillance — watching live feeds for multiple client properties from a monitoring center or home office setup — requires a different kind of sustained attention. Operators typically watch 20–50+ locations simultaneously, triaging between routine activity and genuine security events, and making the call to escalate (contact the client, dispatch police, alert on-site security) on a compressed timeline. The combination of multi-site responsibility and real-time decision-making at $22/hr+ makes this one of the better-compensated security categories for officers who thrive in technology-forward environments.

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license — required for all paid surveillance security positions
  • Computer literacy — CCTV platforms, video management software, access control systems are the primary work tools; basic computer proficiency is required
  • Extended visual attention — surveillance security requires sustained focus on camera feeds for 8–12 hour shifts; this is the primary physical demand
  • Clear communication — control room and dispatch roles require precise radio and verbal communication with field officers and management
  • Accurate documentation — surveillance roles generate more documentation per shift than patrol; written incident reporting is a daily core function
  • Multi-tasking under pressure — control room and remote monitoring roles require simultaneous attention to multiple sites and rapid event prioritization
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • All shifts available — surveillance and control room operations run 24/7; overnight and weekend control room staffing is consistently understaffed
  • Overnight is the highest-demand surveillance segment — camera monitoring at night is where most security events occur; overnight CCTV pays above base
  • 12-hour shifts common at healthcare and campus monitoring centers — reduces handoff complexity in high-event-volume environments
  • Remote video surveillance may offer non-traditional hours — some monitoring positions offer overnight and early morning shifts with home-office flexibility
  • Weekend and holiday coverage mandatory — surveillance systems don't turn off; control room staffing continues 365 days
  • Shift differentials for overnight and weekend surveillance — $1–3/hr above standard rate common at healthcare and multi-site monitoring positions
