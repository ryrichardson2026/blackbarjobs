FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Why do construction sites need security in Houston?+
Equipment theft and material theft are the primary drivers — a single piece of heavy equipment can represent $100k–$500k in assets, and copper, steel, and lumber theft from active sites is a consistent problem in Houston. Unauthorized entry and trespassing is the secondary concern — active construction sites are hazardous environments, and liability for injuries to unauthorized persons is significant. Fire watch is a third function: welding and hot work operations create fire hazards that require a dedicated fire watch officer.
What does construction site security pay in Houston?+
Average $18.17/hr, with most positions running $14.66–$17.88/hr. Posted rates: $18.50/hr (unarmed site supervisor-adjacent roles), $15/hr (standard entry-level site patrol). Overnight posts with outdoor exposure typically carry a slight premium over daytime rates. Construction security doesn't pay as high as hospital or industrial accounts, but it's accessible and consistently available across Houston's active build environment.
Is construction site security mostly overnight?+
Heavily weighted toward nights and weekends — that's when equipment is unattended, contractors aren't present, and theft risk is highest. Some sites staff day-shift access control during active construction hours to manage contractor credentialing and unauthorized visitor entry. The overnight and weekend segments are the highest-demand and most consistently understaffed.
What do I need for construction site security beyond a Level 2 license?+
A valid Texas driver's license with a clean record — most construction site patrol requires vehicle access. Physical tolerance for outdoor work in Houston weather — summer heat index above 100°F is genuine on outdoor construction sites. Some accounts require a fire watch certification (basic training in fire watch responsibilities for hot work operations). TWIC is required for Port-adjacent and certain industrial construction accounts.
What's the difference between fire watch and standard site security?+
Fire watch is a specific function required by OSHA and Texas regulations during and after welding, cutting, and hot work operations on construction sites. A fire watch officer monitors the immediate area for ignition during the work and for at least 30 minutes after. It's a dedicated role — the officer may not leave the area or perform other security functions during fire watch. Some employers staff fire watch as a separate position; others combine it with general site security.

Copper wire, HVAC components, and structural steel are portable and valuable. An overnight security officer provides both deterrence and response — the presence itself prevents the majority of theft attempts, and the documentation trail matters for insurance claims when incidents do occur.

Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Does a Construction Site Security Officer Do in Houston?

Construction site security is outdoor, patrol-dominant work. The primary function during overnight shifts is equipment and materials protection — walking or driving the site perimeter and interior, verifying that equipment is secured, checking for signs of entry, and maintaining a documented patrol log that proves coverage. Officers check gates, fencing, equipment keys, and material staging areas on a defined schedule and document everything. The documentation is as important as the patrol itself — it's what an insurance claim or police report is built on.

During active hours (when contractors are on site), the function shifts toward access control — verifying that everyone on the site is authorized, logging contractor arrivals and departures, managing vendor deliveries, and ensuring that non-authorized personnel can't wander onto an active construction site where liability for injury is significant. This is less patrol-heavy but requires more human interaction and judgment about who belongs on the site.

Fire watch is a distinct responsibility that comes up on sites with active welding and hot work. During fire watch, the officer monitors the welding zone for ignition, keeps a fire extinguisher ready, and maintains watch for 30+ minutes after the work concludes. It's a focused, stationary responsibility that requires attention even when nothing appears to be happening — because the fire that starts slowly in a pile of debris 20 minutes after welding ends is exactly what fire watch is designed to catch.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Conduct overnight patrol of construction site perimeter and interior on foot or vehicle — check gates, fencing, equipment, and materials staging areas
  • Document every patrol check-in with timestamped log — evidence of coverage is the primary deliverable for site security insurance purposes
  • Control access during active construction hours — verify contractor credentials, log arrivals and departures, manage vendor deliveries
  • Perform fire watch during and after welding and hot work operations — monitor immediate area for ignition per OSHA requirements
  • Respond to trespassing, vandalism, and theft attempts — assess, secure, contact HPD, and document per site security protocol
  • Inspect equipment and vehicle staging areas during rounds — verify keys are removed, equipment is secured, no obvious tampering
  • Monitor perimeter fencing and access gates — identify breaches, flag maintenance needs, secure compromised points
  • Write accurate shift reports documenting patrol times, observations, incidents, and any access control events

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license — required for all paid construction site security positions
  • Valid Texas driver's license with clean record — construction sites are large; vehicle patrol is standard at most accounts
  • Physical tolerance for outdoor work in Houston weather — summer heat index above 100°F is genuine on outdoor construction sites; winter rain and mud are real too
  • Fire watch awareness — basic fire watch training is preferred or required at sites with active welding and hot work operations
  • Self-sufficiency — overnight construction security runs with minimal supervision; officers must manage their patrol independently
  • TWIC card for Port-adjacent and industrial construction accounts — required for certain Ship Channel and waterfront project sites
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Overnight dominant — equipment theft concentrates overnight; most Houston construction security shifts run 10pm–6am or 11pm–7am
  • Weekend coverage essential — active construction sites are unattended on weekends; Saturday and Sunday overnight are the highest-risk periods
  • Day shift access control during active build phases — contractor management and visitor control during working hours
  • Fire watch timing varies — scheduled around hot work operations, which may occur during day or evening hours depending on the project
  • Seasonal outdoor exposure — Houston summer heat index is extreme; officers assigned outdoor patrol need heat management protocols
  • Project-based duration — some construction security contracts run for the duration of a build (months to years); others are shorter-term accounts
