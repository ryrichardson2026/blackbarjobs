FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Texas Level 2 security license?+
A Non-Commissioned Security Officer license issued by the Texas Department of Public Safety Private Security Bureau (PSB). It's the base credential for all unarmed security work in Texas — often called a 'guard card.' The license authorizes you to work as an unarmed security officer at any qualifying post. You need it before starting any paid security work.
How do I get a Level 2 security license in Texas?+
Four steps: (1) Complete the Level 2 training course — 4–6 hours, available online through DPS-approved providers, covers Texas security law, report writing, use of force, emergency response; (2) Pass the 50-question final exam with 75% or better; (3) Apply through the Texas Online Private Security (TOPS) portal with your certificate, pay the $38–40 application fee; (4) Submit fingerprints through IdentoGO (~$30). Total cost: $35–120 depending on provider. Processing takes 2–4 weeks, but you can work while your application is pending.
Can I start working before my Level 2 license arrives?+
Yes — this is a significant advantage of Texas law. Once you've submitted your TOPS application with your employer's sponsorship, you can work as a non-commissioned security officer while waiting for your pocket card to arrive. Most employers will hire you with a training certificate in hand and help you through the application process. You typically have 90 days from your start date to receive your license.
What jobs can I get with a Level 2 license in Houston?+
Every unarmed security position in the Houston market: hospital and TMC healthcare security, Galleria and retail LP, HOA gated community gatehouse, high-rise apartment lobby, office building lobby and console, event and festival security (NRG Park, S.A.F.E. Management, 5 STAR), hotel security, warehouse and distribution, construction site patrol, and corporate campus posts. The Level 2 is the universal entry credential — the broadest possible range of Houston security employment opens with it.
What does a Level 2 security job pay in Houston?+
The range is $14–22/hr depending on account type. Entry-level residential and retail runs $14–16/hr. Hospital and TMC accounts run $17–20/hr. Galleria and corporate lobby work runs $16–19/hr. Event security at major venues runs $16–21/hr. The Level 2 license itself is the floor; account type and experience determine where in the range you land.
Overview

The Level 2 License: Houston's Entry Point to 570+ Security Jobs

The Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license is the credential that opens the Houston security market. With 570-plus active security officer listings in the metro at any given time, and the overwhelming majority requiring a valid Level 2, it's the single most valuable piece of paper for anyone starting a security career in Houston. The license is also one of the most accessible professional credentials in Texas — 4–6 hours of online training, a 50-question exam, a background check, and $35–120 in total costs puts the license within reach of virtually anyone who qualifies.

Houston's size and industry diversity mean that a Level 2 license qualifies you for work across radically different environments. The TMC hospital system runs security operations at MD Anderson, Texas Children's, Houston Methodist, and Memorial Hermann that require Level 2 officers around the clock. NRG Park and the Houston sports venues need Level 2 officers for every Texans game, Astros game, Rockets game, and Livestock Show & Rodeo. The Energy Corridor's 26 million square feet of corporate office space needs Level 2 lobby officers on day shift. HOA communities in Katy, Sugar Land, and The Woodlands need Level 2 gatehouse officers 24/7. No other single credential opens as many doors in Houston's employment market as the Level 2.

For candidates who already have experience in customer service, hospitality, military, law enforcement, or related fields, the Level 2 is effectively an accelerator rather than a starting point. Prior experience in any of those fields translates directly to security competency — the license simply provides the legal authorization to be paid for it. Many employers in Houston's premium security accounts (TMC hospitals, Energy Corridor campuses, luxury hotel properties) actively prefer candidates who bring a Level 2 license plus relevant transferable experience.

Role Overview

What Can You Do With a Level 2 License in Houston?

A Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license in Texas authorizes you to work as an unarmed security officer at any qualifying post. The practical range is broad: access control at hospital lobbies and emergency department entrances, event screening at NRG Stadium, floor patrol at the Galleria, gatehouse operation at HOA communities, lobby management at Energy Corridor high-rises, overnight patrol at Beltway 8 warehouses, convention floor coverage at the GRB, hotel corridor patrol at the Hilton Americas, and festival entry management at Freedom Over Texas. In every case, the license is what makes the employment legal.

What Level 2 doesn't authorize: carrying a firearm on duty. Any post that requires an officer to be armed requires a Level 3 Commissioned Security Officer license — a separate, additional credential that requires Level 2 as a prerequisite plus 45 hours of armed training and firearms qualification. Officers who hold Level 2 and want to pursue armed security have a clear path: build experience first, then add Level 3.

For candidates entering security for the first time, the decision point after getting Level 2 is which account type to target. Hospital and healthcare security offers the highest entry pay with the most structured environment and benefits. Event security offers the most flexibility. Residential and retail security offers the most geographic spread across the metro. Office building security offers the most consistent day-shift hours. The Level 2 licenses you for all of them — the choice is yours.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Access control at lobby, gate, and entry point posts — verify identity, issue credentials, log visitors, manage contractor access
  • Conduct scheduled patrol of assigned areas on foot or vehicle — document observations, identify security concerns, report irregularities
  • Monitor CCTV systems and access control platforms — respond to camera events, alarms, and access violations
  • Respond to disturbances, medical emergencies, and security incidents — de-escalate, document, contact HPD or EMS when warranted
  • Screen guests at event and festival entry points — bag checks, ticket verification, magnetometer operation per venue protocol
  • Provide customer service to residents, patients, tenants, and guests — professional interaction is embedded in every Level 2 post type
  • Write accurate daily activity logs and incident reports — required documentation standard across all account types
  • Maintain license currency — renew every 2 years through TOPS; continuing education requirements apply at some account types

Requirements

What You Need

  • Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license — obtainable in 1–2 weeks through DPS-approved online training; $35–120 total cost
  • Minimum age 18 — Texas DPS requirement for all security officer licensing; most employer-specific posts also require 18+
  • Clean background — DPS runs a fingerprint-based background check; felony or Class A misdemeanor convictions are disqualifying
  • High school diploma or GED — standard employer requirement across the market; required by DPS for licensing
  • Valid Texas driver's license — required for patrol, mobile, and many fixed-post accounts even where driving isn't the primary function
  • US citizenship or legal work authorization — required for DPS licensing and fingerprint submission
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • All shifts available — Level 2 qualifies you for day, swing, and overnight posts across every account type in Houston
  • Healthcare and overnight demand is highest — TMC hospital accounts and residential overnight posts are the most consistently understaffed
  • Event and festival scheduling is calendar-based — NRG Park, 5 STAR, and S.A.F.E. Management availability model; work when you're available
  • Office and corporate accounts are day-shift dominant — Energy Corridor and Galleria corridor posts follow business hours
  • Weekend availability valued across all account types — retail, residential, and event accounts all need weekend coverage
  • Part-time and full-time options both widely available — most major Houston security employers offer both
