Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a security license for loss prevention in Houston?+
For uniformed LP roles, yes — a valid Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license is required. For undercover plain-clothes floor-walking positions (like Hobby Lobby's LP agent role), most employers don't require a guard card but strongly prefer candidates with prior LP experience. Check the specific posting; uniformed and undercover are different hiring tracks.
What does loss prevention pay in Houston?+
Current average is $21.27/hr, with most LP professionals earning $17.88–$22.93/hr. Full-time LP specialist and investigator roles at major retailers run $49k–$68k/yr. Wireless retail LP at Mobilelink and similar carriers tends to run mid-range. Director-level LP at Houston-based Landry's and regional multi-unit operators reaches $110k–$120k.
What retailers hire LP officers in Houston?+
Macy's, Walmart, Costco, Best Buy, Saks Global, Hobby Lobby, Abercrombie & Fitch, Ashley Furniture, and WSS are active right now. Wireless and telecom retail — Mobilelink, Portables AT&T — is a growing LP niche in Houston given device theft and fraud. Landry's Corporate, headquartered in Houston, hires LP for its restaurant and hospitality properties. Galleria-district luxury retailers (Neiman Marcus, Nordstrom, Saks) staff LP through both in-house programs and contracted security firms.
What is the difference between uniformed and undercover LP?+
Uniformed LP is a visible deterrent — officers in security attire patrol the sales floor, monitor CCTV, respond to incidents, and represent a visible security presence. Undercover plain-clothes LP (floor-walking) involves blending in as a shopper to identify and observe shoplifters before intervention. Hobby Lobby's program is specifically undercover. Both tracks require strong observational skills and solid documentation, but undercover requires more investigative instinct and comfort with ambiguity.
Is LP work mostly day shift?+
Yes — loss prevention tracks retail hours, which means predominantly day and swing shifts aligned with store traffic peaks. Evening and closing shifts are common; overnight LP is rare outside hotel and hospitality accounts. It's one of the more schedule-friendly security categories for people who want out of graveyard.
Overview

Houston's Loss Prevention Market: The Galleria and Beyond

The Galleria is the defining loss prevention account in Houston. At 2.4 million square feet, 400 stores, and anchored by Neiman Marcus, Nordstrom, Saks Fifth Avenue, and Macy's, it's the largest mall in Texas and the third largest in the United States. It draws international shoppers alongside locals, and the mix of high-value luxury merchandise and high foot traffic creates exactly the shrinkage exposure that drives serious LP investment. A 2025 crime uptick in the Galleria area prompted a multi-million dollar security commitment from Simon Property Group, and LP staffing is a central part of that response.

Beyond the Galleria, Houston's retail LP market spans several distinct segments. Big-box accounts — Walmart, Costco, Best Buy — hire LP at scale across the metro. Specialty and luxury retailers in River Oaks District, Memorial City, and Town & Country Village staff LP programs calibrated to high-ticket merchandise. And wireless and telecom retail has emerged as a distinct Houston LP niche: device theft and fraudulent return schemes at Mobilelink, AT&T Portables, and similar carriers have driven LP investment into a category that didn't exist as a standalone employment track a decade ago.

Houston-headquartered Landry's — the restaurant and hospitality company operating over 60 brands including Landry's Seafood, Golden Nugget, and Aquarium restaurants — hires LP for its citywide footprint. The hospitality LP role blends traditional asset protection with guest experience management in ways that pure retail LP doesn't. For officers who want variety in their LP career, Houston's industry mix provides it.

Role Overview

What Does a Loss Prevention Officer Do in Houston?

Loss prevention in Houston splits cleanly between two tracks, and understanding which one you're applying for changes everything about the role. Uniformed LP is visible deterrence: patrolling the sales floor in security attire, monitoring CCTV feeds, responding to alarm activations and shoplifting incidents, and conducting apprehensions when policy allows. The uniform itself is the primary tool — research consistently shows that visible LP presence reduces shoplifting more than any technology investment. Uniformed LP officers in Houston work at major retailers, Galleria-district stores, and big-box accounts where the volume of traffic makes passive deterrence essential.

Undercover plain-clothes LP — floor-walking — is a different discipline. Officers dress as shoppers, blend into the sales floor, and observe suspect behavior before coordinating an apprehension with uniformed staff or law enforcement. Hobby Lobby's Houston LP program is explicitly undercover, with no risk management, safety, or audit responsibilities — pure shoplifter identification and apprehension. It requires stronger investigative instincts, comfort working alone without visible backup, and the ability to maintain composure through observation periods that can last an entire shift without an incident.

Both tracks share documentation requirements, employee theft awareness, and loss trend analysis that distinguish LP from general security work. Officers who develop both skills — uniformed presence and investigative instinct — build the strongest LP careers in Houston's retail market.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Patrol sales floor and retail areas in uniform to deter shoplifting and provide visible security presence
  • Monitor CCTV systems and EAS (Electronic Article Surveillance) to identify suspicious activity and track subjects of interest
  • Conduct apprehensions of shoplifters per company policy and Texas law — document all incidents accurately
  • Investigate internal theft, fraudulent returns, and cash handling discrepancies in coordination with store management
  • Conduct undercover plain-clothes surveillance on the sales floor where authorized — observe and document before intervention
  • Perform inventory audits and cycle counts to identify shrinkage patterns and high-theft merchandise categories
  • Write detailed incident reports and maintain case files for law enforcement referrals and civil demand letters
  • Train store associates on theft awareness, shoplifter recognition, and proper reporting procedures

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Security License — required for all uniformed LP roles; undercover positions may not require a guard card but prefer prior LP experience
  • Prior retail, LP, or loss prevention experience — strongly preferred at Galleria-district luxury accounts and major big-box retailers; required for investigator-track roles
  • CCTV and EAS system familiarity — comfort with camera monitoring platforms and electronic article surveillance is standard across most LP postings
  • Clean background — retailers run stricter screening than standard commercial security accounts; cash handling and theft-related history are disqualifying
  • Observational and investigative instincts — especially for undercover and plain-clothes positions where documentation must be airtight before any apprehension
  • Professional communication — LP officers interact with customers, store management, and law enforcement; composure under confrontational situations is essential
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Day and swing shifts dominant — LP tracks retail operating hours; most Houston LP posts run 8am–6pm or 12pm–9pm
  • Evening and closing shifts common — peak shoplifting risk concentrates in late afternoon and evening hours; LP staffing reflects that
  • Weekend coverage required — Saturday and Sunday are highest-traffic retail days; LP availability on weekends is a standard expectation
  • Overnight LP is rare in retail — primarily limited to hotel and hospitality LP at properties like Hilton Americas and JW Marriott; most retail LP is day/swing only
  • Hobby Lobby schedules include two 3-day weekends per month, with Sundays, Thanksgiving, and Christmas always off — a scheduling differentiator vs most LP roles
  • Wireless and telecom LP may require split-week scheduling across multiple store locations in the same market
