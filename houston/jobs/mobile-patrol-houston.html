FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is mobile patrol security and how is it different from a fixed post?+
Mobile patrol officers cover multiple sites or properties on a defined route using a company vehicle — rather than being stationed at one location for the entire shift. A fixed-post officer owns one building or post; a mobile patrol officer may check 10, 20, or 30 properties per shift, spending 15–30 minutes at each. The trade-off is more variety and vehicle time versus the stability and ownership of a single-site post.
Do I need my own vehicle for mobile patrol in Houston?+
Usually not. Most Houston mobile patrol accounts provide a company vehicle while on duty — Allied Universal, GardaWorld, and most regional firms include a patrol vehicle as part of the post. You need a valid Texas driver's license and a clean driving record to qualify. Some smaller firms or HOA patrol contracts may require a personal vehicle; check the posting.
What does mobile patrol pay in Houston?+
Standard unarmed patrol runs $16–17.45/hr. Armed mobile patrol runs $18–22/hr. Vehicle patrol officer average is $16.97/hr. HOA patrol accounts in Katy, Sugar Land, and The Woodlands tend to be on the lower end of unarmed rates; Ship Channel and industrial route accounts run higher and may require TWIC.
What types of accounts do mobile patrol officers cover in Houston?+
HOA communities and gated neighborhoods across Katy, Sugar Land, The Woodlands, and Pearland — Houston's suburban sprawl makes residential patrol a large segment. Industrial parks and logistics facilities along the Ship Channel and Beltway 8. Commercial property portfolios covering strip centers, office parks, and parking lots. Construction sites needing overnight perimeter coverage. Utilities facilities run by Allied Universal's utility sector team.
Is mobile patrol mostly overnight?+
It's heavily weighted toward nights and weekends — that's when properties are most vulnerable — but not exclusively. HOA accounts often want day and swing coverage for community safety and parking enforcement. Industrial and construction routes are overnight-dominant. The schedule flexibility of patrol work, where you can sometimes claim shifts through apps like Allied Universal's platform, makes it one of the more adaptable security categories for scheduling.
Overview

Mobile Patrol in Houston: Why the City's Size Creates a Distinct Market

Houston's 665 square miles — the fourth-largest land area of any US city — creates a mobile patrol market that's simply larger and more geographically dispersed than most metros. The suburban HOA segment alone is enormous: master-planned communities in Katy, Sugar Land, The Woodlands, Pearland, and Friendswood run patrol contracts that cover hundreds of homes per route. These communities need access control, parking enforcement, amenity monitoring, and disturbance response — and a vehicle patrol is the only cost-effective way to cover the ground. GuardTexas (in business since 1976), Cascadia Global Security, and regional HOA-focused firms dominate this segment.

The industrial and logistics corridor adds a different layer. The Ship Channel and Beltway 8 distribution belt contain hundreds of industrial parks, warehouse campuses, and logistics facilities that can't justify a full-time fixed-post officer but need consistent overnight patrol coverage. Mobile officers cover these properties on defined routes, checking gates, perimeter fencing, and equipment staging areas on a schedule that documents every check-in. TWIC-required accounts on the Port corridor pay above standard patrol rates.

Commercial property portfolios — strip centers, office parks, and parking structures across the metro — round out the patrol market. Property management firms contract patrol coverage for their full portfolio, which means a single officer might cover 10 commercial sites on a single route. Allied Universal runs large commercial and utilities patrol programs in Houston, providing company vehicles and consistent scheduling for officers who want route-based work without the commitment of a fixed industrial post.

Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Does a Mobile Patrol Officer Do in Houston?

Mobile patrol officers in Houston spend most of their shift in a company vehicle, executing a defined route across multiple properties. The core loop is check-in, observe, document, and move — arrive at a property, conduct a visual inspection and any required foot check of the perimeter or parking area, log the time and any observations in a mobile reporting app, and proceed to the next stop. At HOA accounts, this includes parking enforcement, amenity area checks, and responding to resident complaints. At industrial accounts, it includes gate checks, equipment verification, and perimeter sweeps.

What separates a strong patrol officer from an average one is documentation and situational awareness. Every property check creates a timestamped log that the client reviews. Officers who miss checks, document vaguely, or respond slowly to incidents build a record that costs them the route. Officers who are consistent, thorough, and communicate clearly with dispatch and clients build long-term route assignments and move into account supervisor roles.

Response capability matters on patrol routes in ways it doesn't on fixed posts. When something happens at a property — a car break-in, a trespasser, a suspicious vehicle — the patrol officer is the first responder, often working alone with HPD response times measured in minutes. Comfort working independently, making judgment calls under pressure, and knowing when to call for backup rather than engaging are the skills that define patrol work in Houston.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Execute defined patrol route covering multiple properties per shift — log each check-in with timestamped mobile reporting app
  • Conduct exterior perimeter checks of industrial facilities, commercial properties, and residential communities — verify gates, fencing, and access points
  • Enforce HOA parking regulations, monitor community amenities, and respond to resident complaints and disturbance calls
  • Check construction site equipment staging areas and perimeter during overnight patrol — document condition and any sign of entry
  • Respond to alarms and incidents at any property on the route — assess, secure, and contact HPD and dispatch as warranted
  • Verify TWIC and site-specific access credentials at industrial and Port-adjacent facilities where required
  • Maintain patrol vehicle in clean, serviceable condition — complete pre-shift vehicle inspection log
  • Write accurate daily activity reports and incident documentation for each property on the route

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Security License — required for all paid patrol posts; Level 3 for armed patrol routes
  • Valid Texas driver's license with clean driving record — non-negotiable; most accounts require 21+ and 3 years of clean MVR history
  • Reliable transportation to start location — company vehicle is provided on duty but you need to get to the dispatch point
  • Mobile reporting app comfort — patrol documentation is digital at most major firms; smartphone proficiency is a practical requirement
  • Self-sufficiency and sound judgment — patrol officers work independently with minimal supervisory presence; they make the first-response call on every incident
  • TWIC card for Ship Channel and Port-adjacent industrial routes — applies to specific accounts; plan 60+ days if needed
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Overnight routes (10pm–6am or 11pm–7am) are the highest-demand segment — most industrial and commercial patrol is graveyard
  • Evening patrol (6pm–2am) is the second-largest segment — HOA accounts and commercial properties need coverage through closing hours
  • Day patrol exists at HOA communities and commercial portfolios that want visible daytime coverage for parking and disturbance response
  • Weekend patrol is consistently staffed — properties are at highest risk when building occupancy drops; weekend availability is valued
  • Flex shift claiming available through Allied Universal's platform — officers can claim shifts beyond their base schedule
  • Route assignments can vary by shift — new officers may cover multiple different routes before settling into a consistent assignment
