FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need experience to get a security job in Houston?+
No, not for unarmed positions. Twin City Security (veteran-owned since 1972, 505 N Sam Houston Pkwy) posts uniforms provided, paid training, paid guard license, and paid physical, with zero prior experience required. Securitas states explicitly: 'No experience necessary. If you have retail, food service or hospitality background you are a great fit; if not, we will provide you with the training.' ACT Security Group (offices in Austin, Houston, San Antonio) says: 'No experience is necessary; we will train you.' Reliability and a clean background are what Houston entry-level employers actually screen for.
Do I need a license before I can start?+
You need to be getting one, but you can start working before it arrives. Most Houston employers sponsor your TOPS application after hiring and let you work while your Level 2 processes (typically 2–4 weeks). Some employers pay for the training course as part of onboarding. Others require the certificate before your first shift, so always ask when you apply. Either way, the Level 2 course is 4–6 hours online and costs $47–100. You can have the certificate in hand the same day you take it.
What's the fastest path to getting hired in Houston with no experience?+
Three steps: (1) Complete the Level 2 online training course from any DPS-approved provider, $47–100, 4–6 hours, done in one sitting; (2) Apply same-day to Twin City Security (walk-in interviews Monday–Friday 9am–3pm), ACT Security Group, Securitas, TriCorps Security, and Allied Universal, all of which actively hire no-experience candidates in Houston; (3) Tell them you are available overnight and weekends. Officers with open overnight availability get hired fastest. Total time from zero to first shift: under two weeks if you move immediately.
What does no-experience security pay in Houston?+
Entry unarmed posts run $14–17/hr. No-experience unarmed security averages $16.76/hr in Houston per ZipRecruiter, with most earning $14.90–$17.69/hr. Standard all-experience security averages $18.17/hr, so the gap between zero experience and one year of experience is about $1–2/hr, not the dramatic difference candidates often assume. Accounts that accept zero experience include residential HOA gatehouse, event security (S.A.F.E. Management, NRG Park), warehouse patrol, and retail LP at big-box locations.
What backgrounds transfer best into security with no experience?+
Retail, food service, and hospitality transfer strongly, and Securitas specifically recruits from these fields because customer interaction is the primary daily skill. Military service is a major advantage and typically bumps candidates past entry-level pay immediately. Customer service backgrounds of any kind matter because most Houston security posts involve direct public interaction. Corrections, emergency services, and transportation backgrounds all transfer. What matters most: you show up reliably, you communicate professionally, and you can pass a background check.
Overview

Houston's No-Experience, Entry-Level Security Market: Train You and Put You to Work

Houston is one of the strongest no-experience security markets in Texas. The city's 665-square-mile footprint, the density of commercial and industrial accounts, and the consistent understaffing of overnight and weekend posts create a market where employers actively compete for anyone reliable enough to show up. Twin City Security, one of Houston's most established firms (disabled veteran-owned since 1972), posts uniforms provided, paid training, paid guard license, and paid physical as standard hiring terms, because that is what it takes to attract candidates in this market.

Securitas, which ranks among the top Houston security employers by headcount, explicitly recruits from retail, food service, and hospitality backgrounds. Allied Universal posts “no experience required” on a consistent share of its 90-plus live Houston openings. ACT Security Group's Houston office specifically states that training is provided. The pattern across major employers is the same: background check and availability matter more than a resume.

The practical entry path in Houston is straightforward. Complete the Level 2 online training course ($47–100, 4–6 hours), apply to the firms above, and indicate overnight and weekend availability. Candidates who do all three can realistically be working within 10 days of starting the process. The only real barrier is the Level 2 license, and most employers will help you through it if you are otherwise qualified.

Role Overview

What Do No-Experience Security Officers Do in Houston?

Entry-level no-experience security in Houston starts at one of three post types. Access control, at a gate or lobby, is the most common first assignment. You verify who enters, log visitors, and hold the line on one defined perimeter. The environment is structured, the expectations are clear in the post orders, and a supervisor or senior officer is reachable. Most new officers start here and are moved to more complex posts within 60–90 days once their reliability is established.

Event security through S.A.F.E. Management (NRG Park) and 5 STAR Event Services is the most schedule-flexible entry point. You work the events you commit to: Texans games, Astros games, concerts, and festivals. The work is high-energy and public-facing. S.A.F.E. Management specifically notes that a Level 2 license (or ability to obtain one) is the entry requirement, not prior security experience. Many officers who start in event staffing add fixed-post work as they build experience.

Residential HOA gatehouse is the most stable no-experience entry. HOA communities in Katy, Sugar Land, and The Woodlands consistently hire Level 2 officers with no prior security experience. The post is defined, the incidents are manageable, and the schedule is consistent. It is the kind of posting where showing up reliably and representing the community professionally is the entire job, and most no-experience officers who do that well have their pick of posts within a year.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Control access at assigned gate, lobby, or entry post: verify identity, issue visitor passes, log entries and exits per post orders
  • Conduct scheduled patrol of assigned area on foot: document observations, identify anything unusual, report per employer protocol
  • Provide customer service to residents, tenants, patients, and guests: professional interaction is the primary daily skill at most entry posts
  • Monitor CCTV cameras and alarm systems at console-equipped posts: report events per post orders
  • Staff event entry points: ticket and credential verification, bag screening, crowd direction at NRG Park and major venues
  • Write daily activity log and incident reports: documentation is the skill that separates good officers from great ones
  • Contact dispatch, HPD, or EMS when situations exceed post authority: knowing when to call is as important as what to do
  • Complete employer onboarding training and any site-specific certification: most Houston firms provide all training at no cost

Requirements

What You Need

  • Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license: 4–6 hours online, $47–100 total; most employers help you through it or pay for it outright
  • Minimum age 18: required by Texas DPS for all security officer licensing
  • Clean background: no felony, no Class A misdemeanor; the background check is part of DPS licensing and employer onboarding
  • High school diploma or GED: standard employer requirement; most firms verify at application
  • Reliable transportation: Houston's security posts are distributed across 665 square miles and overnight transit is limited
  • Overnight and weekend openness: not required, but it dramatically increases how fast you get hired and what posts you can access
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • All shifts available for no-experience candidates: day, swing, and overnight posts are all accessible without prior experience
  • Overnight is the highest-demand entry segment: residential, warehouse, and industrial overnight posts are the most consistently understaffed in Houston
  • Event scheduling via S.A.F.E. and 5 STAR: work the events you commit to, with no fixed weekly schedule required
  • Weekend availability highly valued: retail, residential, and event accounts all need Saturday and Sunday coverage
  • Part-time and full-time both available: most major Houston firms offer both from day one
  • 8 or 12-hour shifts depending on account: most entry posts run 8-hour shifts, while hospital and campus accounts may run 12
