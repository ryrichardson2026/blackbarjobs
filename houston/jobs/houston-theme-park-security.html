What is the main theme park near Houston that hires security?+
Six Flags Hurricane Harbor Splashtown, located at 21300 I-45 North in Spring, TX (about 25 miles north of downtown Houston). It's the largest water park in Houston at over 40 acres, in operation since 1984, with 40-plus water slides and attractions including the Hurricane Bay wave pool. The park hires 700-plus seasonal employees annually including security, lifeguards, ride operators, and guest services staff.
Is theme park security seasonal or year-round?+
Seasonal. Hurricane Harbor Splashtown is a water park — the Houston season runs May through early September. The annual hiring push happens in March and April, with the park opening mid-May. Officers who want consistent security income often combine theme park day shifts with year-round evening and weekend event security work during the summer.
How old do I need to be for theme park security?+
15 is the minimum age to apply for most Six Flags positions, but paid security officer roles requiring a Texas Level 2 license require applicants to be at least 18. The park hires a mix of age groups across departments, but licensed security officer positions are adult roles.
What does theme park security pay?+

What is the hiring process like at Hurricane Harbor Splashtown?+

Security positions require a Level 2 license or applicants must obtain one before starting. The park moves applicants quickly into onboarding and paid training ahead of opening day.
Overview

Theme Park Security in Houston: Seasonal but Consistent

Six Flags Hurricane Harbor Splashtown is Houston's largest water park and the defining theme park security account in the market. Located at 21300 I-45 North in Spring — about 25 miles north of downtown Houston — the park covers more than 40 acres with two wave pools, 40-plus water slides, and children's areas. Since opening in 1984, it's been a summer institution for Houston families, and its annual hiring of 700-plus seasonal employees makes it one of the largest single-site seasonal employers in north Houston.

The security function at a water park is meaningfully different from stadium or nightclub security. Officers manage guest behavior in an environment where much of the crowd is in swimwear, many are families with children, the day runs opening to close across a large outdoor footprint, and heat is a genuine management concern for guests and officers alike. Access control at the park gates, behavior management in the wave pool and slide queue areas, and response to the medical situations that occur at a water park are core functions.

For officers who want summer seasonal work that fits around Houston's outdoor festival and sporting event calendar, theme park security fills weekday and weekend day shifts that don't compete with the evening-dominant event security market. Officers who work Hurricane Harbor Splashtown during the day and event security on weekend evenings can maintain consistent income throughout the summer without scheduling conflicts.

Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Does a Theme Park Security Officer Do in Houston?

Theme park security at Hurricane Harbor Splashtown combines access control, guest behavior management, and emergency response across a large outdoor property. At the entry gates, officers check tickets and season passes, manage admission flow, and verify that prohibited items don't enter the facility. The environment is family-oriented and customer-service forward — guests are in vacation mode and expect a welcoming interaction.

Inside the park, security officers manage behavioral situations that arise in a crowded outdoor water environment: line-jumping at popular slides, conflict in the wave pool, minors in restricted areas, and occasional situations involving intoxication or medical distress in the heat. The outdoor, roving nature of the work means officers cover significant ground on foot throughout the shift.

Medical response awareness is particularly important at water parks. Drowning risk, heat exhaustion, and slide injuries generate more medical calls per shift than most comparable entertainment venues. Officers work alongside lifeguards and respond as the first ground-level responder for non-aquatic medical situations — coordinating EMS dispatch, crowd control around incident scenes, and incident report documentation.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Control park entry — verify tickets, season passes, and prohibited item compliance at all entry gates
  • Conduct foot patrol of the park property — wave pool areas, slide queue zones, pavilions, and parking lot
  • Manage guest behavior — enforce park policies on line order, restricted area access, and conduct standards
  • Respond to medical situations — coordinate with lifeguards and dispatch EMS; provide first-response crowd control at incident scenes
  • Monitor for prohibited items inside the park — outside food, glass, and non-compliant flotation devices
  • Manage the parking lot during peak arrival and departure periods — traffic flow and vehicle security
  • Handle ejection of guests who violate park policies — de-escalate first, involve management and HPD when necessary
  • Write incident reports for all medical calls, ejections, and notable security events during the shift

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license — required for paid security officer positions; minimum age 18
  • Heat tolerance and physical stamina — outdoor patrol in Houston summer heat for full shifts; water park environment doesn't eliminate outdoor exposure
  • Customer service orientation — theme park guests are families in vacation mode; friendly, professional interaction is the standard
  • Swim confidence — while lifeguards handle aquatic emergencies, security officers approach pool and waterway areas; basic swim comfort is practical
  • Medical response awareness — theme parks generate more medical calls than most entertainment venues; CPR/AED training recommended
  • Seasonal commitment — park operates May through early September; officers who can commit to the full season are preferred
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Seasonal operation — May through early September; peak hiring in March and April ahead of mid-May opening day
  • Daytime shifts dominant — water parks operate during daylight hours, typically 10am–8pm; security shifts track park hours
  • Weekend-heavy demand — Saturday and Sunday are peak attendance days with highest security staffing requirements
  • Weekday availability valued — busy weekday sessions during summer break require staffing even on non-weekend days
  • Compatible with evening event security — daytime theme park shifts don't conflict with evening bar, festival, and sports event work
  • Seasonal perks — free and discounted park admission available to employees throughout the operating season
