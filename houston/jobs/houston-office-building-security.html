FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the main office building security markets in Houston?+
Three distinct clusters: the Energy Corridor (I-10 West, roughly Eldridge to Barker Cypress) which houses BP, Shell, ConocoPhillips, Citgo, Baker Hughes, and dozens of oilfield services firms — 26 million square feet of office space; the Galleria/Uptown corridor which is the largest Class A office inventory in Texas; and Downtown Houston with its concentration of law firms, banks, and government offices around the Greenway and Main Street corridors.
What does office building security involve day-to-day?+
Lobby access control is the primary function — badging employees, logging and escorting visitors, managing contractor access, and operating the visitor management system. Console monitoring involves CCTV, door alarm, and access control panel oversight from a security desk. Periodic floor and stairwell patrol, tenant relations, and after-hours response round out the role.
What does office building security pay in Houston?+
High-rise office building security runs $15–21/hr in Houston. United Protective Services posts Energy Corridor high-rise roles at $15/hr. Downtown Class A buildings (posted via Allied Universal, Sunstates) run $17–21/hr. Corporate campus security at major energy companies can run higher for experienced officers with relevant backgrounds.
Is prior experience required for office building security?+
Preferred at Class A and Energy Corridor accounts. Current postings note "commercial or high-rise experience preferred" as standard language. Officers who come from corporate lobby, front desk, or concierge backgrounds are competitive. Energy Corridor accounts at oil and gas company campuses may require or prefer candidates with industrial or corporate security familiarity.
What are the shifts like in office building security?+
Day-shift heavy — commercial office buildings are primarily occupied during business hours, and the most-staffed period is 7am–7pm weekdays. Overnight and weekend posts exist but are less staffed than hospitals or residential. The Energy Corridor is particularly day-heavy, tracking the 8am–5pm operations of the energy companies that anchor it.
Overview

Houston's Office Security Market: Energy Corridor to Downtown

Houston's office building security market is organized around three geographic clusters. The Energy Corridor along I-10 West is the largest by square footage — 26 million square feet of corporate campus and high-rise office space housing the global headquarters and regional offices of BP, Shell, ConocoPhillips, Citgo, Halliburton, Baker Hughes, and hundreds of oilfield services and energy technology firms. These campuses range from single-tenant corporate headquarters with on-site security programs to multi-tenant Class A towers served by contract security firms. Energy Corridor accounts often involve visitor screening for clients and contractors traveling internationally, which adds a professional presentation standard above typical commercial security.

The Galleria/Uptown corridor — Westheimer between Beltway 8 and Loop 610 — has the largest Class A office inventory in Texas. High-rise towers housing financial firms, law offices, consulting companies, and medical sector businesses generate lobby security demand that blends access control with high-quality tenant relations. United Protective Services is active in this corridor with dedicated Galleria-area security officer postings. The premium on professional appearance and communication is higher here than in suburban commercial accounts.

Downtown Houston's high-rise core around Main Street, Travis, and Louisiana generates the third major office security cluster, with a population that includes banks (JPMorgan Chase's Houston tower is one of the largest in the south), law firms, and government buildings. Downtown office security also intersects with the convention district — officers in downtown commercial buildings regularly manage the overflow of convention traffic from the GRB corridor.

Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Does an Office Building Security Officer Do in Houston?

Office building security is lobby-first work. The primary post is the building entry — greeting employees, scanning badges, registering visitors in the visitor management system, issuing temporary badges, and managing contractor and vendor access per building protocol. First impressions matter significantly in Class A commercial security: officers interact with the building's tenants, their clients, and executive visitors daily, and the quality of that interaction reflects on both the building ownership and the security firm.

Console duty is the second core function. The security console — CCTV monitors, access control panel, alarm systems, and communication radios — is the hub of after-hours building security. During the day, a console officer supplements lobby coverage and handles tenant service requests. After-hours, the console officer may be the only security presence in a multi-floor building, responsible for monitoring access events, responding to alarms, coordinating maintenance requests, and making the call to contact HPD when something warrants it.

Energy Corridor accounts add a layer of complexity not present in urban commercial buildings: large campus footprints with multiple access gates, vehicle-based patrol requirements, and security protocols designed around the operational security needs of global energy companies. Officers who work Energy Corridor campus security develop experience with corporate security programs that's directly transferable to higher-level corporate security roles.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Manage building lobby access — badge employees, register visitors in VMS, issue temporary credentials, verify contractor and vendor authorization
  • Escort authorized visitors, contractors, and vendors to authorized areas — never allow unescorted access beyond the lobby
  • Monitor CCTV, access control, and alarm systems from security console — respond to camera events, door alarms, and after-hours access
  • Conduct scheduled patrol of building floors, stairwells, parking structures, and exterior areas per post orders
  • Respond to tenant security requests, lock-outs, and safety concerns — coordinate with building management and maintenance
  • Manage after-hours building access — control who enters and exits outside business hours; verify tenant authorization for late access
  • Write accurate visitor logs, access event records, and incident reports — documentation is the primary deliverable for building management
  • Enforce building security policies — no-tailgating, contractor check-in, after-hours protocols — professionally and consistently

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license — required for all commercial office security positions
  • Professional appearance and communication — Class A office security officers interact with executives, clients, and international visitors
  • Computer proficiency — visitor management systems, access control platforms, and digital incident reporting are standard tools
  • Commercial or high-rise experience preferred — noted explicitly in active Houston office building security postings
  • Customer service background — front desk, receptionist, concierge, or hospitality backgrounds translate directly to lobby security
  • Reliability and punctual attendance — office building security is fixed-post work; tardiness directly impacts tenant security coverage
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Day-shift dominant — commercial office buildings are busiest 7am–7pm weekdays; most security staffing concentrates in this window
  • Swing and overnight exists but lighter — after-hours console coverage and patrol required; less staffed than daytime lobby
  • Monday–Friday primary — Energy Corridor and Galleria accounts follow business hours; weekend staffing lighter but required for building security
  • 12-hour shifts common at major corporate campuses — Energy Corridor properties with continuous operations staff longer shifts
  • Holiday coverage required for building security functions — after-hours and perimeter security continues on holidays
  • Overtime available at larger accounts — multi-tenant high-rises with heavy event or visitor volume offer consistent overtime for reliable officers
