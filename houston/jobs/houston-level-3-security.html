FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Texas Level 3 security license?+
A Commissioned Security Officer license issued by the Texas DPS Private Security Bureau. It authorizes you to carry a firearm while on duty as a security officer — making you a 'commissioned' officer. It's the armed counterpart to the Level 2 Non-Commissioned license, and it requires Level 2 as a prerequisite. You must have the physical PSB pocket card in hand before working any armed post.
How do I get a Texas Level 3 security license?+
Prerequisites: must be 18+ (most employers require 21+ for armed posts), hold a valid Level 2 license, and have no disqualifying criminal history. Training: minimum 45 hours total — online coursework covering security law, use of force, and defensive tactics, plus minimum 10 hours of in-person firearms and defensive tactics (mandatory since January 2024 per HB 3424). Must pass a written exam and firearms range qualification. Must also pass an MMPI psychological exam. Application through TOPS with employer sponsorship. Total cost: $230–610+ depending on provider. Processing: 2–6 weeks.
What does a Level 3 license qualify me for in Houston?+
Every armed security post in the city: Ship Channel plant gates (ExxonMobil Baytown, Marathon Galveston Bay, Pemex Deer Park), hospital emergency departments and psychiatric wards (TMC — armed officers required for high-risk areas), manufacturing facility armed posts, ATM route and cash logistics security, armed corporate campus posts, armed retail security at high-theft or high-value locations, and armed patrol routes across the metro.
What does Level 3 security pay in Houston?+
Standard armed posts run $18–21/hr (Valor Force posts $19–21, St. Moritz at $20/hr). Experienced armed officers with 3+ years at manufacturing or plant-gate accounts see $22–25/hr. Armed supervisor and account manager roles run higher. The Level 3 license commands a $3–8/hr premium over comparable Level 2 posts.
Does an LTC (License to Carry) replace the Level 3?+
No. An LTC is a personal carry license for civilians. The Level 3 Commissioned Security Officer license is a professional credential required for armed on-duty security work in Texas. They are separate licenses — having an LTC does not qualify you to work armed security, and having a Level 3 does not replace an LTC for personal carry. You need the Level 3 for armed security employment, period.
Overview

Houston's Armed Security Market: The Level 3 Advantage

The Level 3 Commissioned Security Officer license is the credential that unlocks Houston's highest-paying security accounts. The Ship Channel petrochemical corridor — ExxonMobil Baytown, Marathon Galveston Bay, Pemex Deer Park, and 120-plus member facilities of the East Harris County Manufacturers Association — requires armed officers at plant gates and perimeter posts. These accounts pay $2–6/hr above standard security rates and require the PSB pocket card in hand on day one. There's no waiver, no grace period, and no exception.

The Texas Medical Center generates the second major armed security cluster. Hospital emergency departments, psychiatric wards, and high-security pharmacies at MD Anderson, Houston Methodist, Memorial Hermann, Texas Children's, and Harris Health all require Level 3 officers in designated posts. The armed role in healthcare is specifically for high-risk situations — behavioral health crises, ER violence prevention, and protection of controlled substance storage areas — where Level 2 deterrence isn't sufficient. These posts require additional hospital-specific training on top of the Level 3 base credential.

Beyond plants and hospitals, Houston's armed market includes ATM and cash logistics routes (Brinks, Garda), armed retail accounts at high-theft commercial locations, armed corporate campus security at major energy company headquarters, and armed mobile patrol covering commercial and industrial properties across the metro. Allied Universal has 12-plus active armed openings in Houston at any given time. GardaWorld, St. Moritz, TriCorps, Valor Force, and Sunstates are all active at the Level 3 tier.

Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Does a Level 3 Armed Security Officer Do in Houston?

Armed security in Houston varies dramatically by account. At a Ship Channel plant gate, the armed officer's role is access control at a facility where the combination of industrial hazard, high-value assets, and TWIC-regulated access creates a security environment that justifies commissioned status. The firearm is a presence and deterrent — the actual work is verifying credentials, logging vehicles, and enforcing site access restrictions on a 24-hour cycle.

At a hospital ER or psychiatric ward, the armed officer's role is fundamentally different. The firearm is a last resort in an environment where de-escalation, crisis intervention awareness, and the ability to work alongside clinical staff are the primary competencies. Officers at TMC facilities work directly with security dispatchers, charge nurses, and Crisis Intervention Teams — the armed capacity is for situations where unarmed intervention has failed or isn't appropriate. This is not the same as standing guard at a gate; it requires interpersonal skills and clinical awareness that plant-gate work doesn't.

On armed patrol routes — ATM logistics, commercial property patrol, or armed mobile — the Level 3 officer works independently in a vehicle, covering a defined circuit of stops with armed authority. The combination of driving, routing, documentation, and the situational awareness to recognize when something at a stop warrants engagement is what makes experienced armed patrol officers valuable. Officers who build a record of clean, well-documented armed patrol routes are consistently competitive for account supervisor and operations roles.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Carry and maintain duty firearm in compliance with Texas PSB licensing, employer weapons policy, and account-specific requirements
  • Control access at plant gates, hospital entry points, and restricted facility checkpoints — verify TWIC, badges, and authorization
  • Respond to disturbances, behavioral health incidents, and security threats — de-escalate first; use force strictly within policy and Texas law
  • Conduct armed patrol of assigned facilities, perimeters, and routes — log every check-in and document all observations
  • Work alongside clinical staff at healthcare facilities — respond to Crisis Intervention Team activations and patient management situations
  • Maintain PSB Commissioned license currency — complete biennial renewal, annual firearms requalification, and account-specific training
  • Escort cash, high-value assets, or protected persons per account security protocols
  • Write accurate incident reports for every use-of-force event, weapons display, and significant security incident

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 3 Commissioned Security Officer license — physical PSB pocket card in hand before starting any armed post; no exceptions
  • Valid Texas Level 2 license — prerequisite for Level 3; must be completed before Level 3 training
  • Minimum age 18 (many accounts require 21+) — verify employer-specific age requirement before applying; plant-gate and hospital accounts often require 21
  • Clean background — armed accounts run stricter screening; no felony, no Class A misdemeanor, no weapons-related convictions
  • Personal duty weapon where required — some manufacturing and Ship Channel accounts specify Glock 9mm with duty retention holster and two spare magazines
  • Annual firearms requalification — PSB requires biennial renewal with range qualification; some employers require annual or more frequent qualification
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • All shifts available — armed accounts run 24/7; overnight armed posts at hospitals and industrial facilities are consistently understaffed
  • 12-hour shifts standard at hospitals and plant-gate accounts — continuous coverage requirement drives longer shifts with 3–4 days per week
  • Armed patrol routes are overnight-heavy — ATM and commercial armed patrol concentrates in evening and overnight hours
  • Weekend and holiday coverage mandatory — armed accounts do not reduce staffing on weekends or holidays
  • Shift differentials of $1–2/hr for overnight and weekend shifts at most armed accounts
  • Plant-gate accounts near Ship Channel and Baytown may require commute — verify location before accepting; shuttle or carpool programs common at large industrial sites
