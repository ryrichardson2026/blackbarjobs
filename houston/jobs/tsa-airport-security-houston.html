FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a TSA Transportation Security Officer?+
A TSO is a federal employee working for the Transportation Security Administration, a division of the Department of Homeland Security. At Houston airports, TSOs screen passengers and baggage at security checkpoints, operate X-ray and CT scanning equipment, conduct physical screening, and respond to alarm activations. It's a federal law enforcement-adjacent role with a structured pay scale and full federal benefits.
What does TSA pay at IAH and Hobby Airport in Houston?+
TSO salaries in Houston average $53,831/yr ($26/hr) based on Glassdoor data from March 2026. The range runs $43,353–$67,501 annually. Starting Band D pays $34,454 base, with locality adjustments bringing actual pay higher. TSA offers step increases of up to 67% base salary increase over 5 years — it's one of the most structured pay progression systems in the security industry.
What are the federal benefits for TSA officers?+
Full federal benefits package: health, dental, vision, life, and long-term care insurance; personal leave days and paid federal holidays; tuition assistance; uniform allowance; Flexible Spending Account; transportation subsidies; FERS retirement pension; and a Thrift Savings Plan (TSP, equivalent to a 401k) with government contributions. For entry-level security workers, the benefits package is the most significant differentiator versus private-sector security work.
What are the requirements to become a TSA officer in Houston?+
US citizenship or national status, high school diploma or GED, no FAA/TSA disqualifying criminal offenses, ability to pass a TSA security threat assessment and background investigation, and successful completion of a medical evaluation. New hires complete up to 6 weeks of full-time training that may require travel away from IAH or Hobby. You must be comfortable with physical screening duties, standing for extended periods, and rotating shifts including nights and weekends.
Are there private airport security jobs at IAH and Hobby beyond TSA?+
Yes. ACTS Aviation Security and Aviation Security Company post private officer roles at IAH. The Houston Airport System (city-operated) hires directly for landside operations, including security inspection and TSA 1520/1542 compliance roles. With the 2026 FIFA World Cup in Houston, IAH is a primary international arrival point — private aviation security and airport support staffing has expanded around the tournament.
Overview

Houston's Airport Security Market: IAH, Hobby, and the FIFA Factor

George Bush Intercontinental Airport is one of the largest airports in the United States by passenger volume, operating across five terminals with United Airlines as the primary carrier and dozens of international routes. William P. Hobby Airport serves as Southwest Airlines' Houston hub, handling significant domestic volume in a single-terminal operation. Together, they make Houston one of the most active aviation security markets in the South, with TSA staffing needs that run 24/7 across all operational periods.

The 2026 FIFA World Cup has elevated Houston's airport security profile significantly. IAH is the primary international arrival gateway for World Cup visitors coming into the city, and the surge in international passenger volume has driven both TSA staffing focus and private aviation security expansion at the airport. Houston Stadium (hosting World Cup matches) is 30 minutes from IAH, and security coordination between airport and venue operations has created a degree of aviation security employment activity not seen in a non-Olympic year.

Beyond the tournament, Houston's energy sector drives consistent business travel through IAH — the airport serves more direct international routes to energy-producing nations than almost any other US airport. Corporate aviation security, private terminal access management, and the general demand from a metropolitan area of 7 million people sustain a year-round, full-employment airport security market across both federal and private tracks.

Role Overview

What Does an Airport Security Officer Do in Houston?

TSA Transportation Security Officers at IAH and Hobby perform checkpoint security operations — the passenger-facing work that most people associate with airport security. On a typical shift, a TSO rotates through several checkpoint functions: operating X-ray and CT imaging equipment to screen carry-on bags, conducting Advanced Imaging Technology (AIT) body scanner operations, performing physical pat-down screening when imaging identifies anomalies or passengers opt out, and managing the document verification lane where boarding passes and identification are checked. Rotation between positions is standard — no TSO works one checkpoint post all shift.

The physical and interpersonal demands are real. TSOs stand for extended periods, lift and move baggage, and navigate a continuous stream of passengers ranging from frequent business travelers who know the process to international visitors who don't. Composure, efficient communication, and the ability to de-escalate frustrated travelers while maintaining security protocols are daily requirements — not exceptional events.

Private airport security roles at IAH cover a different set of responsibilities: landside access control, ramp and cargo area security inspections under TSA 1520/1542 regulations, and ground transportation security. The Houston Airport System operations division hires directly for these roles, which require familiarity with FAA and TSA regulatory frameworks and often involve coordinating with airline tenants and ground handlers rather than screening passengers.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Operate X-ray and CT imaging equipment to screen carry-on baggage — identify prohibited items and resolve alarm activations
  • Conduct Advanced Imaging Technology (AIT) body scanning and physical pat-down screening per TSA standard operating procedures
  • Verify passenger identification and boarding documents at document check lanes
  • Rotate through all checkpoint security functions per shift — no officer works a single function all day
  • Respond to checkpoint alarms, security incidents, and passenger behavioral observations per TSA protocols
  • Coordinate with airline staff, law enforcement, and checkpoint supervisors on security incidents and operational needs
  • Complete required TSA continuing training modules and maintain certification currency per TSA standards
  • For private airport roles: conduct TSA 1520/1542 security inspections of airside access points and report compliance issues to airport operations

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • US citizenship or national status — TSA federal employment requires citizenship; private airport security roles may accept legal permanent residents
  • High school diploma or GED — minimum education requirement for TSO positions at IAH and Hobby
  • No FAA/TSA disqualifying criminal offenses — TSA maintains a specific list; review before applying as the background investigation is thorough
  • Medical evaluation clearance — TSOs must meet physical requirements including ability to stand, lift baggage, and perform physical screening duties
  • Up to 6 weeks of new hire training — full-time, may require travel away from your assigned airport; you are paid and reimbursed for authorized travel
  • Rotating shift availability — IAH and Hobby operate 24/7; TSOs work rotating schedules including nights, weekends, and federal holidays
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Rotating shifts across all 24 hours — IAH and Hobby never close; TSO scheduling covers every operational period including overnight
  • Mandatory overtime, weekends, and holidays are standard — airports don't reduce staffing on holidays; TSOs receive premium holiday pay
  • Shift rotation is standard — officers rotate through checkpoint positions and shift times to maintain skills and prevent fatigue
  • Night differential and Sunday premium pay available on top of base and locality pay — adds meaningful additional compensation for off-hour shifts
  • IAH is a 5-terminal operation — assignments may rotate across terminals depending on staffing needs and operational requirements
  • Training period requires full-time availability for up to 6 weeks, potentially including travel — plan accordingly before applying
