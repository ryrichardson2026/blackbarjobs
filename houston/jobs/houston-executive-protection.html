FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What license do I need for executive protection in Texas?+
A Texas Level 4 Personal Protection Officer (PPO) license, issued by the DPS Private Security Bureau. It requires completing Level 2 and Level 3 training first, then an additional 15-hour Level 4 training course. You must also pass psychological evaluation, background screening, and demonstrate prior qualifying experience. This is the most credential-intensive license in the Texas private security system — it's not obtainable without significant prior law enforcement, military, or security experience.
What does executive protection pay in Houston?+
Average $89,340/yr ($42.95/hr) per ZipRecruiter, with most roles running $55,400–$115,100/yr. Entry-level EP roles for qualified candidates start around $55k. Experienced EP agents with strong law enforcement or military backgrounds, defensive driving certification, and multi-language capability reach $100k–$140k. ExxonMobil's Executive Protection Advisor role requires 7 years of law enforcement, military, or security experience and pays at the upper end of the range.
Who are the main clients for executive protection in Houston?+
Energy sector executives are the primary client base — CEOs and senior leadership at BP, Shell, ConocoPhillips, Halliburton, Baker Hughes, ExxonMobil Baytown, and dozens of major oilfield services firms face both corporate and personal security demands. TMC healthcare executives (hospital system CEOs, research directors) are the second segment. High-net-worth family office clients in River Oaks and Memorial, financial industry leaders, and legal professionals round out the Houston EP market.
What prior experience qualifies me for executive protection?+
Law enforcement — especially criminal investigation, homicide, or organized crime units — is the most valued background. Military — particularly special operations, military police, or military intelligence — is equally competitive. Federal agents (FBI, Secret Service, Diplomatic Security Service) are the most competitive candidates for senior EP roles. Prior corporate security director experience can qualify for some roles. Without one of these backgrounds, a Texas Level 4 license alone is unlikely to get you into true EP work.
Is executive protection a realistic career path from standard security?+
Directly, no — not without significant additional credentials. The path from standard guard to EP runs through: (1) gaining Level 3 experience and building a strong armed security record, (2) obtaining Level 4 license training, (3) developing additional EP-specific skills (defensive driving certification, emergency medical training, advance work). Most Houston EP professionals come from law enforcement or military and enter EP after their government career, not from the contracting security track.
Overview

Houston's Executive Protection Market: Energy Sector and High Net Worth

Houston's executive protection market is driven by two forces unique to the city. The concentration of global energy company leadership — CEOs, presidents, and senior executives at BP, Shell, ConocoPhillips, Halliburton, Baker Hughes, TotalEnergies, and dozens of oilfield services firms — creates a class of principals who regularly travel internationally to high-risk energy-producing regions, operate in environments where their corporate position creates personal exposure, and maintain the kind of public profile that generates personal security requirements. ExxonMobil's posted Executive Protection Advisor role requires seven years of experience specifically in governmental or corporate personnel protection, with demonstrated experience in high-threat environments — that's the standard the Houston energy sector EP market expects.

Silent Professionals, a Houston-area EP firm, specifically notes that prior oil and gas sector experience and Spanish language skills are preferred for Houston EP roles — both are logical given the client base and the international travel patterns of energy executives who regularly visit Mexico, Latin America, and the Middle East. The advance work component of EP — assessing every destination before the principal arrives — is particularly demanding when the travel footprint includes countries with elevated kidnapping and extortion risk.

Beyond corporate EP, Houston's TMC healthcare executives, River Oaks high-net-worth families, and financial sector leaders create a residential and event EP market that's separate from corporate travel protection. Atlas Glinn is active in this segment, posting personal protection officer roles with emphasis on discretion, guest engagement, and situational awareness rather than tactical capacity. The Houston EP market covers the full spectrum from tactical corporate travel protection to discreet residential close protection.

Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Does an Executive Protection Officer Do in Houston?

Executive protection in Houston is advance work, logistics coordination, and close protection — in that order of daily time allocation. Before the principal goes anywhere, the EP agent assesses the destination: threat environment, route options, medical facility locations, emergency protocols, contingency exits. For energy executives traveling internationally, this advance work is continuous and detailed. For Houston-based principals moving between Energy Corridor offices, TMC appointments, and Galleria-area client entertainment, it's lighter but still systematic.

The logistics layer covers secure transportation — either operating the vehicle or coordinating with a trusted driver — managing the principal's schedule from a security perspective, coordinating with venue and event security at destinations, and maintaining the communication infrastructure that keeps protection effective. EP agents in Houston regularly interface with hotel security, event organizers, HPD when appropriate, and in some cases with foreign government security services for international visitors.

Close protection itself — the physical proximity work — is the smallest fraction of an EP agent's time but requires the highest-stakes judgment. Most EP incidents are resolved before they become physical through effective advance work and environmental awareness. The rare incident that isn't requires the combination of tactical training, legal authority (the Level 4 license), and the professional composure not to create a worse situation through overreaction. Houston EP clients pay premium rates for agents who can do all three.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Conduct advance work at all principal destinations — assess threat environment, identify secure routes, locate medical facilities, establish contingency plans
  • Provide close protection during principal movements — vehicle operation or coordination, proximity coverage, environmental awareness
  • Coordinate with venue, hotel, and event security at all principal destinations — establish communication and emergency protocols in advance
  • Manage secure transportation — operate or coordinate armored or secure vehicles; plan and assess primary and alternate routes
  • Conduct threat and vulnerability assessments for principal's residence, workplace, and frequent destinations
  • Monitor intelligence feeds and security briefings relevant to principal's travel and operating environments
  • Maintain emergency medical readiness — EP agents typically hold CPR, AED, and trauma first aid certification as a baseline
  • Document all security incidents, threat observations, and route assessments in principal's security file

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 4 Personal Protection Officer (PPO) license — requires Level 2, Level 3, and Level 4 training sequence; must pass psychological evaluation
  • Prior law enforcement, military, or senior security experience — typically 5–10 years minimum; federal law enforcement and special operations military are most competitive
  • Defensive driving certification — EP-specific driving training (EVOC or equivalent) is standard requirement across Houston EP postings
  • Emergency medical training — CPR, AED, and tactical medicine or trauma first aid are standard EP agent qualifications
  • Spanish or additional language capability — strongly preferred for Houston energy sector EP given Latin American and international travel patterns
  • Prior international operation experience — Energy Corridor EP roles often involve high-threat-country travel; prior experience in those environments is a differentiator
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Principal-dependent scheduling — EP agents work when the principal needs coverage, which doesn't follow a standard weekly pattern
  • International travel component — energy sector EP frequently includes multi-day or multi-week international travel to energy-producing regions
  • On-call availability standard — EP agents maintain availability beyond assigned coverage hours for emergency situations
  • Residential and event coverage may be evening and weekend dominant — TMC and River Oaks residential EP follows the principal's personal schedule
  • Shift teams at senior level — principal-level EP for senior energy executives typically uses rotating agent teams to maintain 24/7 coverage capacity
  • Houston-based day operations — many Houston EP agents cover local Energy Corridor and Galleria meetings without overnight travel the majority of the time
