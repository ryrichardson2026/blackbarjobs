FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What types of high-value asset security jobs exist in Houston?+
Four main categories: armored transport and cash logistics (Brink's, Garda — moving currency, ATM loads, and high-value cargo between banks, retailers, and financial institutions); ATM route security (armed officers guarding ATM service technicians during loading and unloading); jewelry and luxury retail security (Galleria-area boutiques, Tiffany, De Beers, jewelry trade shows); and art and auction security (Museum of Fine Arts Houston and auction events requiring specialized asset-aware officers).
Does high-value asset security require Level 3?+
Yes, across the board. Any role that involves carrying a firearm while protecting assets — armored transport, ATM escort, jewelry store armed officer — requires the Texas Level 3 Commissioned Security Officer license. Brink's explicitly requires Level 3 for guard roles. Tiffany & Co. posts brand security officer roles requiring commissioned status. There are no unarmed high-value asset security roles of substance in the Houston market.
What does high-value asset security pay in Houston?+
Armored car security guard averages $18.17/hr in Houston with most earning $14.66–$17.88/hr. Brink's and Garda armored guard roles are consistently at or above market for the category. Jewelry and luxury retail armed security at Galleria boutiques (St. Moritz posts armed luxury retail roles) runs $20–22/hr. ATM escort and route-based armed security runs $19–23/hr, overlapping with general armed patrol rates.
What makes high-value asset security different from standard armed security?+
The threat profile and legal stakes are different. Officers protecting cash in transit or escorting ATM technicians are working environments where robbery is a real, planned risk — not an unlikely event. The training emphasis is robbery response, emergency protocols, and vehicle security rather than access control or de-escalation. The documentation standards are higher because every asset movement is logged for liability. And the financial stakes of an incident are dramatically higher than a standard commercial armed post.
Who are the main employers for high-value asset security in Houston?+
Brink's (armored transport, ATM services, cash logistics — requires Level 3), GardaWorld (armored and valuables transport), Dunbar Armored, Loomis (cash management and courier), and First Cash (pawn and loan chain with asset protection requirements). In retail, St. Moritz posts armed luxury retail officer roles at Galleria boutiques. The Museum of Fine Arts Houston staffs gallery attendant-security positions for art protection.
Overview

Houston's High-Value Asset Security Market: Cash, Jewelry, and Art

Houston's high-value asset security market is anchored by two segments that are larger here than in most Texas metros: cash logistics and armored transport, driven by the density of financial institutions and retail serving Houston's 7 million metro residents; and luxury retail asset protection, driven by the Galleria's position as the third-largest mall in the US and the concentration of high-end jewelry, watch, and luxury boutiques it houses.

The cash logistics segment employs armored transport officers at Brink's, GardaWorld, Loomis, and Dunbar to move currency between banks, ATM networks, and large retail cash processors. This is continuous, route-based work — officers are in an armored vehicle for most of their shift, completing pre-defined stops under strict timing and security protocols. Every stop is logged, every transfer is documented, and the chain of custody for every dollar moved is maintained. Brink's Houston operations are active and hiring; Level 3 is a hard requirement.

The luxury retail segment at the Galleria creates a distinct high-value asset security role. Tiffany & Co., De Beers, Gucci, Louis Vuitton, and other high-end jewelry and accessory retailers at the Galleria staff security that blends asset protection with brand representation — officers in professional attire who deter theft at the high end of the retail LP market while maintaining the service environment the brand requires. St. Moritz Security posts armed luxury retail officer roles at these accounts at $20–22/hr. The Museum of Fine Arts Houston staffs gallery attendants who combine security and visitor engagement for the city's premier art collection.

Overview & Duties
Role Overview

What Does a High-Value Asset Security Officer Do in Houston?

High-value asset security in Houston concentrates into three distinct role profiles. Armored transport is the most operationally structured — officers follow defined routes and protocols, never deviating without dispatch authorization, and maintain continuous radio contact throughout every stop. The threat model is robbery, not access control, which means the officer's primary security function is deterrence through armed presence and rapid response if deterrence fails. Cash handling and chain of custody documentation are core functions.

ATM route security is adjacent to armored transport but more reactive — an armed officer accompanies the ATM service technician to each machine, providing coverage during the brief, high-exposure period when the ATM vault is open and accessible. The officer's job is to make that window as unattractive as possible for robbery attempts through visible armed presence, situational awareness, and quick-response positioning. It's patrol-like work with a concentrated threat window at each stop.

Luxury retail and art security is the most relationship-intensive of the three. Officers at Tiffany or a MFAH gallery interact continuously with clients and visitors — the security function is embedded in a hospitality environment. The primary deterrence is a professional, aware presence that identifies shoplifting or theft behavior before merchandise leaves the floor. At the MFAH, gallery attendants also provide visitor information and manage crowd flow around significant works during exhibitions. The art protection function requires cultural awareness and familiarity with the works being protected alongside the standard security competencies.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Escort or operate armored vehicle on defined cash transport routes — maintain continuous dispatch contact and strict stop protocols
  • Provide armed coverage during ATM service stops — position to deter robbery during vault access window, maintain situational awareness throughout
  • Staff jewelry and luxury retail security — monitor sales floor for theft behavior, deter shoplifting through professional armed presence
  • Document all asset transfers, custody changes, and route stops — chain of custody documentation is a core function across all high-value asset categories
  • Respond to robbery attempts, alarm activations, and security breaches — follow specific employer response protocols for high-value-asset incidents
  • Conduct gallery patrol at art institutions — monitor artwork and visitor behavior, respond to incidents, enforce access protocols for restricted areas
  • Verify credentials for service personnel at ATM and vault locations — prevent unauthorized access during high-exposure service windows
  • Write incident reports for all security events, near-misses, and route anomalies — documentation quality matters significantly in high-value asset security

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 3 Commissioned Security Officer license — required across all high-value asset security roles; Brink's and armored transport are explicit
  • Clean background with no financial crimes — asset security accounts run stricter background checks; any theft, fraud, or financial crime history is disqualifying
  • Valid Texas driver's license with clean MVR — armored transport and ATM route roles require driving; MVR check is standard
  • Prior armed security, law enforcement, or military experience — strongly preferred at armored transport and specialized asset accounts
  • Physical fitness — armored transport involves lifting currency bags and equipment; some routes require repeated vehicle ingress/egress at multiple stops
  • Professional appearance — luxury retail and art institution accounts require attire and demeanor that matches the brand or institutional environment
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Armored transport routes run business hours primarily — bank-to-bank and retail-to-bank cash movement concentrated weekday daytime
  • ATM routes run 24/7 — ATM replenishment happens at all hours; overnight ATM escort is common and typically includes a premium
  • Luxury retail security tracks store hours — Galleria accounts run 10am–9pm with closing coverage
  • Gallery and museum security runs with institution hours — MFAH open Tuesday–Sunday; special exhibitions extend hours
  • Weekend and holiday cash logistics coverage required — retail cash volumes peak weekends and holidays; armored routes don't stop
  • Route-based scheduling — armored transport and ATM security officers typically have consistent route assignments that define their weekly schedule
