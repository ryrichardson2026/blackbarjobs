FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What types of residential security jobs exist in Houston?+
Three main categories: gatehouse officers at HOA gated communities (control vehicle and pedestrian access, enforce community rules, verify visitors), lobby and concierge security at high-rise and midrise apartments (visitor management, package handling, elevator monitoring), and patrol officers covering large residential communities by vehicle. The gatehouse category is the largest by volume in Houston given the metro's extensive master-planned community footprint.
What does residential security pay in Houston?+
Gated community security averages $18.17/hr with most earning $14.66–$17.88/hr. Apartment security averages $25.41/hr — higher because apartment accounts often involve overnight coverage and more incident response. HOA patrol positions run $16–19/hr. Luxury high-rise lobby officer roles at properties in Midtown, Galleria, and River Oaks cluster toward the top of the range.
Which Houston suburbs have the most HOA security jobs?+
Katy, Sugar Land, The Woodlands, Pearland, Friendswood, and Cypress. Houston's master-planned community model — where entire neighborhoods like Cinco Ranch, First Colony, and The Woodlands are built as gated or access-controlled communities — generates a residential security market that's distinct from other Texas metros. The Woodlands alone is a 28,000-acre community with multiple manned gate positions.
Is residential security full-time?+
Yes — residential properties need 24/7 coverage including overnight, weekends, and holidays. Unlike event security, residential is fixed-post work with consistent weekly scheduling. Gate and lobby positions are staffed on rotating 8 or 12-hour shifts. Residential is one of the most stable employment categories in Houston security.
Do I need a vehicle for residential security in Houston?+
For gatehouse and lobby posts, no — you're assigned to a fixed post. For HOA patrol positions covering large master-planned communities, yes — either a company vehicle is provided or the posting requires a personal vehicle. Always confirm before accepting a residential patrol assignment.
Overview

Houston's Residential Security Market: Master-Planned Communities and Urban High-Rises

Houston's residential security market is shaped by two forces unique to the city: the massive master-planned community footprint in the suburbs and the high-rise apartment boom in urban neighborhoods. The suburban HOA segment is the larger of the two. Communities like Cinco Ranch in Katy, First Colony in Sugar Land, and The Woodlands (28,000 acres, developed since 1974) have manned gate positions covering controlled-access entrances to thousands of homes. These communities staff gatehouse officers around the clock and contract HOA patrol vehicles to cover interiors. The geographic spread of Houston's suburbs means this segment is genuinely distributed across the metro — Katy, Sugar Land, Pearland, Friendswood, Cypress, Spring, Humble, and The Woodlands all have active HOA security demand.

The urban high-rise segment has expanded significantly over the past decade. Midtown, Montrose, the Galleria/Uptown corridor, and Downtown have all added high-rise and midrise residential towers that staff lobby security officers as a standard amenity. These posts blend security and concierge functions — lobby officers manage visitor access, coordinate package and delivery handling, monitor elevator systems, and provide the first point of contact for residents who have issues at all hours. Pay at luxury high-rises trends higher than suburban gatehouse work.

International Guard Services (IGS), Allied Universal, United Protective Services, Green City Security, and GuardTexas (since 1976) are all active in the Houston residential security market. The stability of the residential segment — consistent scheduling, year-round demand, and low event-based variability — makes it one of the most reliable employment categories for officers who want predictable income.

Role Overview

What Does a Residential Security Officer Do in Houston?

Residential security in Houston centers on access control and community management. At a gated community gatehouse, the primary function is controlling who enters: every vehicle and pedestrian must be verified against a resident list or visitor authorization before the gate opens. The officer manages delivery drivers, service vendors, contractors, and guests — coordinating with residents when someone is unexpected. Parking enforcement, towing coordination, and noise/disturbance response are secondary functions that come with the territory in active residential communities.

At a high-rise apartment lobby, the work shifts toward concierge-style security. Officers greet residents by name, manage the visitor sign-in process for a building that may have hundreds of units, coordinate with the package room and delivery systems, and monitor elevator and common-area camera feeds. The incident profile is different from a gatehouse — less access control decision-making, more interpersonal management of residents who have complaints, guests who arrive drunk, or deliveries that go wrong at 2am.

HOA patrol is the most independent residential security role. Officers drive a defined patrol route through the community, check perimeter fencing and common areas, respond to resident-reported incidents, manage parking violations, and log every check-in digitally. It's vehicle-based, solo work that requires self-sufficiency and sound judgment — the kind of posting that builds a strong independent security track record for officers who want to move into patrol supervisor or account manager roles.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Control vehicle and pedestrian access at HOA community gates — verify against resident and approved visitor lists, manage delivery and vendor access
  • Log all entries and exits — maintain accurate visitor records and document any unauthorized access attempts
  • Conduct scheduled foot and vehicle patrol of community common areas, parking lots, and perimeter zones
  • Respond to resident-reported disturbances, noise complaints, and security concerns — de-escalate and document
  • Manage lobby visitor sign-in, package and delivery coordination, and elevator access at high-rise residential properties
  • Enforce community parking regulations — issue violation notices and coordinate towing per HOA policy
  • Monitor CCTV systems and gate camera feeds — document vehicle and pedestrian activity at controlled access points
  • Write accurate daily activity logs and incident reports — residential management relies on officer documentation for HOA board reporting

Requirements & Schedules
Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license — required for all paid residential security positions
  • Customer service and community relations skills — residential officers interact with residents daily; professional, friendly demeanor is essential
  • Clean driving record and valid Texas driver's license — required for HOA patrol and any multi-site residential assignment
  • Reliable transportation to assigned gatehouse or community — residential posts are fixed-location; you must be able to get there consistently
  • Overnight and weekend availability — residential properties need 24/7 coverage including nights, weekends, and holidays
  • Bilingual Spanish — preferred at many southwest Houston and suburban HOA accounts; noted explicitly in some postings
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • Fixed rotating shifts — gate and lobby posts run 8 or 12-hour rotations covering 24 hours per day year-round
  • All three shifts available — residential security needs day (7am–7pm), swing (3pm–11pm), and overnight (11pm–7am) coverage
  • Overnight demand consistent — overnight gatehouse and lobby posts are the highest-staffing-need shifts at most residential properties
  • Weekend and holiday coverage mandatory — residential properties never reduce staffing; residents are home more on weekends and holidays
  • 12-hour shifts common at luxury high-rises — reduces handoff complexity for properties with high resident interaction volume
  • Suburban vs urban scheduling — HOA community gates often run split gate access hours; confirm shift structure before accepting
