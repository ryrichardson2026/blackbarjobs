FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Texas Level 2 security license?+
A Non-Commissioned Security Officer license issued by the Texas Department of Public Safety Private Security Bureau. It authorizes you to work as an unarmed security officer in Texas. Training is 6 hours online plus a background check. Most candidates complete it in 1–2 weeks. The license must be in hand before starting any paid security post.
What does unarmed security pay in Houston?+
Current postings run $15–17/hr for standard commercial and residential posts. TMC hospital accounts (MD Anderson, Texas Children's, Houston Methodist) run $17–20/hr. Sunstates Security is posting $16–17/hr for flex officers. Some supervisor and lead positions reach $18–21/hr.
Do I need experience to get hired?+
No. Unarmed positions in Houston are widely accessible to candidates with a guard card and a clean background. Twin City Security (veteran-owned, since 1972) trains new hires. Allied Universal, Securitas, and most major firms will hire entry-level officers. Retail, hospitality, customer service, and military backgrounds are all noted as competitive in current postings.
Is bilingual (Spanish) required?+
Not required, but it's a frequent plus — especially in the southwest Houston residential market and certain TMC hospital accounts. Officers who are bilingual in Spanish are noted as preferred in a meaningful share of current Houston postings.
What kinds of accounts are there for unarmed officers in Houston?+
Healthcare (TMC hospitals and clinic campuses), high-rise corporate lobbies in the Energy Corridor and Galleria area, residential apartment communities, retail loss prevention, university and school campuses, government buildings, and hotel security. The spread across account types makes unarmed Level 2 work the broadest employment category in Houston security.

Houston's Unarmed Security Market: The Broadest Category in the City

Unarmed Level 2 security is the largest employment category in Houston's security market by volume, and the city's size and industry mix make it genuinely diverse. The Texas Medical Center drives a category of healthcare unarmed security that's unique in Texas — 60 institutions, 1,345 acres, and more than 106,000 employees generate continuous demand for lobby officers, access control posts, and patient-facing security across Houston Methodist, Memorial Hermann, Texas Children's Hospital, Harris Health System, and MD Anderson. Healthcare security in the TMC pays above standard commercial rates and includes benefits at the major system employers.

Beyond healthcare, Houston's corporate and residential base generates consistent demand across different environments and shifts. The Energy Corridor's 26 million square feet of office space anchors a corporate lobby market that's heavily day-shift. The Galleria and Uptown corridor — the largest Class A office inventory in Texas — generates upscale high-rise demand where presentation and customer-service competency matter as much as security fundamentals. And Houston's sprawling residential market, from Midtown apartment towers to suburban gated communities in Katy, Spring, and Pearland, staffs unarmed officers around the clock.

Active hirers right now include Allied Universal (90-plus live openings citywide), Sunstates Security (posting $16–17/hr, flex scheduling), TriCorps, Prosegur, Inner Parish Security Corporation, DSI Security, Walden Security, Platinum Group, and Twin City Security. The volume of open positions makes Houston one of the strongest markets for Level 2 officers in Texas.

Role Overview

What Does an Unarmed Security Officer Do in Houston?

Unarmed security officers in Houston work across enough different environments that the day-to-day role varies significantly by account type. At a TMC hospital or clinic, the work centers on patient and visitor management — access control at entry points, responding to behavioral disturbances alongside clinical staff, monitoring camera systems, and managing the flow of a facility that operates 24 hours with high emotional stakes. De-escalation and composure under pressure are real daily requirements in healthcare security, not theoretical ones.

At a corporate lobby in the Energy Corridor or Galleria area, the role is front-of-house: greeting and badging visitors, managing deliveries and contractor access, console monitoring, and representing the client professionally to every person who walks through the door. These accounts hire for presentation and communication as much as security fundamentals.

At residential apartment communities, the work varies by shift — daytime involves gate management and resident services; overnight is perimeter patrol and incident response. Officers who can work independently, document accurately, and maintain professional behavior across all three account types build the most durable careers in Houston unarmed security.

Duties

Common Duties & Responsibilities

  • Manage lobby access, visitor sign-in, and temporary badge issuance at corporate, healthcare, and residential facilities
  • Monitor CCTV systems and access control panels — respond to alarms, door-hold alerts, and access violations
  • Conduct scheduled foot patrol of assigned buildings, campus areas, and perimeter zones
  • Respond to disturbances, medical emergencies, and behavioral incidents — de-escalate and document per post orders
  • Verify and log contractor credentials, work orders, and vendor access throughout the shift
  • Control vehicle access at residential gates, parking structures, and loading docks
  • Write accurate shift logs and incident reports — documentation standard varies by account type but is always required
  • Provide customer service assistance to residents, patients, visitors, and employees as directed by post orders

Requirements

What You Need

  • Valid Texas Level 2 Non-Commissioned Security Officer license — must be in hand before starting any paid post
  • Clean background and drug screen — required by all employers; specific standards vary by account type
  • Valid Texas driver's license — required for patrol, mobile, and multi-site accounts; effectively required for most posts
  • Professional appearance and communication — corporate lobby and TMC hospital accounts hold officers to presentation standards
  • Bilingual Spanish — not required but noted as preferred in a meaningful share of current Houston postings, especially southwest and residential accounts
  • Flexibility across shifts — day, swing, and overnight positions all available; willingness to cover weekends increases hiring speed
Schedules

Typical Schedules & Environments

  • All three shifts available — day (6am–2pm or 7am–3pm), swing (2pm–10pm or 3pm–11pm), and overnight (10pm–6am or 7pm–7am)
  • TMC hospital accounts run 12-hour rotating shifts across all three — 24/7 patient care requires continuous officer coverage
  • Corporate campus accounts are predominantly day and swing — Energy Corridor and Galleria posts track business hours
  • Residential and apartment community posts run all shifts — overnight is highest demand, weekend availability valued
  • Flex scheduling available through major firms — Allied Universal, Sunstates, and TriCorps all post flex officer positions in Houston
  • Overnight differentials of $0.50–1.50/hr above base at most accounts; healthcare system employers often include full benefits
