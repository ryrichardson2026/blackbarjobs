FAQ

Security Job FAQs

Common questions from officers and employers.

Do I need a license to apply for security jobs?
▼
Most security jobs require a state-issued security officer license. Requirements vary by state, and some employers will hire candidates while they complete licensing. Entry-level and unarmed roles are the most flexible on this requirement.
How do I get a security license?
▼
Most states require a short approved training course, a background check, and a state application. Costs and timelines vary by state. Armed roles typically require additional firearms training and qualification.
What's the difference between armed and unarmed security?
▼
Unarmed officers carry no firearm and focus on access control, patrol, and observation. Armed officers carry a firearm and work higher-risk sites like banks, dispensaries, and government facilities. Armed roles require an armed license and firearms qualification, and typically pay $4–10/hr more than comparable unarmed positions.
Which markets does BlackBarJobs cover?
▼
We're rolling out market by market. Texas is live now, with dedicated pages for Dallas–Fort Worth, Houston, San Antonio, and Austin, and more regions coming. Every listing comes from a live feed rather than a stale aggregator.
Is BlackBarJobs a staffing agency?
▼
No. BlackBarJobs is a candidate network and media property built specifically for security officers. We surface who's hiring and what they pay so officers can apply with context. We do not place, employ, or contract officers.
I'm an employer. How do I reach officers?
▼
Employers can put a role in front of active, licensed officers across every live market through the employer page. It's built for direct candidate reach, not staffing markup. See the employer page for current options.
Can I work security with no experience?
▼
Yes. Many unarmed positions are entry-level and require only a license, a clean background check, and reliable transportation. Customer service, retail, military, and hospitality backgrounds all count as relevant. Event security is often the easiest entry point for candidates new to the industry.
